## Definition

In the real quaternions, let $B=\mathbb Q1+\mathbb Qi+\mathbb Qj+\mathbb Qk$ be the rational Hamilton algebra. Its basis satisfies $i^2=j^2=k^2=-1$ and $ij=k=-ji$, with the other products obtained by cyclic permutation of $i,j,k$. An order $O$ in $B$ is a subset containing $1$ and closed under subtraction and multiplication, with a $\mathbb Z$-basis $b_0,\ldots,b_3$ that is also a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $B$.

For a commutative unital $\mathbb Q$-algebra $R$, write $B_R=B\otimes_{\mathbb Q}R$ for the algebra with the same basis and multiplication rules over $R$. For $x=x_0+x_1i+x_2j+x_3k$ in $B_R$, write $\bar x=x_0-x_1i-x_2j-x_3k$ and call $N(x)=\sum_{r=0}^3x_r^2$ its reduced norm. A superscript $\times$ denotes the group of elements with a two-sided multiplicative inverse in the indicated ring.

For each prime $\ell$, let $\mathbb Q_\ell$ be the completion of $\mathbb Q$ in the $\ell$-adic metric $d_\ell(a,b)=|a-b|_\ell$. Let $\mathbb Z_\ell$ be its ring of integers, the ball $|a|_\ell\le 1$ for the extended absolute value. The local algebra is $B_\ell=B_{\mathbb Q_\ell}$, and the local order is $O_\ell=O\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_\ell=\sum_{r=0}^3\mathbb Z_\ell b_r$ inside $B_\ell$. Give $B_\ell$ the product topology of its four Hamilton coordinates, giving each copy of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ the topology induced by the extended metric. Give $B_\ell^\times$ the induced subspace topology.

## Justification

**Given:** An order $O$ as in the definition, a prime $\ell$, and a commutative unital $\mathbb Q$-algebra $R$.

1.1 Inside the real quaternions the rational coordinates are closed under subtraction and the Hamilton multiplication formula. The rational span also contains $1$, so it inherits the unital ring structure by the subring criterion. The coordinate formula makes the map $a\mapsto a1$ into $B$ a unital homomorphism with central image, giving the $\mathbb Q$-algebra. The action $(a,x)\mapsto(a1)x$ is coordinatewise multiplication by $a$. Componentwise field arithmetic makes addition abelian, the action distributive over both additions and associative with scalar multiplication, and $1$ acts as the identity. Thus $B$ is a $\mathbb Q$-vector space, with basis $1,i,j,k$ by uniqueness of the four coordinates. The same subring criterion applies to $O$ by its defining conditions. The integer scalar map $m\mapsto m1$ takes values in $O$, and the ring laws give preservation of addition, multiplication and $1$, with central image. This gives its $\mathbb Z$-algebra structure. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 For rational $a,b,c$, the formula in [F3] gives $d_\ell(a,b)\ge0$, with equality exactly when $a=b$. The valuation agreement in [F3] gives rational valuation zero at $1$ and $-1$, so $|1|_\ell=|-1|_\ell=1$. Multiplicativity gives $|b-a|_\ell=|-1|_\ell|a-b|_\ell=|a-b|_\ell$. Since $a-c=(a-b)+(b-c)$, the rational strong triangle inequality bounds $d_\ell(a,c)$ by $\max(d_\ell(a,b),d_\ell(b,c))$, which is at most $d_\ell(a,b)+d_\ell(b,c)$. Thus $(\mathbb Q,d_\ell)$ satisfies the metric hypothesis of the Cauchy-sequence construction in [F3]. Let $I=(\ell)\subseteq\mathbb Z$. The multiples $\ell\mathbb Z$ form an additive subgroup stable under multiplication by integers and contain $\ell$. Every ideal containing $\ell$ contains all its integer multiples, so $I=\ell\mathbb Z$ by [F6]. If $I^n=\ell^n\mathbb Z$, each product of an element of $I^n$ and one of $I$ is a multiple of $\ell^{n+1}$, as is any finite sum of such products. Conversely, $\ell^{n+1}m=(\ell^nm)\ell$ belongs to $I^nI$ for every integer $m$, so $I^{n+1}=I^nI=\ell^{n+1}\mathbb Z$. Apply [F7] to $P(k)$ given by $I^{k+1}=\ell^{k+1}\mathbb Z$. The calculation of $I$ gives $P(0)$ and the product calculation with $n=k+1$ gives $P(k)\Rightarrow P(k+1)$. Hence $I^n=\ell^n\mathbb Z$ for every $n\ge1$. Multiplication of $I^n$ by the module $\mathbb Z$ leaves it unchanged, since sums of integer multiples stay in the ideal and each element is its own product with $1$. The multiples of $\ell^n$ are closed under subtraction and multiplication by integers, so $\ell^n\mathbb Z$ is a $\mathbb Z$-submodule of $\mathbb Z$. The quotients $\mathbb Z/I^n\mathbb Z$ in the adic completion are therefore $\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$. The reduction $\rho_n$ from modulus $\ell^{n+1}$ to $\ell^n$ sends the class of an integer to its class modulo $\ell^n$. Equality of classes at the higher modulus means their representatives differ by a multiple of $\ell^{n+1}$, hence also by a multiple of $\ell^n$, giving equality at the lower modulus. It preserves sums, products, and $1$ since the residue operations are computed on integer representatives and the class of $1$ is the identity. Writing $[a]_m$ for the class of $a$ modulo $m$, for every $k\in\mathbb Z$ the quotient scalar rule gives $\rho_n(k[a]_{\ell^{n+1}})=[ka]_{\ell^n}=k\rho_n([a]_{\ell^{n+1}})$. Thus the reductions are $\mathbb Z$-linear transition maps. Thus the compatible tuples form a commutative unital ring under componentwise operations. For each integer $m$, the rational sequence with every term equal to $m$ is Cauchy because the distance between any two terms is zero. Under the canonical comparison of [F3], the constant Cauchy sequence of any integer $m$ maps to $(m\bmod\ell^n)_{n\ge1}$, since these residues are already constant at each modulus. Consequently the integer scalar map to the completed integer ring agrees, inside $\mathbb Q_\ell$, with the composite of the integer embedding in $\mathbb Q$ and the constant-sequence embedding. [F2, F3, F6, F7, given]

2.1 Negating a rational Cauchy sequence preserves all its termwise distances by step 1.2. Since addition of classes is termwise and a sequence plus its negative is the zero sequence, $a-b=[(a_n-b_n)]$ for the representatives in [F3]. The two limit formulas therefore give $\widehat d_\ell(a,b)=|a-b|_\ell$. In particular $|a|_\ell=\widehat d_\ell(a,0)>0$ for $a\ne0$, by nonnegativity of the absolute value and metric separation. An element of $\mathbb Z_\ell$ is a unit there exactly when its absolute value is $1$, since $|1|_\ell=1$ extends from the rationals and multiplicativity gives $|a^{-1}|_\ell=1/|a|_\ell$ for $a\ne0$. An increment of absolute value less than $1$ preserves membership in each of $\{|a|_\ell\le1\}$ and $\{|a|_\ell=1\}$, by applying the ultrametric inequality in both directions. The radius-one open ball about $a$ consists of $a+h$ with $|h|_\ell<1$ and therefore lies on the same side of each membership test as $a$. Both sets and both complements are thus open by the metric criterion, so the sets are open and closed. [F3, F5, step 1.2]

2.2 The fields $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb Q_\ell$ are commutative rings by [F2], and the field inclusion $\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q_\ell$ gives the latter its $\mathbb Q$-algebra structure. Use the pairs $(B,R)$ and $(B,\mathbb Q_\ell)$ over $\mathbb Q$, and $(O,\mathbb Z_\ell)$ over $\mathbb Z$. In the last pair give $\mathbb Z_\ell$ the integer scalar map of step 1.2. For any of these pairs write $(A,S,K)$ for the two factors and base. On each first factor use the right action $ak=a\eta_A(k)$, where $\eta_A$ is its central scalar map. On each second factor use the left action $ks=\eta_S(k)s$ from its scalar map. The ring laws and unital homomorphisms give the left and right module axioms on the additive groups of the rings. Apply the tensor-algebra rule. In each tensor the map $s\mapsto 1\otimes s$ is additive by the tensor relations, and is unital and multiplicative by the pure-tensor multiplication rule. It is central since $S$ is commutative and pure tensors generate. The resulting $S$-scalar action is multiplication on the second factor. Write $(u_r)$ for the basis of $A$ and $c_r:A\to K$ for its coefficient maps. The tensor relations expand $a\otimes s$ as $\sum_r u_r\otimes c_r(a)s$, so the $u_r\otimes1$ span over $S$. Each pairing $(a,s)\mapsto c_r(a)s$ takes values in the abelian additive group of $S$ and is additive in both variables. It is balanced because $c_r(ak)s=c_r(a)(ks)$ for $k\in K$. The descended map sends $\sum_j u_j\otimes s_j$ to $s_r$, proving uniqueness of all four coefficients. The pure-tensor multiplication rule gives the same multiplication constants in each extended basis. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 The pairing $(o,z)\mapsto z(o\otimes1)$ from $O\times\mathbb Z_\ell$ to the abelian additive group of $B_\ell$, using the copy of $\mathbb Z_\ell$ in $\mathbb Q_\ell$, is additive in both variables. It is balanced over $\mathbb Z$ because the two integer actions agree by step 1.2, so $z((om)\otimes1)=(mz)(o\otimes1)$ for every integer $m$. Its descended map is unital and multiplicative on pure tensors by centrality of $z$ and the tensor multiplication rule, hence unital and multiplicative on the entire tensor. Its image is the displayed $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span. Let $P$ be the rational matrix whose columns are the Hamilton coordinates of the $b_r$, and let $T$ be the rational matrix whose columns are the order coordinates of the Hamilton basis vectors. The Hamilton basis and $(b_r)$ are ordered bases of the four-dimensional $\mathbb Q$-vector space $B$, so $PT=TP=I_4$ by [F4]. The embedding $\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q_\ell$ preserves the sums, products, $0$ and $1$ in these matrix identities, so the same matrices are inverses over $\mathbb Q_\ell$. By step 2.2, the image of a tensor with column of order coefficients $z$ has Hamilton coordinate column $Pz$. Since $T$ inverts $P$, that image vanishes only when the column $z$ is zero, so the map is injective. The image is therefore a unital subring of $B_\ell$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, step 2.2]

3.2 The multiplication table gives $\overline{xy}=\bar y\bar x$ by bilinearity. For basis pairs involving $1$ or two equal imaginary units the two sides agree directly. For distinct imaginary units their product changes sign both under conjugation and under reversal of factors. Expanding $x\bar x$ or $\bar x x$, the cross terms cancel and each square contributes $x_r^2$, giving $x\bar x=\bar x x=N(x)1$. Thus $(xy)\overline{xy}=x(y\bar y)\bar x=N(y)N(x)1$. Comparing the coefficients of $1$ gives $N(xy)=N(x)N(y)$. If $N(x)$ is a unit of $R$, the displayed identity gives the two-sided inverse $\bar x/N(x)$. Conversely, if $x$ is a unit, applying the norm to $xx^{-1}=1$ gives $N(x)N(x^{-1})=1$. Over a field of coefficients, this criterion is $N(x)\ne 0$. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.2]

4.1 For $x\in O_\ell$, let the $j$-th column of $L_x$ be the $b_r$-coordinates of $xb_j$. This is a $4\times4$ matrix over the commutative ring $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and acts on coordinate columns by left multiplication. The row-by-column rule composes the actions, so associativity gives $L_{xy}=L_xL_y$ and $L_1=I_4$. If $x$ is a unit in $O_\ell$, $L_{x^{-1}}$ thus inverts $L_x$. Conversely, invertibility of $L_x$ gives $y\in O_\ell$ with $xy=1$ by applying the inverse matrix to the coordinate column of $1$. Then $x(yx-1)=0$, so injectivity of $L_x$ gives $yx=1$. Hence $O_\ell^\times$ is specified by $\det L_x\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$. If $a\in\mathbb Q_\ell$ and $a1\in O_\ell$, the integral matrix $L_{a1}=aI_4$ forces $a\in\mathbb Z_\ell$. For a scalar unit of $O_\ell$, applying this to the inverse also gives $a^{-1}\in\mathbb Z_\ell$. Conversely, $a,a^{-1}\in\mathbb Z_\ell$ give $a1,a^{-1}1\in O_\ell$ since it contains $1$ and is a $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module. Thus $O_\ell^\times\cap(\mathbb Q_\ell^\times 1)=\mathbb Z_\ell^\times 1$. 5.1 For continuity of the field operations at $(a,b)$, a pair of increments $h,k$ changes $a+b$ by at most $\max(|h|_\ell,|k|_\ell)$ in absolute value, and changes $ab$ by at most $\max(|a|_\ell|k|_\ell,|b|_\ell|h|_\ell,|h|_\ell|k|_\ell)$. Given an output radius $\varepsilon>0$, set $M=\max(1,|a|_\ell,|b|_\ell)$ and $\delta=\min(1,\varepsilon/M)$. By the completed distance formula in step 2.1, the box $|h|_\ell,|k|_\ell<\delta$ is an open neighbourhood of $(a,b)$ in increment coordinates. It keeps both changes below $M\delta\le\varepsilon$. Thus addition and multiplication are continuous. Coordinate projections are continuous because their open preimages are boxes, and constant functions have either full or empty preimages of open sets. For continuous coordinate functions the preimage of a basic box is a finite intersection of open coordinate preimages. Each open set in the target product is a union of basic boxes by [F5]. Its preimage under the tuple is the union of the corresponding open preimages, so it is open and the tuple is continuous. Compositions are continuous because preimages under a composition are successive preimages. Polynomials are formed by finite compositions of addition and multiplication applied to coordinate projections and constants, so they are continuous. In particular, the linear coordinate maps given by $P$ and $T$ from step 3.1 are continuous in both directions between Hamilton and order coordinates. The order-coordinate map has $O_\ell$ as the preimage of the open box $\mathbb Z_\ell^4$, so $O_\ell$ is open in $B_\ell$. Each entry of $L_x$ is linear in the order coordinates of $x$, by the products of basis elements in step 2.2. The Leibniz formula therefore makes $\det L_x$ a polynomial in those coordinates. The preimage of $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$ under this determinant on $O_\ell$ is open and closed in $O_\ell$. [F3, F4, F5, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 2.1]

6.1 The Hamilton product has polynomial coordinates and is continuous. For $z\ne0$ the open ball about $z$ of radius $|z|_\ell>0$ excludes $0$ by step 2.1, so $\mathbb Q_\ell\setminus\{0\}$ is open. The units of $B_\ell$ are its preimage under the continuous norm $N$ by step 3.2, so the unit set is open. Given $z\ne0$ and $\varepsilon>0$, take $\delta=\min(|z|_\ell,\varepsilon|z|_\ell^2)>0$. In the open ball $|w-z|_\ell<\delta$ of the completed metric, the ultrametric inequality in both directions gives $|w|_\ell=|z|_\ell$, so $w\ne0$. Thus $|z^{-1}-w^{-1}|_\ell=|z-w|_\ell/(|z|_\ell|w|_\ell)<\varepsilon$. This proves continuity of reciprocation, and the inverse formula $\bar x/N(x)$ is then continuous in each coordinate on the unit set. Products and inverses take values in that set by the ring-unit lemma. Basic boxes for pairs of units are traces of ambient basic boxes, so multiplication remains continuous on the product of the unit subspaces. For each operation the preimage of an open set in the unit codomain is the preimage of an ambient open whose trace is that set, giving continuity into the unit subspace. Since $O_\ell$ is a unital subring, its units contain the common identity and are closed under ambient products and inverses. They form an open subgroup of $B_\ell^\times$, by the openness of $O_\ell$ and the open determinant preimage in step 5.1. [F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 5.1] ∎
