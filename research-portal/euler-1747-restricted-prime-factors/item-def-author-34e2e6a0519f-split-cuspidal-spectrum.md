## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice, which provides a choice function for any family of nonempty sets. For $L=\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb A)$ and its scalar subgroup $Z$, identify $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q)$ with its image in $L$ under the entrywise diagonal embedding, which uses the same rational entries at every place. Put $X_L=(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q)Z)\backslash L$, the space of orbits for left multiplication by the subgroup $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q)Z$ with the quotient topology.

Give the additive group $\mathbb A=\mathbb R\times\mathbb A_f$ the product of the usual real topology and the restricted-product topology on the finite adeles. In the additive quotient $\mathbb Q\backslash\mathbb A$, the rational subgroup is diagonal and its cosets carry the quotient topology.

Use a nonzero invariant Radon measure on $X_L$ for right translation by $L$, and translation-invariant Haar probability on the additive quotient $\mathbb Q\backslash\mathbb A$.

For a continuous complex square-integrable function $\phi$ on $X_L$, viewed on $L$ by composition with the coset projection, define its constant term by the complex Lebesgue integral against Haar probability.
$$C\phi(g)=\int_{\mathbb Q\backslash\mathbb A}\phi\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\0&1\end{pmatrix}g\right)\,dx.$$
Write $L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}(X_L)$ for the closure in the square-integral norm of these continuous functions for which $C\phi(g)=0$ for every $g$. An irreducible cuspidal representation with trivial central character is a strongly continuous unitary representation occurring on a nonzero closed invariant subspace of this space with no proper nonzero closed invariant subspace. Its action is right translation, $R(h)\phi(g)=\phi(gh)$. Its projective realization uses the same action through $L/Z$.

## Justification

**Given:** The adele ring, matrix group and quotients in the definition, and the Axiom of Choice.

1.1 The additive adeles are a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group. Indeed $\mathbb R\times\prod_\ell\mathbb Z_\ell$ is an open subgroup with its product topology. Tracing restricted-product boxes onto this block gives the basis that restricts only finitely many coordinates, and every such basic product open is a trace. Thus $[-1,1]\times\prod_\ell\mathbb Z_\ell$ is a compact identity neighborhood. The open coordinate conditions separate distinct adeles. To test the diagonal rational subgroup, use the box $(-1/2,1/2)\times\prod_\ell\mathbb Z_\ell$. For a nonzero rational, take a fraction $a/b$ with the least positive denominator $b$ by [F11], using a positive denominator by moving its sign to the numerator if needed. If $b>1$, let $\ell$ be a prime divisor of $b$. It cannot divide $a$, since cancellation would give a smaller positive denominator. The integer valuation criterion in [F10] gives $v_\ell(a)=0$ and $v_\ell(b)\ge 1$. The rational absolute value formula in [F10] then gives $|a/b|_\ell>1$. Thus a rational integral at every prime is an integer, and the only such integer in the indicated real interval is zero. In a Hausdorff group, a subgroup with an isolated identity is discrete and closed. To see closedness, choose an identity neighborhood $V$ with $V^{-1}V$ meeting the subgroup only in the identity. Each translate $xV$ contains at most one subgroup point, so for $x$ outside the subgroup one can remove that point, if present, to obtain an open neighborhood disjoint from the subgroup. Apply the diagonal ring map of [F1] entrywise to a rational invertible matrix $M$ and its rational inverse $M^{-1}$.
