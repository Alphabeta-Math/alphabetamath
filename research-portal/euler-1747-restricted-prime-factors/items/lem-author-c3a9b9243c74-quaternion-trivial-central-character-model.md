## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $B$ be the rational Hamilton algebra and fix a full order $O\subset B$, a unital subring with a $\mathbb Z$-basis that is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $B$. At each prime $\ell$ put $O_\ell=O\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_\ell\subset B_\ell=B\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q_\ell$. Let $\widehat B^\times$ be the restricted product of $B_\ell^\times$ with respect to $O_\ell^\times$, with its restricted product topology. Give $B_{\mathbb R}^\times$ the subspace topology of Euclidean $\mathbb R^4$ in the Hamilton coordinates and give the full quaternion idele group $\widetilde H=B_{\mathbb R}^\times\times\widehat B^\times$ the product topology. Use the same order in the finite projective construction of $H=G(\mathbb A)$, where $G=PB^\times$, and let $\Gamma=G(\mathbb Q)$ be its diagonal rational subgroup. Embed $B^\times$ in $\widetilde H$ diagonally by extending coefficients at each place.

The center of $\widetilde H$ is the scalar idele group $Z=\mathbb R^\times\times\mathbb A_f^\times$, embedded by multiplying the quaternion identity by each scalar. The real and finite scalar projections give a surjective homomorphism $p:\widetilde H\to H$ with kernel $Z$. On the spaces of right cosets with their quotient topologies it induces an equivariant homeomorphism
$$\Phi:Y=B^\times Z\backslash\widetilde H\longrightarrow X=\Gamma\backslash H,\qquad B^\times Zx\longmapsto\Gamma p(x).$$
Equivariance means that right translation by $a\in\widetilde H$ corresponds to right translation by $p(a)$. For a right-invariant Radon measure $\mu$ on $X$, the measure $\nu(E)=\mu(\Phi(E))$ on Borel sets of $Y$ is right-invariant Radon. Pullback gives a surjective linear isometry from $L^2(X,\mu;\mathbb C)$ to $L^2(Y,\nu;\mathbb C)$. It intertwines the projective right regular action lifted along $p$ with the action of $\widetilde H$ by right translation on $Y$. The latter has trivial central character, meaning that every scalar idele acts as the identity. The projective and lifted actions on $L^2(X,\mu;\mathbb C)$ have the same closed invariant subspaces. These are complex-linear subspaces closed in the $L^2$ norm topology.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice. It implies Countable Choice, which is used for the Hilbert space conclusion for complex $L^2$ ([[math:def-axiom-of-choice]], [[math:thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F1] At each place the Hamilton basis satisfies $i^2=j^2=k^2=-1$, $ij=k=-ji$ and the cyclic relations. The local order $O_\ell$ contains $1$ and has the free $\mathbb Z_\ell$-basis obtained from the chosen order basis. The finite quaternion ideles lie in $O_\ell^\times$ at all but finitely many primes. The finite scalar ideles have nonzero local scalars in $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$ at all but finitely many primes ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]], [[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]], [[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-scalar-adeles-and-ideles]]).

[F2] A topological group has continuous multiplication and inversion ([[math:def-topological-group]]). The real and finite factors of $\widetilde H$ are topological groups in the specified topologies. Their projective groups use the scalar quotient topologies and together form the topological group $H$ with its product topology. Quotient group multiplication is induced by multiplication of representatives. The rational diagonal embedding into the finite ideles extends coefficients, and the image of the full rational diagonal under the scalar projections is $\Gamma$ ([[def-author-5b8600b4f4af-full-adelic-hamilton-group]], [[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]], [[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]], [[math:def-quotient-group]]). The full idele group $\widetilde H$ is the external direct product of its real and finite factors. The product is a group with componentwise multiplication and inversion ([[math:def-external-direct-product-of-groups]], [[math:thm-external-direct-product-is-a-group]]).

[F3] Left and right translations in a topological group are homeomorphisms. Binary product topologies have bases of products of two opens. A set in a quotient topology is open exactly when its preimage under the projection is open. For a subgroup $L$ and an element $x$ in a group, the right coset is $Lx=\{lx:l\in L\}$ ([[math:lem-topological-group-translations-and-inversion]], [[math:def-product-topology]], [[math:def-quotient-topology]], [[math:def-coset]]). Open sets are closed under binary intersections and arbitrary unions ([[math:def-topological-space]]). For a subgroup $L$ and elements $x,y$ of the ambient group, $Lx=Ly$ exactly when $yx^{-1}\in L$ ([[math:lem-coset-membership-and-equality]]).

[F4] The projective quotient $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff under [A1]. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse. Local compactness means that each point has a compact neighborhood, and Hausdorffness means distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods. Continuous maps send compact subsets to compact subsets. Right invariance means $\mu(Dh)=\mu(D)$ for every Borel $D\subset X$ and every $h\in H$ ([[lem-author-5b8600b4f4af-hamilton-invariant-quotient]], [[math:def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps]], [[math:def-locally-compact-space]], [[math:def-hausdorff-space]], [[math:thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]]). A compact subset is compact in its subspace topology, and every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed ([[math:def-compact-space]], [[math:thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed]]). Right multiplication on $X$ is the action $(\Gamma h)a=\Gamma(ha)$, and each right translation is a homeomorphism ([[lem-author-5b8600b4f4af-hamilton-invariant-quotient]]).

[F5] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets. A Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space is a Borel measure finite on compact subsets, outer regular on Borel subsets and inner regular on open subsets ([[math:def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[math:def-radon-measure-on-an-lch-space]]). An algebra of subsets contains the empty set and is closed under complements relative to the ambient set and under binary unions ([[math:def-algebra-of-subsets]]). A sigma-algebra is an algebra of subsets closed under countable unions ([[math:def-sigma-algebra]]). A measure space is a triple $(W,\mathcal A,\lambda)$ with $\mathcal A$ a sigma-algebra on $W$ and $\lambda$ a measure on it ([[math:def-measure-space]]).

[F6] Complex $L^2$ consists of complex measurable functions with finite square integral modulo equality almost everywhere. Under Countable Choice, complex $L^2$ on a measure space is complete for the norm induced by its complex inner product, and this norm is the square root of the square integral ([[math:def-complex-lp-and-euclidean-test-function-conventions]], [[math:lem-complex-lp-completeness-density-and-inner-product]]). A complex Hilbert space is a complex inner-product space complete for its induced norm ([[math:def-hilbert-space]]). The product $K_f=\prod_\ell K_\ell$ of the images of $O_\ell^\times$ is a compact open subgroup of $G(\mathbb A_f)$ ([[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]]). For a nonzero right-invariant Radon measure on $X$, the projective right regular representation $R(h)[f]=[x\mapsto f(xh)]$ is strongly continuous by Justification step 2.1 of the automorphic fixed-space definition, applied with $U=K_f$ ([[def-definite-quaternion-automorphic-fixed-space]]).

[F7] A nonnegative simple measurable function is a finite sum of nonnegative multiples of indicators of pairwise disjoint measurable sets ([[math:def-nonnegative-simple-measurable-function]]). The integral of a nonnegative simple function equals the sum of its coefficients times the measures of their disjoint sets, with zero coefficients contributing zero even on sets of infinite measure ([[math:def-integral-of-a-nonnegative-simple-function]], [[math:prop-the-nonnegative-integral-agrees-with-the-simple-integral]]).

[F8] A nonnegative measurable function is the pointwise increasing limit of nonnegative measurable simple functions. Monotone convergence identifies its integral with the increasing limit of the integrals of these approximations, allowing infinity ([[math:thm-increasing-simple-approximation-of-a-nonnegative-measurable-function]], [[math:thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]).

[F9] The center of a group consists of elements commuting with every group element. A group homomorphism preserves products and its kernel is the preimage of the identity. A subgroup contains the identity and is closed under products and inverses ([[math:def-center-of-a-group]], [[math:def-group-homomorphism]], [[math:def-kernel-and-image-of-group-homomorphism]], [[math:def-subgroup]]).

[F10] A measure has value zero on the empty set and is countably additive on pairwise disjoint measurable sets. A measurable map pulls back measurable sets to measurable sets. Functions agree almost everywhere if they agree outside a measurable set of measure zero ([[math:def-measure]], [[math:def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]], [[math:def-measure-null-set-and-almost-everywhere]]).

[F11] A linear isometry is a linear map preserving norms. For a group representation $\rho$, a closed linear subspace $M$ is invariant if $\rho(g)M=M$ for every group element $g$. An intertwiner $T$ between actions $\rho$ and $\sigma$ is a bounded linear map with $T\rho(g)=\sigma(g)T$ for every $g$ ([[math:def-linear-isometry-and-isometric-isomorphism]], [[math:def-strongly-continuous-unitary-representation]]). A complex-linear subspace contains zero and is closed under addition and multiplication by complex scalars ([[math:def-linear-subspace]]). Closedness in $L^2$ means that the complement is open for the metric $d(u,v)=\|u-v\|_2$ ([[math:def-normed-vector-space-over-an-absolutely-valued-field]], [[math:def-metric-topology]]). A complex-linear map preserves sums and multiplication by complex scalars ([[math:def-linear-map]]). A linear map $T$ between normed spaces is bounded if $\|Tv\|\le C\|v\|$ for some $C\ge0$ and every $v$ ([[math:def-bounded-linear-operator]]). A strongly continuous unitary representation on a complex Hilbert space is a group homomorphism into its bijective complex-linear isometries with norm-continuous orbit maps ([[math:def-strongly-continuous-unitary-representation]]).

[F12] A measurable self-map $\tau$ of a measure space with measure $\lambda$ preserves the measure if $\lambda(\tau^{-1}D)=\lambda(D)$ for every measurable set $D$. For such a map and any nonnegative measurable $g$, $\int g\circ\tau\,d\lambda=\int g\,d\lambda$, allowing infinity ([[math:def-measure-preserving-transformation-and-system]], [[math:thm-integrals-are-invariant-under-measure-preserving-maps]]).

[F13] A rational number lies in $\mathbb Z_\ell$ whenever $\ell$ does not divide its denominator. See [[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-scalar-adeles-and-ideles|the definition of finite scalar adeles and ideles]], Justification step 2.2.

[F14] A map of topological spaces is continuous exactly when every open set has open preimage. It suffices to test the open sets of a basis for the target topology (clauses (b) and (d) of [[math:thm-continuity-characterisations-top]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The order and groups in the statement, a right-invariant Radon measure $\mu$ on $X$, and [A1].

**Goal:** Identify the full quaternion quotient and its invariant measure with the projective model, and show that pullback intertwines the actions and that scalar ideles act trivially.

1.1 By the center criterion in [F9], a central idele commutes with the ideles that equal $i$ or $j$ at a single place and equal $1$ elsewhere. The Hamilton relations in characteristic zero imply that commutation with $i$ eliminates the $j,k$ coefficients and commutation with $j$ then eliminates the $i$ coefficient. Each component is therefore a nonzero scalar. If $t1\in O_\ell$ with $t\in\mathbb Q_\ell$, multiplication by $t1$ preserves $O_\ell$ and acts by the scalar matrix $tI$ in the free $\mathbb Z_\ell$-basis of $O_\ell$. Thus $t\in\mathbb Z_\ell$. Conversely $t1\in O_\ell$ for every $t\in\mathbb Z_\ell$ since $1\in O_\ell$. Applying this intersection equality to $t$ and $t^{-1}$ gives $O_\ell^\times\cap\mathbb Q_\ell^\times 1=\mathbb Z_\ell^\times 1$. The restricted product conditions now identify the central ideles with $Z$, whose scalar components commute with every idele. [F1, F9]

2.1 The product group law and inverses in [F2] give the componentwise operations on $\widetilde H$. By the product topology, the projections to the factor domains of multiplication and inversion are continuous. The preimage of a basic rectangle under either operation is the intersection of the two open preimages obtained from the continuous factor operations and these projections. By the basis test in [F14], both operations are continuous, so $\widetilde H$ is a topological group by [F2]. Multiplication of representatives in [F2] makes the product of the scalar projections a homomorphism $p$. Each factor is surjective by construction and continuous by the quotient topology and [F14]. Hence $p$ is surjective, and preimages of basic open rectangles are open rectangles by [F3], so $p$ is continuous by the basis test in [F14]. Its kernel is the preimage of the identity. A pair maps to the identity exactly when each factor is in its respective scalar subgroup, so $\ker p=Z$. For each component quotient map $q$ and each open $V$ in its domain, $q^{-1}(q(V))$ is the union of the left translates of $V$ by the scalar subgroup. Each translate is open by [F3], and their union is open by the topology axioms in [F3], so the quotient topology makes $q(V)$ open. The image under $p$ of any open set is the union of the images of its basic open rectangles. Each rectangle maps to the product of its two open images, so [F3] gives openness of $p$. The projection $\pi_X:H\to X$ is also open, since $\pi_X^{-1}(\pi_X(V))=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma V$ for open $V\subset H$. [F2, F3, F9, F14, step 1.1]

3.1 For $b\in B^\times$, apply [F13] to the finitely many coefficients of $b$ and $b^{-1}$ in the order basis. Outside the primes dividing a common denominator for these coefficients, both tuples have components in $O_\ell$ by [F1]. Coefficient extension preserves multiplication, so the components of the two tuples are inverse at every prime and belong to $O_\ell^\times$ outside those denominator primes. Thus both tuples are finite quaternion ideles. The real component gives injectivity. By the diagonal descriptions in [F2], $p(b)$ is the diagonal projective class of $b$ and every element of $\Gamma$ is such a class, so $p(B^\times)=\Gamma$. Since $p(1)=p(1)^2$, one has $p(1)=1$. Then $p(uu^{-1})=p(u)p(u^{-1})=1$ gives $p(u^{-1})=p(u)^{-1}$. As $\Gamma$ contains the identity and is closed under products and inverses, these identities and multiplicativity give the same properties for $L=p^{-1}(\Gamma)$, so $L$ is a subgroup. If $x\in L$, choose $b\in B^\times$ with $p(b)=p(x)$. Then $p(b^{-1}x)=1$, so $b^{-1}x\in Z$. Conversely $p(bz)=p(b)$ for $b\in B^\times$ and $z\in Z$. Thus $L=B^\times Z$. [F1, F2, F9, F13, step 2.1]

4.1 By the right-coset criterion in [F3], for $x,y\in\widetilde H$ the equality $Lx=Ly$ holds exactly when $yx^{-1}\in L$, or equivalently $p(y)p(x)^{-1}\in\Gamma$. This last condition is $\Gamma p(x)=\Gamma p(y)$, so the coset map is well defined and injective. It is surjective because $p$ is surjective. For the quotient projection $\pi_Y:\widetilde H\to Y$ and every open $D\subset X$, the set $\pi_Y^{-1}(\Phi^{-1}(D))=p^{-1}(\pi_X^{-1}(D))$ is open by [F3], [F14] and step 2.1. The quotient topology and [F14] then give continuity of $\Phi$. For open $W\subset Y$, its image $\Phi(W)=(\pi_X\circ p)(\pi_Y^{-1}(W))$ is open by step 2.1. By [F14], this proves continuity of $\Phi^{-1}$. Right multiplication by $a$ preserves the representative rule since $(ya)(xa)^{-1}=yx^{-1}$. 5.1 Under [A1], the projective quotient is locally compact Hausdorff by [F4]. Pulling back compact neighborhoods and disjoint open neighborhoods under $\Phi$ shows that $Y$ is locally compact Hausdorff as well. The homeomorphism and its inverse carry opens to opens and compact sets to compact sets. For either map, the subsets of its target with Borel preimages include the empty set and the whole target, and are closed under complements and under binary and countable unions, because inverse images preserve these operations and Borel sets are closed under them. The family is therefore a sigma-algebra by [F5]. They also include all open sets by continuity and [F14]. By the generation property in [F5], every Borel set therefore has Borel preimage in either direction. The formula for $\nu$ gives $\nu(\varnothing)=\mu(\varnothing)=0$. The bijection $\Phi$ sends a countable disjoint union to the disjoint union of the images, so countable additivity of $\mu$ gives countable additivity of $\nu$. Thus $\nu$ is a Borel measure. By [F5], $(X,\mathcal B(X),\mu)$ and $(Y,\mathcal B(Y),\nu)$ are measure spaces, where $\mathcal B$ denotes the Borel sigma-algebra. By [F4], compact subsets of $X$ and $Y$ are closed. Their complements are open, so the compact sets are Borel by [F5]. The correspondences of open and compact sets transfer finiteness on compact sets, outer regularity on Borel sets and inner regularity on open sets from $\mu$ to $\nu$. Right translations on $X$ are homeomorphisms by [F4]. Equivariance transports these homeomorphisms to $Y$. It gives $\nu(Ea)=\mu(\Phi(E)p(a))=\mu(\Phi(E))$ for every Borel $E\subset Y$, using right invariance of $\mu$. [A1, F4, F5, F10, F14, step 4.1]

6.2 By step 5.1 every right translation $\tau$ on either coset space is a homeomorphism, so $\tau$ and its inverse are Borel measurable by the sigma-algebra argument of that step. The inverse image under $\tau$ of a Borel set is its translate by the inverse group element, so right invariance gives $\lambda(\tau^{-1}D)=\lambda(D)$ for the measure $\lambda$ of the corresponding measure space from step 5.1. Thus $\tau$ preserves $\lambda$ by [F12]. For either translation $\tau$, one has $(cf+dg)\circ\tau=c(f\circ\tau)+d(g\circ\tau)$ for complex scalars $c,d$. Hence each translation operator is complex-linear by [F11]. The operator for the inverse group element is its inverse. The identity acts as the identity and $f((xh)k)=f(x(hk))$ gives $R(h)R(k)=R(hk)$. 7.1 Taking $g=|f|^2$ gives norm preservation for pullback. If two representatives agree outside a Borel $\mu$-null set $N$, their pullbacks agree outside $\Phi^{-1}(N)$, which is $\nu$-null. 8.1 The map $T$ is bounded with bound $1$ by [F11], since $\|Tv\|_2=\|v\|_2$ for all $v$. For $a\in\widetilde H$, equivariance gives $f(\Phi(ya))=f(\Phi(y)p(a))$ for every $y\in Y$.
