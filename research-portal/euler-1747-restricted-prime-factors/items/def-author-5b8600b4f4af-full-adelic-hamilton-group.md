## Definition

Let $B$ be the rational Hamilton quaternion algebra and $G=PB^\times$ its group of projective units. Write $G_f=G(\mathbb A_f)$ for the finite adelic group and $G_\infty=G(\mathbb R)=B_{\mathbb R}^\times/\mathbb R^\times$ for the real projective group. In the real quotient, two units represent the same element if they differ by a nonzero real scalar. Give $B_{\mathbb R}^\times$ the subspace topology from Euclidean $\mathbb R^4$ in the Hamilton coordinates and give $G_\infty$ the quotient topology.

Let $\mathbb A_f$ be the finite adele ring. The full adele ring is the product ring $\mathbb A=\mathbb R\times\mathbb A_f$, with componentwise operations. Its rational coefficient map sends $a$ to $(\iota_\infty(a),\iota_f(a))$, where $\iota_\infty$ is the canonical real embedding and $\iota_f$ is the diagonal map into the finite adeles.

The projections identify the full adelic group $H=G(\mathbb A)$ with $G_\infty\times G_f$. Give it componentwise multiplication and the product topology. Write $\Gamma\subset H$ for the diagonal image of $G(\mathbb Q)$, sending each projective rational unit to its real and finite adelic classes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rational Hamilton algebra and a full order $O$ in it, meaning a subring with identity and a $\mathbb Z$-basis that is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $B$.

[F1] The Hamilton product is bilinear in the four coordinates. The norm is $N(x)=\sum_{r=0}^3x_r^2$, with $x\bar x=N(x)1$, where $\bar x$ negates the three imaginary coordinates. The inverse is $\bar x/N(x)$ when $N(x)\ne0$. Over a field, a quaternion is a unit exactly when its norm is nonzero. Each local algebra $B_\ell$ has the topology of four coordinates over the metric field $\mathbb Q_\ell$. Its units carry the subspace topology and have continuous multiplication and inversion ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]]).

[F2] The projective group is a finite-type group scheme over $\mathbb Q$ with an affine model given by polynomial equations. For a field extension $F/\mathbb Q$, its field points are the units of $B_F$ modulo $F^\times$. The affine model's points over $\mathbb A_f$ are identified as a group with $\widehat B^\times/\mathbb A_f^\times$ and hence with the restricted product for the given order. For a unit $x$ the comparison uses matrix entries $t_{rs}=x_rx_s/N(x)$ ([[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]]). Its finite adelic group is the restricted product of $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)=B_\ell^\times/\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ with compact open subgroup $K_f=\prod_\ell K_\ell$, where $K_\ell$ is the image of $O_\ell^\times$. A restricted product consists of tuples in $K_\ell$ at all but finitely many primes, and its basic opens use $K_\ell$ at all but finitely many primes. The restricted product is a topological group. The rational to finite adelic map is injective ([[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]]). The local unit subgroup $O_\ell^\times$ is open in the local quaternion units ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]]).

[F3] A subset of a group is a subgroup if it contains the ambient identity and is closed under multiplication and inverses ([[math:def-subgroup]]). If $Z$ is a subgroup, the left cosets $uZ$ and $vZ$ are equal exactly when $v^{-1}u\in Z$ ([[math:lem-coset-membership-and-equality]]). A subgroup $Z$ of a group $U$ is normal when $gZg^{-1}=Z$ for every $g\in U$ ([[math:def-normal-subgroup]]). The cosets of a normal subgroup form a group whose product and inverse are induced by those of representatives ([[math:def-quotient-group]], [[math:thm-quotient-group-laws]]). The quotient topology tests openness by inverse image ([[math:def-quotient-topology]]). The product topology has basic opens that restrict finitely many coordinates ([[math:def-product-topology]]). A topological group has continuous multiplication and inversion ([[math:def-topological-group]]).

[F4] Hausdorff means distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods. Metrizable spaces are Hausdorff, and singletons in Hausdorff spaces are closed ([[math:def-hausdorff-space]]). A space is locally compact if each point has a compact neighborhood ([[math:def-locally-compact-space]]). A subset is compact when every open cover in its subspace topology has a finite subcover ([[math:def-compact-space]]). For $n\ge1$, a closed, Euclidean-bounded subset of the product of $n$ real lines is compact in its subspace topology ([[math:cor-heine-borel-in-the-product-topology]], claim 2). Continuous images of compact spaces are compact ([[math:thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]]), and finite products of compact spaces are compact ([[math:thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces]]).

[F5] The Euclidean metric on $\mathbb R^4$ is $d_2(x,y)=\sqrt{\sum_{r=0}^3(x_r-y_r)^2}$ ([[math:lem-metrics-on-rn]]), and its topology is the product of the four real-line topologies ([[math:lem-product-topology-on-rn]]). Subspace opens are traces of ambient opens, and the subspace inclusion is continuous ([[math:def-subspace-topology-top]]). Finite sums and products of continuous real functions are continuous, and a quotient is continuous where the denominator is nonzero ([[math:lem-algebra-of-continuous-real-maps-on-a-space]]).

[F6] The Cauchy-sequence real field is complete in the sense of the least-upper-bound property ([[math:cor-cauchy-reals-lub-complete]]). In an ordered field with this property each nonnegative element has a nonnegative square root ([[math:thm-of-square-roots]]). In a complete ordered field every nonzero square is positive ([[math:thm-of-square-roots]]). Positive elements of an ordered field are closed under addition ([[math:def-ordered-field]]).

[F7] The canonical inclusion of $\mathbb Q$ in $\mathbb R$ is a field embedding ([[math:lem-rat-embeds-dense]]). The diagonal finite embedding makes $\mathbb A_f$ a commutative unital $\mathbb Q$-algebra ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-scalar-adeles-and-ideles]]). The product of commutative unital rings is a commutative unital ring with componentwise operations and identity ([[math:def-product-ring]]). A unital ring homomorphism from $\mathbb Q$ to such a ring gives a $\mathbb Q$-algebra, and maps between such algebras commute with their maps from $\mathbb Q$ ([[math:def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]). A map of rings is a unital ring homomorphism exactly when it preserves addition, multiplication and the multiplicative identity ([[math:def-ring-homomorphism]]). A unital ring homomorphism $f$ sends any unit $u$ to a unit with inverse $f(u^{-1})$ ([[math:lem-ring-homomorphism-basic-properties]], clause 3).

[F8] An affine scheme is isomorphic to the spectrum of its coordinate ring ([[math:def-affine-scheme]]). A morphism over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q$ commutes with the structure maps to that base ([[math:def-scheme-over-base]]). For an affine $\mathbb Q$-scheme with coordinate ring $C$ and a commutative unital $\mathbb Q$-algebra $R$, an $R$-point is a morphism $\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{Spec}C$ over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q$ ([[math:def-group-scheme-over-a-field]]). Under the contravariant affine correspondence these are the unital ring maps $C\to R$ that commute with the coefficient maps from $\mathbb Q$ ([[math:thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]], [[math:def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]). For a finite-type group scheme over $\mathbb Q$, maps of commutative unital $\mathbb Q$-algebras induce group homomorphisms on points in the same direction ([[math:def-group-scheme-over-a-field]]).

[F9] A map between topological spaces is continuous exactly when the inverse image of every open set is open (condition (b)). It suffices to test the inverse images of the members of a basis for the target topology (condition (d) for a basis). A continuous map pulls back closed sets to closed sets (condition (c)) ([[math:thm-continuity-characterisations-top]]). Every open set is a union of basic open sets, so each point of an open set lies in a basic open set contained in it ([[math:def-topology-basis-subbasis]]). Arbitrary unions and finite intersections of open sets are open, and finite intersections of closed sets are closed ([[math:def-topological-space]], axioms (T1)–(T3) and (C1)–(C2)).

[F10] In a topological group every left and right translation is a homeomorphism ([[math:lem-topological-group-translations-and-inversion]]). A map is open when it sends every open set to an open set. A homeomorphism is open because its inverse is continuous ([[math:def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps]]).

[F11] The open metric ball about $x_0$ of radius $r>0$ consists of the points $x$ with $d(x_0,x)<r$ ([[math:def-metric-ball]]). A subset of a metric space is bounded if it is empty or is contained in an open ball ([[math:def-metric-bounded-diameter]]).

[F12] The direct product of two groups has componentwise multiplication and inversion, and its identity is the pair of identities ([[math:def-external-direct-product-of-groups]], [[math:thm-external-direct-product-is-a-group]]). A map of groups that preserves multiplication is a group homomorphism ([[math:def-group-homomorphism]]). A bijective group homomorphism is a group isomorphism ([[math:def-group-isomorphism-and-automorphism]]).

## Justification

**Given:** The rational Hamilton algebra, a full order and the topologies specified above.

**Goal:** Verify the real and full adelic group constructions and show that $G_\infty$ is compact Hausdorff, $H$ is locally compact Hausdorff and the rational map into $H$ is injective.

1.1 The real coordinate projections are continuous by the Euclidean product description. The Hamilton product has polynomial coordinates, and inversion has quotient coordinates with nonzero denominator on the units. On the real unit domains these formulas are continuous coordinatewise by [F5], using the product and subspace topologies for the domain projections. The preimages of basic open boxes under these coordinate formulas are finite intersections of open coordinate preimages, so the maps into the ambient space are continuous by the basis test in [F9]. Both operations take values in the units, so the preimage of a subspace open set is the preimage of its ambient open set by the trace description in [F5]. This gives continuity into the unit group by the open-set test in [F9]. For the local unit groups, continuity holds by [F1]. [F1, F3, F5, F9]

1.2 The diagonal rational coefficient map preserves addition, multiplication and the identity in each component by [F7]. Its image is central because the product ring is commutative. The projections are unital ring maps that respect these rational coefficient maps. Write $C$ for the coordinate ring of the affine matrix model of $G$ in [F2]. Write $\eta_C:\mathbb Q\to C$ and $\eta_R:\mathbb Q\to R$ for the coefficient maps. For a ring map $u:C\to R$, the condition $u\circ\eta_C=\eta_R$ is equivalent by contravariance and faithfulness in [F8] to $\operatorname{Spec}(\eta_C)\circ\operatorname{Spec}(u)=\operatorname{Spec}(\eta_R)$. This is the condition that the scheme morphism commutes with the structure maps to $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q$. A map $C\to\mathbb A$ of $\mathbb Q$-algebras is exactly a pair of such maps to $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb A_f$, since pairing the maps preserves operations, identity and rational scalars componentwise. This yields the asserted bijection on points by [F8]. By [F2] the real field points give $G_\infty$ and the finite scheme points give the projective idele group with the restricted-product description, namely $G_f$. By naturality in [F8], both projections induce group homomorphisms on points. The resulting map to the direct product preserves multiplication in each component, hence is a group homomorphism for the componentwise law in [F12]. Since it is bijective, [F12] makes it an isomorphism. [F2, F7, F8, F12]

2.1 For a real unit $x$, at least one coordinate is nonzero by [F1]. By [F6] each nonzero coordinate has a positive square. Dropping the zero squares leaves a nonempty sum of positive terms, and repeated addition preserves positivity. Thus $N(x)>0$. By [F6] there is $r\ge 0$ with $r^2=N(x)$. Then $r>0$ and $N(r^{-1}x)=r^{-2}N(x)=1$, so every real projective class has a representative on the sphere $S=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:N(x)=1\}$. The coordinate projections and finite sums and products in [F5] give continuity of $N$ on $\mathbb R^4$. Since the real line is metrizable, $\{1\}$ is closed by [F4], and $S=N^{-1}(\{1\})$ is closed by [F9]. Each point of $S$ has $d_2(0,x)=\sqrt{N(x)}=1<2$, so $S$ lies in the open Euclidean ball of radius $2$ about $0$ and is bounded by [F11]. The topological form of Heine–Borel in [F4] gives compactness in the Euclidean subspace topology, which is inherited from the product topology by [F5]. For an open set $W$ of $G_\infty$, its preimage under the scalar quotient map is open in $B_{\mathbb R}^\times$ by [F3]. Intersecting this preimage with $S$ gives an open set of $S$ by the subspace topology in [F5]. Thus the restricted quotient map from $S$ is continuous by [F9]. Its image is $G_\infty$, which is therefore compact by [F4]. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F9, F11, step 1.1]

2.2 In each real or local unit group, the nonzero scalars form a subgroup by [F3] because they contain the ambient identity and are closed under products and inverses. For any quaternion unit $g$ and nonzero scalar $s$, $g(s1)g^{-1}=s1$ because $s1$ commutes with $g$. Thus conjugation fixes the scalar subgroup pointwise and it is normal by [F3], so the coset laws apply. The scalars are precisely the intersection, within the units, of the three inverse images of $\{0\}$ under the imaginary coordinate projections. These projections are continuous by the coordinate product topology and subspace topology. The coefficient fields $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb Q_\ell$ are metrizable, so $\{0\}$ is closed by [F4]. The three inverse images are closed by [F9], so their finite intersection is closed by the closed-set axiom there. Write $Z$ for the scalar subgroup and $p$ for the scalar quotient map. By [F3], $p(u)=p(v)$ exactly when $v^{-1}u\in Z$. For an open set $V$, this gives $p^{-1}(p(V))=\bigcup_{v\in V}vZ=\bigcup_{z\in Z}Vz$. The translates are open by [F10], applied to the unit groups in step 1.1, so their union is open by [F9] and $p$ is open by the quotient topology. For open subsets $U,V$ of the unit group, $(p\times p)(U\times V)=p(U)\times p(V)$ is open by the product topology in [F3]. Every open set in the domain is a union of such rectangles by [F9], and its image is the union of the open images of those rectangles since images commute with unions. Thus $p\times p$ is open by [F9] and [F10], and it is surjective because $p$ is surjective. The pullback under $p\times p$ of a multiplication preimage downstairs is the multiplication preimage upstairs of the corresponding pullback under $p$. The latter is open for an open set downstairs, and its image under $p\times p$ is the multiplication preimage downstairs. The same calculation with $p$ proves that the inversion preimage downstairs is open. Both quotient operations are therefore continuous by the open-set test in [F9]. For distinct scalar cosets of $a$ and $b$, the element $b^{-1}a$ is outside the closed scalar subgroup by [F3]. The map $(v,u)\mapsto(v^{-1},u)$ is continuous because the inverse image of a basic open rectangle $V\times U$ is the open rectangle $\{v:v^{-1}\in V\}\times U$ by continuity of inversion and the product topology in [F3]. The basis test in [F9] therefore applies. Composing with continuous multiplication gives the continuous map $(v,u)\mapsto v^{-1}u$, since inverse images under the composition are successive inverse images of opens. Its preimage of the complement of the scalar subgroup is open and contains $(b,a)$. By [F3] and [F9] it contains an open rectangle about $(b,a)$. The images of the two factors of this rectangle under $p$ are open and disjoint, since equal images of $v,u$ would make $v^{-1}u$ scalar by [F3]. Each $K_\ell$ is open because it is the image of the open local unit subgroup under the open quotient map. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F9, F10, step 1.1]

3.1 For an open set $W\subseteq G(\mathbb Q_\ell)$, every tuple in its preimage under the $\ell$-th coordinate map from $G_f$ has a basic box about it. Intersect the factor at $\ell$ with $W$ and leave the other factors as they are. This is still a restricted-product basic box by [F2]. The preimage is a union of these boxes and is open by [F9], so the coordinate map is continuous by the open-set test there. Distinct tuples can therefore be separated in a coordinate where they differ, using the local Hausdorff property of step 2.2. The real and finite factors are Hausdorff topological groups by step 2.2 and [F2]. The product topology separates points in any differing factor. For the componentwise multiplication and inversion of [F12], a basic open rectangle pulls back to the intersection of the inverse images of its two open factors. Each is open by continuity of the factor operations and the product projection formulas in [F3]. Their intersection is open by [F9]. The basis test in [F9] gives continuity of the two operations on the full product. The subgroup $K_f$ is compact and open in $G_f$ by [F2]. The subspace topology on $G_\infty\times K_f$ is the product of the indicated factor topologies, since basic rectangles restrict to rectangles with open factors. Thus $G_\infty\times K_f$ is compact by [F4] and step 2.1, and is open in $H$. Its left translate by any $h\in H$ is compact and open, by the translation homeomorphism in [F10] and the compact-image assertion in [F4]. The original product contains the identity, so the translate is an open set containing $h$ and is thus a neighborhood of $h$. This proves local compactness by [F4]. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F9, F10, F12, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 The rational coefficient map induces a group homomorphism $G(\mathbb Q)\to H$. Each projection sends the class of a unit of $B$ to its class after extending coefficients in that factor, since the Hamilton coordinates and scalar equivalence are preserved by the embeddings. For $x\in B^\times$, the rational norm $N(x)$ is nonzero by [F1], so it is a unit of $\mathbb Q$. By [F7], $\iota_f(N(x))$ is a unit with inverse $\iota_f(N(x)^{-1})$. The formula $t_{rs}=x_rx_s/N(x)$ in [F2] therefore commutes with the finite coefficient map, which preserves sums, products and this inverse. Under the finite comparison in [F2], this component is therefore the diagonal projective idele map. The rational homomorphism into $H$ is injective because that finite component is injective. [F1, F2, F7, F8, step 1.2] ∎
