## Statement

Let $B$ be the rational Hamilton quaternion algebra, with reduced norm $N$, and let $O$ be an order, a unital subring that is a full integral lattice in $B$. At a prime $\ell$ write $B_\ell$ for the quaternion algebra with coefficients in $\mathbb Q_\ell$ and $O_\ell$ for the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span of $O$. The superscript $\times$ denotes the elements with two-sided inverses in the indicated ring. A finite quaternion idele $x=(x_\ell)_\ell$ has $x_\ell\in B_\ell^\times$ everywhere and $x_\ell\in O_\ell^\times$ for all but finitely many primes. Write $\widehat B^\times$ for the group of these tuples and $\widehat O^\times=\prod_\ell O_\ell^\times$.

The assignment
$$x\widehat O^\times\longmapsto I_x=\{b\in B:b\in x_\ell O_\ell\text{ for every prime }\ell\}$$
is a bijection from the cosets for right multiplication by $\widehat O^\times$ to the locally principal right fractional $O$-ideals. Such an ideal is a full integral lattice stable under right multiplication by $O$ whose completed span at each prime is generated by one invertible local quaternion on the left, meaning a generator in $B_\ell^\times$. The completion satisfies $(I_x)_\ell=x_\ell O_\ell$. Moreover, $I_x\subseteq O$ exactly when $x_\ell O_\ell\subseteq O_\ell$ for every $\ell$. If $d$ is the positive generator of the integer span of the norms $N(b)$ for $b\in I_x$, then
$$v_\ell(d)=v_\ell(N(x_\ell))\quad\text{for every prime }\ell,\qquad d=\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(N(x_\ell))}.$$

For a rational unit $a\in B^\times$ and a locally principal right fractional $O$-ideal $I$, write $aI=\{ab:b\in I\}$. This is again a locally principal right fractional $O$-ideal, and $I_{ax}=aI_x$. Call two such ideals $I,J$ equivalent if $J=aI$ for some $a\in B^\times$. The double cosets $B^\times\backslash\widehat B^\times/\widehat O^\times$ classify these equivalence classes. A double coset consists of the representatives $a x k$ with $a\in B^\times$ and $k\in\widehat O^\times$.

Write $G(\mathbb Q)=B^\times/\mathbb Q^\times$, $G(\mathbb A_f)=\widehat B^\times/\mathbb A_f^\times$, and $K$ for the image of $\widehat O^\times$ in $G(\mathbb A_f)$. Here $\mathbb A_f^\times$ is the group of finite scalar ideles, the tuples in $\prod_\ell\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ that belong to $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$ at all but finitely many primes, embedded as scalar quaternions. Projection induces a bijection from the unprojectivized double cosets to $G(\mathbb Q)\backslash G(\mathbb A_f)/K$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The Hamilton products over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb Q_\ell$ are associative and bilinear, with the same rational multiplication law under coefficient extension and with scalars central ([[def-author-24fda87362bc-hamilton-algebra-and-reduced-norm]]). An order and each of its completed spans are unital subrings. A rational basis of the order that generates it over $\mathbb Z$ remains a basis over $\mathbb Q_\ell$ and spans $O_\ell$ over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ ([[def-author-24fda87362bc-quaternion-orders-and-lattice-completions]]).

[F2] The quaternion idele group and its integral unit subgroup use coordinatewise multiplication ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]]). For a subgroup $U$ of a group, $xU=\{xu:u\in U\}$ and $yU=xU$ exactly when $x^{-1}y\in U$, since $y\in yU$ and multiplication by an element of $U$ permutes $U$ ([[math:def-coset]]).

[F3] A family of local $\mathbb Z_\ell$-spans of $\mathbb Q_\ell$-bases equal to $O_\ell$ outside finitely many primes comes from one full integral lattice by rational intersection. Every rational full lattice is recovered by that intersection and has the same completion as $O$ outside finitely many primes ([[lem-author-f5ba73d33e6e-reconstructing-quaternion-lattices]]).

[F4] A locally principal right fractional ideal has a positive rational norm generator $d$ over $\mathbb Z$. Its completion at $\ell$ has local norm ideal $d\mathbb Z_\ell$, where the local norm ideal is the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span of the norms of elements, and a local lattice generated by $x_\ell\in B_\ell^\times$ has local norm ideal $N(x_\ell)\mathbb Z_\ell$ ([[def-quaternion-right-ideal-and-reduced-norm]], [[lem-author-24fda87362bc-quaternion-lattice-norms-and-local-generators]]). Here $v_\ell$ is the normalized valuation with $v_\ell(\ell)=1$, additive under multiplication of nonzero scalars and zero exactly on units of $\mathbb Z_\ell$. On nonzero rationals it is the exponent in the numerator minus the exponent in the denominator ([[def-author-24fda87362bc-local-adic-valuation]]). Here $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is the completion of $\mathbb Q$ for the absolute value $\ell^{-v_\ell(\cdot)}$ on nonzero inputs, with absolute value zero at zero. The ring $\mathbb Z_\ell$ consists of the elements of nonnegative valuation, including zero. Positive integers have unique prime factorizations ([[math:thm-fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic]]).

[F5] The two projective unit groups are the quotient groups by the indicated rational and finite scalar subgroups, and the rational projective group embeds by the diagonal map ([[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]]). A finite scalar idele is a nonzero scalar in each coordinate and a unit of $\mathbb Z_\ell$ in all but finitely many coordinates ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-scalar-adeles-and-ideles]]).

[F6] For subgroups $L,R$ of a group $H$, the double coset of $h$ is $LhR=\{u h v:u\in L,\ v\in R\}$ ([[math:def-double-cosets-of-two-subgroups]]). The double cosets of $h$ and $h'$ are equal exactly when $h'=u h v$ for such $u,v$. Indeed, $h'$ belongs to its own double coset, and if $h'=u h v$ then $Lh'R=LhR$ because $Lu=L$ and $vR=R$.

[F7] A finite set admits a bijection from some natural number $n$ viewed as the set of its predecessors ([[math:def-countable]]). If $F$ is a function with domain $n$ whose values are all nonempty sets, its family of values has a function $g$ satisfying $g(S)\in S$ for every set $S$ in that family ([[math:lem-finite-choice]]).

## Proof

**Given:** An order $O$ in $B$ and the local orders, unit groups and norm conventions above.

**Goal:** Establish the ideal bijection, the local tests, and the two equivalent descriptions of the ideal classes.

1.1 For $x\in\widehat B^\times$, set $J_\ell=x_\ell O_\ell$. Take an order basis $b_1,\ldots,b_4$ as in [F1]. Given $w\in B_\ell$, expand $x_\ell^{-1}w$ in that basis over $\mathbb Q_\ell$ and multiply the expansion by $x_\ell$ on the left. Bilinearity and associativity express $w$ in the vectors $x_\ell b_1,\ldots,x_\ell b_4$. Applying $x_\ell^{-1}$ on the left to a linear relation among these vectors gives the same relation among the $b_i$, so all coefficients vanish. The same bilinearity shows that $J_\ell$ is the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span of this basis. If $x_\ell\in O_\ell^\times$, then $x_\ell O_\ell=O_\ell$ by closure under multiplication by $x_\ell$ and by $x_\ell^{-1}$. Thus [F3] gives a full integral lattice $I_x$ with these completions. Each $J_\ell$ is stable on the right under $O_\ell$ because $O_\ell$ is a ring. For $b\in I_x$ and $o\in O$, this gives $bo\in J_\ell$ at every prime and hence $bo\in I_x$. So $I_x$ is a locally principal right fractional ideal. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 For two ideles $x,y$, the equality $J_\ell=x_\ell O_\ell=y_\ell O_\ell$ holds exactly when $h=x_\ell^{-1}y_\ell$ satisfies $hO_\ell=O_\ell$. If the latter equality holds then $h=h\cdot1\in O_\ell$ and $1=hu$ for some $u\in O_\ell$, so $h^{-1}=u\in O_\ell$. Conversely an element of $O_\ell^\times$ acts bijectively on $O_\ell$. It follows from step 1.1 that $I_x=I_y$ exactly when all these local spans agree, using recovery by intersection for the reverse implication. This is equivalent to $x^{-1}y\in\widehat O^\times$, which is exactly equality of the cosets. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

2.2 If $I_x\subseteq O$, taking completed spans gives $x_\ell O_\ell\subseteq O_\ell$ for every $\ell$. Conversely these inclusions put every element of $I_x$ in all $O_\ell$, whose rational intersection is $O$. For the norm claim [F4] gives $d\mathbb Z_\ell=N(x_\ell)\mathbb Z_\ell$. The ratio of the generators and its reciprocal are both in $\mathbb Z_\ell$, so the ratio is a unit and the generators have equal valuations. Factoring the positive numerator and denominator of $d$ gives $d=\prod_\ell \ell^{v_\ell(d)}$. This is a finite product of its nontrivial factors because the numerator and denominator are integers. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

2.3 For $a\in B^\times$, express $a$ and $a^{-1}$ in an order basis. The denominators of their finitely many rational coefficients have only finitely many prime divisors. At every other prime, each nonzero coefficient has nonnegative valuation because its denominator has valuation zero, so all the coefficients of both elements lie in $\mathbb Z_\ell$. Thus $a$ and its inverse lie in $O_\ell$ at those primes. The diagonal map preserves products and is injective by the coordinate inclusion $\mathbb Q\subseteq\mathbb Q_\ell$, so it identifies $B^\times$ with a subgroup of $\widehat B^\times$. For any $b\in B$, the membership $b\in a x_\ell O_\ell$ is equivalent to $a^{-1}b\in x_\ell O_\ell$. Intersecting the conditions over all primes yields $I_{ax}=aI_x$. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $I$ be a locally principal right fractional $O$-ideal. The set $E=\{\ell:I_\ell\ne O_\ell\}$ of exceptional primes is finite by [F3], so take a bijection $e:n\to E$ by [F7]. For each $i\in n$ put $S_i=\{u\in B_{e(i)}^\times:uO_{e(i)}=I_{e(i)}\}$. Each $S_i$ is nonempty by local principality. Apply [F7] to the function $i\mapsto S_i$ on $n$ to obtain a choice function $g$ on its family of values. Set $x_{e(i)}=g(S_i)$ for $i\in n$ and $x_\ell=1$ for $\ell\notin E$. Thus $x_\ell O_\ell=I_\ell$ at every prime, and $x$ is a finite quaternion idele since $1\in O_\ell^\times$ outside $E$. The intersection of these spans is $I$ by [F3]. This proves surjectivity and completes the bijection. [given, F2, F3, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 By step 3.1, every locally principal right fractional ideal has the form $I_x$, and $aI_x=I_{ax}$ is again such an ideal by steps 2.3 and 1.1. For any such ideal $I$ and rational units $a,b$, the identity gives $1I=I$ and associativity gives $b(aI)=(ba)I$. Thus $I$ is equivalent to itself, $J=aI$ gives $I=a^{-1}J$, and $J=aI$ and $L=bJ$ give $L=(ba)I$. For ideles $x,y$, the ideals $I_x,I_y$ are equivalent exactly when $I_y=I_{ax}$ for some $a\in B^\times$, which by step 2.1 holds exactly when $y=axk$ for some $k\in\widehat O^\times$. By [F6] this is equality of double cosets. [F1, F2, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.3]

5.1 Let $\pi:\widehat B^\times\to G(\mathbb A_f)$ be the quotient map. The rational projective group embeds in $G(\mathbb A_f)$ by [F5]. The image $K=\pi(\widehat O^\times)$ is a subgroup since $\pi$ preserves products, identities and inverses. If $y=a x k$ with $a\in B^\times$ and $k\in\widehat O^\times$, then $\pi(y)=\pi(a)\pi(x)\pi(k)$. By [F6] the projective double coset thus depends only on the original double coset. Every projective representative lifts to a quaternion idele by surjectivity of $\pi$, giving surjectivity on double cosets. [F2, F5, F6, step 2.3, step 4.1]

6.1 If $\pi(x)$ and $\pi(y)$ belong to the same projective double coset, [F6] gives $\pi(y)=\bar a\pi(x)\bar k$ with $\bar a\in G(\mathbb Q)$ and $\bar k\in K$. Choose lifts $a\in B^\times$ and $k\in\widehat O^\times$ of these multipliers. The quotient descriptions give $y=c a x k$ for a finite scalar idele $c=(c_\ell)_\ell$. Set $r=\prod_\ell\ell^{v_\ell(c_\ell)}\in\mathbb Q^\times$, which uses only finitely many nonzero exponents by [F5]. At a fixed prime $\ell$, any distinct prime $q$ is a rational integer with $v_\ell(q)=0$. Additivity on products and the rule $v_\ell(t^{-1})=-v_\ell(t)$ give $v_\ell(r)=v_\ell(c_\ell)$, using $v_\ell(\ell)=1$ for the factor at $\ell$. Hence $e_\ell=c_\ell/r\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$. Both $e_\ell\cdot 1$ and its inverse belong to $O_\ell$ because that ring contains the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span of $1$. So $e=(e_\ell\cdot 1)_\ell\in\widehat O^\times$. Centrality gives $y=(ra)x(ek)$, which puts $x,y$ in the same unprojectivized double coset by [F6]. This proves the projective bijection. [F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, step 2.3, step 5.1] ∎
