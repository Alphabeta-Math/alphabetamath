## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Write $H=G(\mathbb A)$ for the full adelic group of rational Hamilton projective units, $\Gamma=G(\mathbb Q)$ for its diagonal rational subgroup, and $X=\Gamma\backslash H$ for the quotient by left multiplication by $\Gamma$. The group $H$ is unimodular, meaning its left Haar measure is also right invariant. The space $X$ has a nonzero finite right-invariant Radon measure, and any two nonzero right-invariant Radon measures on $X$ differ by a positive scalar. A Radon measure is a Borel measure finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets and inner regular on open sets.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] The Axiom of Choice gives a choice function for each indexed family of nonempty sets. It implies Dependent Choice, which permits successive choices along a relation with a successor at every point. These principles supply Haar measure, the quotient criterion and averaging, and uniqueness of Radon measures from continuous test integrals ([[math:def-axiom-of-choice]], [[math:thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The group $H$ is a locally compact Hausdorff topological group, and $\Gamma$ is closed and discrete. The coset space $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff, with a continuous right action of $H$ and a nonzero right-invariant Radon measure. Right invariance means that $\mu(Ea)=\mu(E)$ for every Borel set $E$ and every $a\in H$. The quotient $X$ is compact, so every Radon measure on it is finite ([[def-author-5b8600b4f4af-full-adelic-hamilton-group]], [[lem-author-5b8600b4f4af-hamilton-invariant-quotient]], [[lem-author-c3a9b9243c74-compact-projective-hamilton-quotient]], [[math:def-radon-measure-on-an-lch-space]]).

[F2] Left Haar measure exists on a locally compact Hausdorff group under [A1]. A left Haar measure is a nonzero left-translation-invariant Radon measure on a group. Its modular function $\Delta_H$ is the continuous homomorphism to the positive reals such that right translation by $h$ scales the left Haar integral by $\Delta_H(h)^{-1}$ and pushes left Haar measure forward to $\Delta_H(h)^{-1}$ times itself. The subspace topology has open sets given by traces of ambient open sets. By [F1], every singleton of $\Gamma$ is open in this topology. Every subset is a union of these singletons, so $\Gamma$ has the discrete topology. In $\Gamma\times\Gamma$ each singleton is a rectangle of open singletons and hence is open by [F5]. Its subsets are likewise unions of open singletons, so $\Gamma\times\Gamma$ is discrete. Since preimages of open sets under inversion and multiplication are open in their respective discrete domains, [F5] makes both operations continuous. Thus $\Gamma$ is a topological group. Distinct points have disjoint open singleton neighborhoods, which gives Hausdorffness. In the subspace topology on a singleton, any open cover has a member containing its point, so that member alone is a finite subcover. Thus every point of $\Gamma$ has an open compact singleton neighborhood, which gives local compactness. Counting measure on the discrete group $\Gamma$ is both left and right Haar, so $\Delta_\Gamma=1$ ([[math:cor-existence-of-left-and-right-haar-measures]], [[math:def-left-haar-integral-and-left-haar-measure]], [[math:def-modular-function-of-a-locally-compact-group]], [[math:thm-the-modular-function-is-a-continuous-homomorphism]], [[math:lem-counting-measure-on-a-discrete-group]], [[lem-author-5b8600b4f4af-hamilton-invariant-quotient]], [[math:def-hausdorff-space]], [[math:def-compact-space]], [[math:def-locally-compact-space]], [[math:def-subspace-topology-top]], [[math:def-standard-topologies]]).

[F3] Since $\Gamma$ is closed, $Y=H/\Gamma$ with its quotient topology is locally compact Hausdorff. The invariant-measure criterion for its left action gives $\Delta_H|_\Gamma=\Delta_\Gamma$ when a nonzero invariant Radon measure exists. For such a measure $\eta$, the necessity argument in the criterion’s proof constructs a left Haar measure on $H$ represented on real test functions by the positive functional $f\mapsto\int_Y T_\Gamma f\,d\eta$. Here $T_\Gamma f(h\Gamma)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma} f(h\gamma)$ is quotient averaging with counting measure, onto $C_c(Y)$ from $C_c(H)$. The notation $C_c$ denotes continuous functions whose support, the closure of the nonzero set, is compact ([[math:prop-invariant-measure-on-g-mod-h-iff-modular-functions-agree]], [[math:lem-closed-subgroup-quotient-averaging-and-compact-lifts|Compact lifts and averaging onto $C_c(G/H)$]], [[math:def-compact-support-c-c-and-c-zero-on-an-lch-space]]).

[F4] Left Haar measures are unique up to a positive scalar under [A1]. On a locally compact Hausdorff space, Radon measures agreeing on $C_c$ are equal under Dependent Choice. Continuous maps send compact sets to compact sets. Every continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact space attains its maximum and minimum. Compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are closed. ([[math:thm-uniqueness-of-left-haar-measure-up-to-scale]], [[math:thm-rmk-uniqueness-among-radon-measures]], [[math:thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]], [[math:thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed]]).

[F5] Let $q_X(h)=\Gamma h$ and $q_Y(h)=h\Gamma$ be the coset projections from $H$. A subset of either quotient is open exactly when its preimage under the corresponding projection is open in $H$. The right coset $\Gamma h$ consists of the elements $\gamma h$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma$, and the left coset $h\Gamma$ consists of the elements $h\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma$. Inversion is continuous in a topological group. A map is continuous exactly when the preimage of every open set is open. This is the equivalence of conditions (a) and (b) of the continuity criterion, taking all open sets in the codomain as the subbasis. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse. In a binary product, rectangles of open sets form a basis. An arbitrary union of open sets is open. ([[math:def-quotient-topology]], [[math:def-coset]], [[math:def-topological-group]], [[math:def-continuous-map-top]], [[math:thm-continuity-characterisations-top]], [[math:def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps]], [[math:def-product-topology]], [[math:def-topological-space]], [[math:def-topology-basis-subbasis]]).

[F6] A sigma-algebra on a set contains the whole set and is closed under complements relative to it and countable unions. These conditions also suffice because the complement of the whole set is empty and a finite union is a countable union after padding with empty sets. The Borel sigma-algebra of a topological space is generated by its open sets. It is contained in every sigma-algebra containing those open sets. A Borel measure takes values in $[0,+\infty]$, vanishes on the empty set and is countably additive on pairwise disjoint Borel sets. A nonnegative extended series is the supremum of its finite partial sums. ([[math:def-sigma-algebra]], [[math:def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[math:def-measure]], [[math:def-nonnegative-extended-series]]).

[F7] A real-valued function on a topological space with its Borel sigma-algebra is measurable when its strict upper level sets are Borel for every real level. A nonnegative simple measurable function has finite range and can be written on pairwise disjoint measurable sets with nonnegative coefficients. The integral of a nonnegative simple function given on disjoint measurable sets is the sum of its values on those sets weighted by their measures, with $0\cdot(+\infty)=0$. The simple integral is independent of the chosen disjoint representation. The integral of a nonnegative measurable function is the supremum of the integrals of its nonnegative simple minorants. A real measurable function $f$ is integrable when both $f^+=\max(f,0)$ and $f^-=\max(-f,0)$ have finite integral. The integral of an integrable real function is the integral of its positive part minus the integral of its negative part. ([[math:def-extended-real-valued-measurable-function]], [[math:def-nonnegative-simple-measurable-function]], [[math:def-integral-of-a-nonnegative-simple-function]], [[math:lem-well-definedness-of-the-simple-integral]], [[math:def-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]], [[math:def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The groups in the statement and [A1].

**Goal:** Establish unimodularity and uniqueness of the finite invariant measure up to scale.

1.1 If $h$ is replaced by $\gamma h$ in $q_X(h)$, its inverse is $h^{-1}\gamma^{-1}$, which has the same image under $q_Y$ as $h^{-1}$. If $h$ is replaced by $h\gamma$ in $q_Y(h)$, its inverse is $\gamma^{-1}h^{-1}$, which has the same image under $q_X$ as $h^{-1}$. Thus $I(\Gamma h)=h^{-1}\Gamma$ and $J(h\Gamma)=\Gamma h^{-1}$ are well defined and inverse to each other. For open $V\subseteq Y$, the $q_X$-preimage of $I^{-1}(V)$ is the preimage of $q_Y^{-1}(V)$ under inversion in $H$, hence open by [F1] and [F5]. Thus $I^{-1}(V)$ is open. For open $W\subseteq X$, the $q_Y$-preimage of $J^{-1}(W)$ is the preimage of $q_X^{-1}(W)$ under the same inversion, so $J^{-1}(W)$ is open too. This gives a homeomorphism. [F1, F5]

1.2 If $\rho$ is a Radon measure and $c>0$ is finite, define $(c\rho)(E)=c\rho(E)$ on Borel sets. Multiplication by $c$ is an order-preserving bijection of $[0,+\infty]$, so it preserves the suprema of partial sums in countable additivity and the infima and suprema in Radon regularity. The partial sums for $c\rho$ are $c$ times those for $\rho$ by distributivity, including $c(+\infty)=+\infty$. The rescaling also preserves zero mass of the empty set and finiteness on compact sets. Thus $c\rho$ is Radon. Use the same disjoint representation for each simple function under both measures. Its coefficient sum is independent of that representation by [F7]. Multiplying the measure by $c$ scales each summand by $c$, including the zero terms by the convention in [F7]. Taking the supremum over the same simple minorants gives $\int g\,d(c\rho)=c\int g\,d\rho$ for nonnegative measurable $g$. Subtracting the finite integrals of the negative parts from those of the positive parts gives the same identity for integrable real functions. [F1, F6, F7]

1.3 Let $f$ be a real compactly supported continuous function on $H$ or $Y$, and let $\rho$ be a Radon measure on its domain. For any real $a$, the set $\{f>a\}$ is open by continuity. For $a\ge0$ the sets $\{f^+>a\}$ and $\{f^->a\}$ are respectively $\{f>a\}$ and $\{f<-a\}$, which are open. For $a<0$ both sign-part level sets are the whole space. Thus $f$ and both sign parts are Borel measurable by [F6] and [F7]. Write $K$ for the compact support of $f$. A preimage of an open set under the restriction of $f$ to $K$ is the trace on $K$ of the corresponding open preimage under $f$, so this restriction is continuous by [F2] and [F5]. On $K$, the maximum and minimum of $f$ given by [F4] yield a finite bound $M>0$ for $|f|$ when $K$ is nonempty. If $K$ is empty, take $M=1$. Both sign parts vanish outside $K$ by [F3]. The set $K$ is closed by [F4] and therefore Borel. For a nonnegative simple minorant $s=\sum_j c_j\mathbf{1}_{E_j}$ of either sign part, each disjoint measurable $E_j$ with $c_j>0$ lies in $K$ and has $c_j\le M$ if $E_j$ is nonempty. Discard empty sets and terms with $c_j=0$. The remainder $K\setminus\bigcup_j E_j$ is the intersection of $K$ with the complement of a finite measurable union, so it is measurable by [F6] and disjoint from the retained sets. Apply countable additivity to the retained sets and the remainder, padded with empty sets. The partial sums are constant after the last of these terms, so [F6] gives $\rho(K)=\sum_j\rho(E_j)+\rho(K\setminus\bigcup_j E_j)$ and hence $\sum_j\rho(E_j)\le\rho(K)$. The coefficient sum for the retained disjoint representation gives the integral of $s$ by [F7], so $\int s\,d\rho\le M\rho(K)$. Taking suprema bounds each sign integral by $M\rho(K)<\infty$. Hence $f$ is integrable. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7]

2.1 The family of subsets $E\subseteq Y$ for which $I^{-1}(E)$ is Borel contains $Y$ because $I^{-1}(Y)=X$ is open in $X$. Preimages commute with relative complements and countable unions, and the Borel sets of $X$ are closed under both operations. By [F6], this family is a sigma-algebra on $Y$. This sigma-algebra contains all open sets by continuity of $I$, so it contains every Borel set by [F6]. Applying the same argument to $J$ gives a bijection between the Borel sets of $X$ and $Y$. For a nonzero right-invariant Radon measure $\mu$ on $X$, set $\eta(E)=\mu(I^{-1}(E))$ for Borel $E\subseteq Y$. Then $\eta(\varnothing)=0$. For pairwise disjoint Borel sets $E_n\subseteq Y$, the preimages $I^{-1}(E_n)$ are pairwise disjoint Borel sets, so $\eta(\bigcup_n E_n)=\mu(\bigcup_n I^{-1}(E_n))=\sum_n\mu(I^{-1}(E_n))=\sum_n\eta(E_n)$ by [F6]. Thus $\eta$ is a Borel measure. Both directions of the homeomorphism send compact sets to compact sets. Compact subsets of $X$ and $Y$ are closed by Hausdorffness and [F4], so they are Borel by [F6]. Consequently $\eta$ is finite on compact sets. Open supersets of a Borel set $E$ correspond to open supersets of $I^{-1}(E)$, and compact subsets of an open set correspond under $I^{-1}$ to compact subsets of its preimage. The same infima and suprema therefore give outer regularity on Borel sets and inner regularity on open sets. The Borel correspondence and nonzero $\mu$ give nonzero $\eta$. Transport along $J$ recovers $\mu$ because $J$ and $I$ are inverses. [F1, F3, F4, F6, step 1.1]

3.1 Fix $a\in H$. For the slice $x\mapsto(x,a)$, the preimage of a basic open rectangle $U\times V$ is $U$ if $a\in V$ and empty otherwise. Every open set in $X\times H$ is a union of those rectangles, so the slice is continuous by [F5]. The preimage of an open subset of $X$ under $x\mapsto xa$ is its preimage under the continuous action in [F1] pulled back along the continuous slice. It is therefore open, giving continuity of right translation by [F5]. Its inverse is $x\mapsto xa^{-1}$, which is continuous by the same reasoning, so right translation is a homeomorphism. The identity $I(xa)=a^{-1}I(x)$ gives $I^{-1}(aE)=I^{-1}(E)a^{-1}$. For Borel $E$, this preimage and hence $aE$ are Borel by the homeomorphism argument in step 2.1. It follows that $\eta(aE)=\eta(E)$ by right invariance of $\mu$. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Use the measure in [F1] in step 2.1 and apply the criterion in [F3] to the resulting left-invariant measure on $Y$. By [F2], $\Delta_H|_\Gamma=\Delta_\Gamma=1$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 For $\gamma\in\Gamma$, multiplicativity gives $\Delta_H(\gamma h)=\Delta_H(h)$. Hence $\bar\Delta(\Gamma h)=\Delta_H(h)$ is well defined. For each open $V\subseteq\mathbb R$, the preimage of $\bar\Delta^{-1}(V)$ under $q_X$ is the open set $\Delta_H^{-1}(V)$ in $H$. By [F5], $\bar\Delta$ is continuous. [F2, F5, step 4.1]

6.1 The compact space $X$ contains $\Gamma 1$, so the real-valued function $\bar\Delta$ attains its maximum $M=\bar\Delta(\Gamma u)=\Delta_H(u)>0$ for some $u\in H$. For every $h\in H$, $M\Delta_H(h)=\Delta_H(uh)\le M$. Thus $\Delta_H(h)\le1$. Applying the inequality to $h^{-1}$ gives $\Delta_H(h)^{-1}\le1$, so $\Delta_H(h)=1$. The scaling relation in [F2] now gives right invariance of left Haar measure, which is unimodularity. [F1, F2, F4, step 5.1]

7.1 For two nonzero right-invariant Radon measures $\mu_1,\mu_2$ on $X$, transport them by steps 2.1 and 3.1 to left-invariant Radon measures $\eta_1,\eta_2$ on $Y$. Write $\lambda_j$ for the left Haar measure on $H$ representing $f\mapsto\int_Y T_\Gamma f\,d\eta_j$ by [F3]. By [F4], $\lambda_2=c\lambda_1$ on Borel sets for some finite $c>0$. Steps 1.3 and 1.2 give $\int_H f\,d\lambda_2=c\int_H f\,d\lambda_1$ on $C_c(H)$. Surjectivity of $T_\Gamma$ gives $\int_Y \phi\,d\eta_2=c\int_Y \phi\,d\eta_1=\int_Y \phi\,d(c\eta_1)$ on $C_c(Y)$. The two measures $\eta_2$ and $c\eta_1$ are Radon by steps 2.1 and 1.2, and $Y$ is locally compact Hausdorff by [F3]. Radon uniqueness under the Dependent Choice furnished by [A1] gives $\eta_2=c\eta_1$. Transport back gives $\mu_2=c\mu_1$. Existence and finiteness follow from [F1]. [A1, F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.3, step 1.2] ∎
