## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice for compactness of the integral subgroup. A finite prime $\ell$ is split for the rational Hamilton algebra $B$ when its local algebra $B_\ell$ admits a $\mathbb Q_\ell$-algebra isomorphism $\phi:B_\ell\to M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$. For a field $F$, the two-by-two matrix algebra $M_2(F)$ consists of matrices with two rows, two columns, and entries in $F$. It has entrywise addition and row-by-column multiplication, with identity $I_2$ and scalar map $a\mapsto aI_2$. The isomorphism is a unital ring isomorphism that respects the scalar maps $a\mapsto a1$ in $B_\ell$ and $a\mapsto aI_2$ in the matrix algebra.

Choose such an isomorphism. It identifies $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)=B_\ell^\times/\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ with $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)=\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)/N_\ell$, the quotient by the central subgroup $N_\ell=\{aI_2:a\in\mathbb Q_\ell^\times\}$. Here $\mathrm{GL}_2(R)$ denotes the matrices with a two-sided inverse over the commutative ring $R$, and the projective quotient identifies matrices differing by a nonzero scalar. Let $J_\ell\subseteq G(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ correspond to the image of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$, where $\mathbb Z_\ell=\{a\in\mathbb Q_\ell:|a|_\ell\le1\}$.

Give $M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ the product topology of its four entries. Give $\mathbb Z_\ell$ the subspace topology from $\mathbb Q_\ell$ and give $M_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ and both general linear groups the subspace topologies from $M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$. Give $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ the quotient topology of the scalar projection.

A strongly continuous unitary representation $\pi_\ell$ of the topological group $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ on a complex Hilbert space $H_\ell$ is unramified if there is a nonzero vector $v$ with $\pi_\ell(j)v=v$ for every $j\in J_\ell$. The same test applies to a strongly continuous unitary representation $\pi$ of the full adelic topological group $G(\mathbb A)$ on a complex Hilbert space $H$, by restricting to the copy of $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ supported at $\ell$, with all other components equal to the identity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rational Hamilton algebra $B$, a split prime $\ell$, a chosen matrix isomorphism, and a strongly continuous unitary representation of either $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ or $G(\mathbb A)$ on a complex Hilbert space.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice asserts the existence of a choice function for each indexed family of nonempty sets. Under this assumption the space of compatible residue tuples modulo $\ell^n$ is compact for its residue metric ([[math:def-axiom-of-choice]], [[math:def-p-adic-integers-as-compatible-residue-classes]], [[math:def-p-adic-metric-on-zp]], [[math:thm-zp-is-compact-hausdorff-totally-disconnected-and-complete]]).

[F1] The local projective units form a topological group in the scalar quotient topology. The map from quaternion units to their projective classes is open. The finite adelic projective group has the restricted-product topology, with open local factors at all primes and the prescribed integral subgroups outside finitely many primes ([[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]], Justification, steps 4.1 and 5.1). Invertible quaternions over $\mathbb Q_\ell$ have the topology of their four coefficients, and the absolute value defining $\mathbb Z_\ell$ is multiplicative and ultrametric ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]]). The quotient group law multiplies representatives. If the subgroup is normal, this law is well defined and gives a group ([[math:def-quotient-group]], [[math:thm-quotient-group-laws]]). A subgroup $N$ of a group $G$ is normal if $XNX^{-1}=N$ for every $X\in G$ ([[math:def-normal-subgroup]]).

[F2] Over a commutative ring a square matrix of positive size is invertible exactly when its determinant is a unit. Finite products of compact spaces and continuous images of compact spaces are compact. A closed subspace of a compact space is compact ([[math:cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]], [[math:thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces]], [[math:thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]], [[math:thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]], claim 1). The two terms of the determinant formula in size two give $\det X=x_{11}x_{22}-x_{12}x_{21}$ for $X=(x_{ij})$. [[math:def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]

[F3] For a topological group $S$ and a complex Hilbert space $H$, a strongly continuous unitary representation is a homomorphism into the group of bijective complex-linear isometries of $H$ for which each orbit map $s\mapsto\pi(s)v$ is norm-continuous ([[math:def-topological-group]], [[math:def-hilbert-space]], [[math:def-strongly-continuous-unitary-representation]]).

[F4] The full adelic group $G(\mathbb A)=G(\mathbb R)\times G(\mathbb A_f)$ has componentwise multiplication and the product topology, making it a topological group ([[def-author-5b8600b4f4af-full-adelic-hamilton-group]], Justification, step 3.1).

[F5] The canonical identification of the compatible residue tuples with the unit ball of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ takes agreement modulo $\ell^n$ to the distance condition $|a-b|_\ell\le\ell^{-n}$ on their images $a,b$. Agreement at level $n$ gives an inverse-limit neighborhood basis and corresponds under this identification to a subspace neighborhood basis on the unit ball ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]], Justification, step 1.3). The inverse-limit topology is the subspace topology from the product of the finite discrete residue groups. [[math:def-inverse-limit-topology-for-finite-discrete-groups]] The inverse-limit and residue-metric topologies on the compatible residue tuples coincide ([[math:thm-inverse-limit-topology-equals-the-p-adic-metric-topology]]).

[F6] An algebra over a commutative field has a unital central scalar map, and an algebra isomorphism preserves it and is linear over the field ([[math:def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]). Every nonzero element of a field has a multiplicative inverse. [[math:def-field]] Multiplication in a commutative ring is commutative. [[math:def-commutative-ring]] A unital ring isomorphism is a bijection preserving addition, multiplication and the multiplicative identity. [[math:def-ring-homomorphism]] A matrix over a commutative ring is a rectangular array of entries in the ring, and each column of a matrix with two rows can be written as a two-by-one matrix ([[math:def-matrices-over-a-commutative-ring]]). Matrix addition and scalar multiplication are entrywise. Matrix multiplication is row-by-column with identity $I_2$, and it is associative and distributive ([[math:def-ring-matrix-product-identity-and-transpose]], [[math:thm-ring-matrix-arithmetic-laws]]). For a commutative ring $R$, $\mathrm{GL}_2(R)$ is the group of matrices with a two-sided inverse over $R$ ([[math:def-invertible-matrix-and-similarity-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F7] In any topology, the empty set, finite intersections of open sets, and arbitrary unions of open sets are open. [[math:def-topological-space]] Finite product topologies have open boxes as a basis ([[math:def-product-topology]]). A subspace has the traces of ambient open sets as its open sets ([[math:def-subspace-topology-top]]). A set in a quotient topology is open exactly when its preimage under the projection is open ([[math:def-quotient-topology]]).

[F8] Addition and multiplication in $\mathbb Q_\ell$ are continuous for its metric topology ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]], Justification, step 5.1).

[F9] A map is continuous exactly when preimages of open sets are open, or equivalently when preimages of closed sets are closed ([[math:thm-continuity-characterisations-top]], conditions (a)–(c)). A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse, and an embedding is an injection that is a homeomorphism onto its image with the subspace topology ([[math:def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps]]).

[F10] A compact subset is compact in its subspace topology ([[math:def-compact-space]]). A subgroup contains the identity and is closed under multiplication and inversion ([[math:def-subgroup]]).

[F11] A group homomorphism preserves products, the identity and inverses. Composites of group homomorphisms are group homomorphisms. [[math:def-group-homomorphism]] A group isomorphism is a bijective group homomorphism. [[math:def-group-isomorphism-and-automorphism]]

[F12] For each prime $\ell$, the local integer ring $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and its unit group $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times=\{a\in\mathbb Q_\ell:|a|_\ell=1\}$ are open and closed in $\mathbb Q_\ell$. ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]], Justification, step 2.1).

## Justification

**Goal:** Show that $J_\ell$ is compact open and that the test does not depend on the chosen splitting.

1.1 The isomorphism $\phi$ and its inverse are linear by [F6], so each output entry or Hamilton coefficient is a finite sum of scalar multiples of input coordinates. These sums are continuous by [F8], and the preimage of an open box is an intersection of four open coordinate preimages and is open by [F7]. Every open set is a union of basic boxes, so its preimage is a union of these open preimages and is open by [F7]. Thus $\phi$ and its inverse are continuous for the coefficient topologies and restrict to continuous maps on the subspaces of units. The set $N_\ell$ of nonzero scalar matrices contains $I_2$ and is closed under multiplication and inversion since $(aI_2)(bI_2)=(ab)I_2$ and $(aI_2)^{-1}=a^{-1}I_2$. It is a subgroup by [F10]. The scalar map in [F6] is central, so $X(aI_2)X^{-1}=aI_2$ for each invertible $X$. Thus $XN_\ell X^{-1}=N_\ell$, and the subgroup is normal by [F1]. By [F1] the cosets form a group under multiplication of representatives. In particular $q_M(XY)=q_M(X)q_M(Y)$ for the matrix scalar projection $q_M$. The isomorphism preserves units and takes $a1$ to $aI_2$, so it gives a bijection of scalar cosets, with inverse induced by $\phi^{-1}$. Because both coset laws multiply representatives, this bijection is a group isomorphism $\bar\phi$ by [F11]. For the quaternion scalar projection $q_B$, we have $q_M\circ\phi=\bar\phi\circ q_B$ on units. The left side is continuous, so the quotient open-set criterion for $q_B$ gives continuity of $\bar\phi$. Applying the same argument to $\phi^{-1}$ gives continuity of $\bar\phi^{-1}$. Thus $\bar\phi$ is a homeomorphism by [F9]. [F1, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11]

2.1 Two splittings differ by a $\mathbb Q_\ell$-algebra automorphism of $M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$. For such an automorphism let $A_{ij}$ be the image of the matrix unit $E_{ij}$, which has a single $1$ in row $i$, column $j$ and zeros elsewhere. Row-by-column multiplication gives $E_{ij}E_{rs}=\delta_{jr}E_{is}$, where $\delta_{jr}$ is $1$ when $j=r$ and $0$ otherwise. Multiplicativity and linearity give $A_{ij}A_{rs}=\delta_{jr}A_{is}$. Write column vectors as two-by-one matrices. Since $A_{11}\ne0$, choose a column containing a nonzero entry. For the vector $v$ with $1$ at that column's index and $0$ at the other index, $v_1=A_{11}v$ is that nonzero column. Set $v_2=A_{21}v_1$. Then $A_{11}v_1=v_1$, $A_{11}v_2=0$, and $A_{12}v_2=v_1$. If $\lambda v_1+\mu v_2=0$ for scalars $\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb Q_\ell$, applying $A_{11}$ gives $\lambda v_1=0$. Since $v_1\ne0$ this gives $\lambda=0$, and $A_{12}v_2=v_1\ne0$ gives $v_2\ne0$, so $\mu=0$. Let $T=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{pmatrix}$ have columns $v_1,v_2$. If $\det T=ad-bc=0$ and $a\ne0$, then $d=bc/a$ and $v_2=(b/a)v_1$. If $\det T=0$ and $a=0$, then $c\ne0$ because $v_1\ne0$, so $b=0$ and $v_2=(d/c)v_1$. Either proportionality gives a zero relation with coefficient $1$ on $v_2$, which is impossible by the preceding calculation. The nonzero determinant is a unit in $\mathbb Q_\ell$, so $T$ is invertible by [F2]. The matrix-unit relations give $A_{ij}v_r=\delta_{jr}v_i$, so the $r$th columns of $A_{ij}T$ and $TE_{ij}$ agree for $r=1,2$. Thus $A_{ij}T=TE_{ij}$ and $A_{ij}=TE_{ij}T^{-1}$. By entrywise operations, every matrix $X=(x_{ij})$ equals $\sum_{i,j=1}^2 x_{ij}E_{ij}$, since entry $(i,j)$ of the sum is $x_{ij}$. The automorphism and conjugation by $T$ are linear, so their agreement on the four matrix units gives agreement on every $X$. Consequently the subgroups $J_\ell$ obtained from any two splittings are conjugate in the quaternion projective group. [F1, F2, F6, step 1.1]

2.2 The common inverse-limit neighborhood bases in [F5] make the canonical identification from the residue-metric space to the local unit ball continuous in both directions. It is a homeomorphism by [F9]. The domain is compact by [A1], so the continuous image rule in [F2] gives compactness of $\mathbb Z_\ell$ in its subspace topology. By [F12], both the integral ball $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and the unit set $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$ are open and closed in $\mathbb Q_\ell$. The size-two determinant from [F2] is continuous by [F8] as a sum of products of entry projections and constants. By the determinant unit test, $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ is the set of matrices in $M_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ whose determinants have absolute value $1$. The four open entry conditions give an open box in $M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$. The determinant condition is open by [F9], so its intersection with this box is open by [F7]. Its trace in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ is $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$. The determinant condition is also closed by [F9], and its trace in $M_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ is $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$. The subspace topology on $M_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ is the product of the four unit-ball topologies because tracing an open box to the four integral entries traces each factor separately. The integral matrix space is compact by finite-product compactness, so the closed subspace $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ is compact. The inclusion of coefficient rings makes $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ because the identity, products and inverses over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ are the same over $\mathbb Q_\ell$. The pullback under $\phi$ and its image under $q_B$ contain the identity and are closed under products and inverses by the group homomorphism laws in [F11]. Thus both are subgroups by [F10]. The pullback is open by continuity of $\phi$ and compact by continuity of $\phi^{-1}$ on the unit subspaces, using step 1.1 and [F2]. The projective image $J_\ell$ is compact by continuity of $q_B$ and open because $q_B$ is open. [A1, F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, F12, step 1.1]

3.1 For a subgroup $J$ and a vector fixed by $J$, the vector $\pi_\ell(t)v$ is fixed by $tJt^{-1}$ because $\pi_\ell(tjt^{-1})\pi_\ell(t)v=\pi_\ell(t)\pi_\ell(j)v=\pi_\ell(t)v$. It is nonzero when $v$ is nonzero, and applying the same equality with $t^{-1}$ proves the converse. To embed $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ in the full adelic group, insert the local element at $\ell$ and use the identity at every other place, including the real place. This defines an injective homomorphism because the integral subgroups contain the identity and multiplication is componentwise. In the full product topology a basic open set is a real open factor times a finite adelic box. Its preimage is empty if some fixed coordinate is excluded, and is otherwise its open local factor at $\ell$. The inverse on the image is the local coordinate projection. For an open local set, its preimage under this inverse is the trace on the image of the cylinder imposing that open condition only at $\ell$. For each point of the cylinder, intersect the factor at $\ell$ of a basic neighborhood with the prescribed open set. This factor is open by [F7]. The resulting basic box is still allowed in the restricted product because only one factor was changed. It lies inside the cylinder, which is therefore a union of open boxes and is open by [F7]. Thus the injection is an embedding by [F7] and [F9]. For a full adelic representation $\pi$ on $H$, let $\iota_\ell$ be this embedding and set $\pi_\ell=\pi\circ\iota_\ell$. By [F11], this is a homomorphism into the same group of bijective complex-linear isometries of $H$. For each $v\in H$ write $o_v(h)=\pi(h)v$. For a norm-open set $W\subseteq H$, its preimage under the restricted orbit map $o_v\circ\iota_\ell$ is $\iota_\ell^{-1}(o_v^{-1}(W))$. The inner preimage is open by strong continuity of $\pi$, and the outer preimage is open by continuity of $\iota_\ell$, using [F9]. Thus $\pi_\ell$ is strongly continuous and unitary on the same complex Hilbert space. The conjugacy in step 2.1 and the fixed-vector argument therefore apply to this restriction as well. [F1, F3, F4, F7, F9, F11, step 2.1] ∎
