## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G=PB^\times$ be the group of units modulo nonzero scalars of the definite rational Hamilton division algebra $B$. Put $H=G(\mathbb A)=G(\mathbb R)\times G(\mathbb A_f)$ and $\Gamma=G(\mathbb Q)$ embedded diagonally. The quotient $X=\Gamma\backslash H$ is compact and has a nonzero finite right-invariant Radon measure $\mu$, a regular Borel measure finite on compact sets. On $\mathcal H=L^2(X,\mu;\mathbb C)$, the Hilbert space of complex square-integrable functions modulo equality almost everywhere, right translation acts by $R(h)f(x)=f(xh)$.

The representation $R$ is a Hilbert direct sum of irreducible unitary automorphic representations. Its summands are pairwise orthogonal closed invariant subspaces $M_i$ with closed linear span $\mathcal H$, and irreducibility means that each $M_i$ has no proper nonzero closed invariant subspace. Each equivalence class occurs with finite multiplicity. For a compact open subgroup $U\le G(\mathbb A_f)$ and $K=G(\mathbb R)\times U$, the fixed space $\mathcal H^K=\{v:R(k)v=v\text{ for all }k\in K\}$ is the orthogonal sum of the spaces $M_i^K$, with only finitely many nonzero terms. A summand contributing to this space has trivial real action.

For a prime $\ell$, let $\mathbb Q_\ell$ be the $\ell$-adic completion of $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb Z_\ell=\{a\in\mathbb Q_\ell:|a|_\ell\le1\}$ its ring of integers. Write $B_\ell=B\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q_\ell$ for $B$ with coefficients extended to $\mathbb Q_\ell$. Use the scalar quotient $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)=B_\ell^\times/\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ for the finite local group. Call $\ell$ split if $B_\ell$ is isomorphic as a $\mathbb Q_\ell$-algebra to $M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$, the algebra of two-by-two matrices over $\mathbb Q_\ell$. At a split prime choose such an isomorphism and let $J_\ell$ be the projective image of the invertible matrices over $\mathbb Z_\ell$. The copy of $J_\ell$ supported at $\ell$ in the finite adelic restricted product consists of the tuples with $\ell$-coordinate in $J_\ell$ and identity at every other prime. A local component is the factor on which the local group acts in the restricted Hilbert tensor product of a constituent. This tensor product is the completion of the span of elementary tensors equal to specified unit vectors at all but finitely many places, with the product inner product. A local component is unramified when its $J_\ell$-fixed space is nonzero. In that case the fixed space is a line, and the spherical Hecke algebra acts on it by a character. This algebra consists of compactly supported complex functions invariant on both sides by $J_\ell$. Its convolution uses Haar measure with $J_\ell$ of mass one, and a character is a unital complex-algebra map to $\mathbb C$. When $U$ contains the copy of $J_\ell$ supported at $\ell$, the same local Hecke operators act on every nonzero $M_i^K$ by that constituent’s local character.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] The Axiom of Choice provides choice functions for indexed families of nonempty sets. It implies Countable Choice, the corresponding principle for sequences. Use these hypotheses in the quotient and Haar constructions, Hilbert direct sums and tensor products, and the spherical-line result ([[math:def-axiom-of-choice]], [[math:thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The full projective group $H$ has the product topology and componentwise multiplication and is locally compact Hausdorff, $G(\mathbb R)$ is compact, and $\Gamma$ is closed and discrete. The quotient is compact. The group $H$ is unimodular, meaning left Haar measure is also right invariant, and the invariant Radon measure on $X$ is finite, nonzero and unique up to positive scale ([[def-author-5b8600b4f4af-full-adelic-hamilton-group]], [[lem-author-5b8600b4f4af-hamilton-invariant-quotient]], [[lem-author-c3a9b9243c74-compact-projective-hamilton-quotient]], [[lem-author-c3a9b9243c74-projective-hamilton-haar-normalization]]).

[F2] The quotient representation is strongly continuous and unitary ([[def-definite-quaternion-automorphic-fixed-space]]). A unitary representation is a homomorphism to the group of bijective linear isometries. Strong continuity means that the orbit map of every vector is continuous in the Hilbert norm ([[math:def-strongly-continuous-unitary-representation]]). With $K=G(\mathbb R)\times U$, its fixed space identifies isometrically with $L^2(D)$ for $D=G(\mathbb Q)\backslash G(\mathbb A_f)/U$ and the measure assigning each double coset the measure of its preimage. The map $X\to D$ is a continuous surjection to a discrete space, with fibers the right $K$-orbits. A double coset of $g_f$ consists of the elements $\gamma_f g_f u$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma$ and $u\in U$ ([[def-definite-quaternion-automorphic-fixed-space]]). In a Hilbert direct sum the group acts in each coordinate separately, and vectors are families with finite sum of squared norms, defined as the supremum of the finite subsums ([[math:def-hilbert-direct-sum-of-unitary-representations]]).

[F3] On the full quaternion ideles $\widetilde H=B_{\mathbb R}^\times\times\widehat B^\times$, the quotient by the center of scalar ideles $Z$ identifies with $H$ as a topological group. Lifting along this quotient identifies $R$ with the trivial central-character right translation action on $B^\times Z\backslash\widetilde H$, for the transported measure. The lift preserves closed invariant subspaces ([[lem-author-c3a9b9243c74-quaternion-trivial-central-character-model]]).

[F4] A complex Hilbert space is complete in the norm of its inner product ([[math:def-hilbert-space]]). The completion of the algebraic tensor product of two complex Hilbert spaces uses the product pairing on elementary tensors, and their finite sums are dense. If a subgroup acts on only one Hilbert tensor factor, the fixed space is the completed tensor product of that factor’s fixed space with the other factor. The same scalar action of any finite operator sum on the fixed space passes to this tensor product ([[math:lem-nrqm-composites-hilbert-tensor-construction]], [[lem-author-c3a9b9243c74-fixed-space-of-tensor-factor]]).

[F5] A split identification gives a continuous surjection $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)\to G(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ modulo scalars and a compact open subgroup $J_\ell$ given by the image of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$. If the lifted local representation has irreducible admissible smooth vectors, its nonzero $J_\ell$-fixed space is a line with a Hecke character. The spherical action is the finite sum $T_fv=\sum_{xJ_\ell} f(x)\rho(x)v$, with one term per contributing coset $xJ_\ell=\{xj:j\in J_\ell\}$ ([[def-author-5b8600b4f4af-split-unramified-quaternion-representation]], [[lem-author-c3a9b9243c74-projective-local-spherical-line]], [[def-author-d45da46285be-spherical-hecke-algebra]]).

[F6] Use the compact-quotient, factorization, smooth-vector and reductivity results and the source measure convention stated below.

[F7] Write $O$ for the full order in the central-character model. Its completion $O_\ell$ is the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span of a full order basis in $B_\ell$, and $\mathbb Z_\ell$ is the unit ball for the nonarchimedean absolute value of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]]). The finite idele group uses basic open boxes equal to $O_\ell^\times$ outside a finite set ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]]). The Hurwitz order $O_{\mathrm H}$ is a full order with a $\mathbb Z$-basis that is also a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $B$ ([[thm-hurwitz-order-integral-structure]]). At each odd prime an integral splitting carries the completed Hurwitz order onto $M_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ ([[thm-hurwitz-integral-splitting-at-odd-primes]]).

[F8] The $F$-points of the general linear algebraic group of degree two over a field $F$ are the invertible two-by-two matrices over $F$ ([[math:lem-general-linear-group-scheme-and-its-coordinate-ring]]). The local-points topology on $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$ is the topology from the embedding $A\mapsto(A,\det(A)^{-1})$ into $M_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)\times\mathbb Q_\ell$. For a smooth connected affine algebraic group, reductivity means that the unipotent radical after base change to an algebraic closure is trivial ([[math:def-radical-and-unipotent-radical-of-an-algebraic-group]]). With Countable Choice the complete nonarchimedean field $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is locally compact and its ring of integers is compact open ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]], [[math:cor-p-adic-field-is-locally-compact-and-totally-disconnected]]).

[F9] The rational Hamilton basis $1,i,j,k$ satisfies $i^2=j^2=k^2=-1$, $ij=k=-ji$, $jk=i=-kj$, and $ki=j=-ik$. Rational scalars are central ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]]). Every nonzero $x\in B$ has the two-sided inverse $\bar x/N(x)$, where $\bar x$ negates the three imaginary coefficients and $N(x)$ is the sum of all four coefficient squares ([[lem-rational-hamilton-norm-and-inverse]]).

[F10] An element of $\mathbb Z_\ell$ is a unit exactly when its reduction modulo $\ell$ is nonzero ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]], Justification, step 1.2). The kernel of scalar reduction modulo $\ell^n$ is $\ell^n\mathbb Z_\ell$ for $n\ge1$. Its translates by $b$ are the balls $\{a:|a-b|_\ell\le\ell^{-n}\}$ and give a neighborhood basis at $b$ in $\mathbb Q_\ell$ ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]], Justification, step 1.3).

[F11] The finite adelic scalar quotient is the restricted product of the groups $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)=B_\ell^\times/\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$, each with its local quotient topology, with respect to the projective images of $O_\ell^\times$. The identification sends a finite idele class to its tuple of local classes ([[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]], Justification, steps 4.1 and 5.1).

[F12] A finite product of compact spaces is compact in the product topology ([[math:thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces]]).

[F13] A number field is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$ ([[math:def-number-field]]). A global field of characteristic zero is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$. A place of $\mathbb Q$ is an equivalence class of nontrivial absolute values ([[math:def-place-of-the-rationals]]). Ostrowski’s theorem identifies these places with the classes of the usual absolute value and of the $\ell$-adic absolute values, one for each prime $\ell$ ([[math:thm-ostrowskis-theorem-for-the-rationals]]). The corresponding completions are $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb Q_\ell$, respectively ([[math:def-rational-local-fields]]).

[F14] With Countable Choice, a bounded linear map from a normed space to a Banach space extends uniquely to a bounded linear map on the completion of its domain ([[math:thm-completion-universal-property-for-bounded-linear-maps]]).

[F15] Addition and multiplication on $\mathbb Q_\ell$ are continuous in the $\ell$-adic metric, as is inversion on $\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$. The estimates in the local order construction also give continuity of polynomial coordinate functions and finite tuples of them ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]], Justification, steps 5.1 and 6.1).

[F16] The modular function records the scaling of left Haar integrals by right translations ([[math:def-modular-function-of-a-locally-compact-group]]). When the modular functions of a locally compact Hausdorff group and its closed subgroup agree on the subgroup, the Weil measure for the constant weight one is a nonzero left-invariant Radon measure. It is the unique Radon measure giving the quotient integration identity for the chosen left Haar measures, with the inner integral averaging over right multiplication by the subgroup ([[math:prop-invariant-measure-on-g-mod-h-iff-modular-functions-agree]], [[math:thm-weil-quotient-integration-formula-with-rho-function]]). Counting measure is left and right Haar on a discrete group, and its integrals are sums ([[math:lem-counting-measure-on-a-discrete-group]]). In a topological group inversion is a homeomorphism, and in a unimodular group it preserves Haar measure ([[math:lem-topological-group-translations-and-inversion]], [[math:lem-haar-change-of-variables-under-inversion]]).

[F17] For a normal subgroup $N$, its quotient group has multiplication $(aN)(bN)=abN$. A subset of a quotient space is open exactly when its preimage under the quotient map is open ([[math:def-quotient-group]], [[math:def-quotient-topology]]).

## External input

Arthur’s compact-quotient result gives the orthogonal Hilbert decomposition, with finite multiplicities, for right translation on a compact quotient by a discrete subgroup of a locally compact unimodular group.

Badulescu and Renard’s factorization statement applies to each irreducible closed invariant summand in the trivial central-character square-integrable action of the quaternion ideles. It supplies irreducible unitary representations of the individual local unit groups and an equivalence with their restricted Hilbert tensor product. Their adelic group is the restricted product defined by the integral matrix unit subgroups in the chosen local matrix-algebra identifications. At all but finitely many finite places the local factors have unit vectors fixed by those subgroups. For their square integral they first take the quotient of adelic Haar measure by the Haar measure on the adelic center. The resulting measure on the projective group defines the measure on the quotient by the discrete rational image.

Getz and Hahn’s smooth-vector proposition and the Harish-Chandra–Bernstein admissibility theorem give irreducibility and admissibility of the smooth vectors of an irreducible unitary representation of the group of local points of a reductive group over a nonarchimedean local field. Smooth vectors in their Hilbert-space convention are the union of the spaces fixed by compact open subgroups. Admissibility means finite dimensionality of each compact-open fixed space.

Milne’s example gives reductivity of the general linear algebraic group over a field.

Getz and Hahn define global fields as finite extensions of $\mathbb Q$ or of $\mathbb F_p(t)$ for some prime $p$.

Getz and Hahn give $\mathrm{GL}_n(F_v)$ the subspace topology from the embedding $A\mapsto(A,\det(A)^{-1})$ into $M_n(F_v)\times F_v$, where $F_v$ is the completion at the place $v$.

## Proof

**Given:** $H,\Gamma,X,\mu$ as in the statement, $U$ compact open in $G(\mathbb A_f)$, and [A1].

**Goal:** Obtain the discrete decomposition, identify its finite-level fixed spaces, and compute the form of the split unramified action.

1.1 The compactness, nonzero finite invariant measure, locally compact Hausdorff group and discrete subgroup are supplied by [F1]. Its unimodularity meets Arthur’s remaining group hypothesis. Apply the first input in [F6] to get $\mathcal H=\widehat\bigoplus_i M_i$ with each $M_i$ irreducible and with finite multiplicities. Any choice of $\mu$ gives the same unitary equivalence class because scaling the invariant measure by a positive constant rescales the square norm by that constant and a constant multiplier implements the isometry. 1.2 With the Hamilton rules of [F9], $x=a+bi+cj+dk$ satisfies $xi-ix=2dj-2ck$ and $xj-jx=2bk-2di$. If $x$ commutes with both $i$ and $j$, then the independence of the basis over $\mathbb Q$ implies $b=c=d=0$. Since rational scalars commute with all of $B$, its center is $\mathbb Q1$. The inverse formula in [F9] makes $B$ a division algebra. $\mathbb Q$ has characteristic zero and is a number field of degree one since $\{1\}$ is a basis of $\mathbb Q$ over $\mathbb Q$. Thus $\mathbb Q$ is a global field of characteristic zero by [F13]. By [F13] the base-field completions in the source are $\mathbb R$ at the single archimedean place and $\mathbb Q_\ell$ at the place of each prime $\ell$. Thus its local unit groups are $B_{\mathbb R}^\times$ at infinity and $B_\ell^\times$ at the finite places, obtained by extending the Hamilton coefficients. Apply the adelic convention in [F6] with base field $\mathbb Q$, central division algebra $B$, and matrix size one over $B$. By [A1] and [F7] choose their odd-prime identifications to carry the completed Hurwitz orders onto the integral matrix rings. The identifications are homeomorphisms on local units because the linear maps and their inverses use finite linear coordinate functions, continuous by [F15] in the two subspace topologies. The source’s odd-prime defining subgroups then pull back to $(O_{\mathrm H})_\ell^\times$. Take a positive integer $m$ clearing the denominators of the rational basis changes between $O$ and $O_{\mathrm H}$ in both directions. For $\ell\nmid m$ the two change-of-basis matrices are integral over $\mathbb Z_\ell$, so each completed order lies in the other and their unit groups agree. Outside the primes dividing $2m$, the source’s defining subgroups thus agree with those in [F7]. The restricted products have the same tuples, and each basic open box in either system is basic in the other after allowing the primes dividing $2m$ as exceptional coordinates. Including the real factor gives a topological group identification. It carries the diagonal rational units to the diagonal rational units by the same coefficient extensions in the local identifications, and carries the center onto the center. [A1, F3, F6, F7, F9, F13, F15]

2.1 Under step 1.2 and [F3], the two successive quotients in [F6] are by $Z$ and by $\Gamma$. The source provides Haar measures on the locally compact Hausdorff adelic group $\widetilde H$ and on $Z$. The latter is closed as the kernel of the continuous projection to the Hausdorff group $H$. For $z\in Z$, right translation by $z$ on $\widetilde H$ equals left translation by $z$, so the modular function of $\widetilde H$ is one there. The abelian group $Z$ is unimodular for the same reason. The first Haar quotient is a left Haar measure $dh$ on $H$ by [F16]: it is a nonzero left-invariant Radon measure, and the left and right coset spaces for $Z$ agree because $Z$ is central. Now $\Gamma$ is closed and discrete by [F1]. Its counting Haar measure and $dh$ have modular functions equal to one. Apply [F16] to $H/\Gamma$ to obtain the nonzero left-invariant Radon measure with the Weil identity for $dh$ and counting measure. Push it to $X$ by $h\Gamma\mapsto\Gamma h^{-1}$, which is a homeomorphism by inversion and the quotient topologies in [F17]. Denote the pushforward by $\nu$. The homeomorphism preserves the Borel, open and compact sets used in Radon regularity, so $\nu$ is nonzero Radon. It is right invariant because the homeomorphism changes left translation by $a$ to right translation by $a^{-1}$. Applying the Weil identity to $h\mapsto f(h^{-1})$ and reindexing the counting sum by $\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}$ gives $\int_H f(h)\,dh=\int_X\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma} f(\gamma h)\,d\nu(\Gamma h)$ for every compactly supported continuous $f$. The left side uses inversion invariance of $dh$ from [F16]. The reverse substitution gives uniqueness for this right-coset Haar quotient. Thus the second quotient measure of [F6] is $\nu$ with counting normalization. Using a different Haar multiple of counting measure only rescales $\nu$ by the inverse multiple. In either normalization the source measure is nonzero, right invariant and Radon, and it is finite by compactness of $X$. [A1, F1, F3, F6, F16, F17, step 1.2]

2.2 Using the coordinate action in [F2], the equality $R(k)v=v$ for every $k\in K$ holds exactly when each component of $v$ belongs to $M_i^K$. The discrete space $D$ in [F2] is compact by continuity and surjectivity from the compact $X$: an open cover of $D$ pulls back to an open cover of $X$, whose finite subcover maps onto $D$. Its singleton cover shows $D$ is finite. The fixed space is therefore finite dimensional by its identification with $L^2(D)$. Any finite family of nonzero distinct fixed summands supplies the same number of linearly independent vectors by orthogonality, so only finitely many summands are nonzero. For $h=(a,b)\in H$ and $g\in G(\mathbb R)$, the product law gives $h^{-1}(g,1)h=(a^{-1}ga,1)$. If $v\in M_i^{G(\mathbb R)}$, the representation law gives $R((g,1))R(h)v=R(h)R((a^{-1}ga,1))v=R(h)v$. Thus $M_i^{G(\mathbb R)}$ is invariant under the full group. This space is closed as an intersection of kernels of continuous operators $R(g)-I$. If $M_i^K\ne0$ then $M_i^{G(\mathbb R)}\ne0$, and irreducibility forces it to equal $M_i$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F3] and step 1.2, the lift of each $M_i$ is irreducible for the trivial central-character action with the transported measure. On the identified projective quotient, the transported measure of [F3] and the source Haar quotient measure of step 2.1 are nonzero right-invariant Radon measures. They differ by a positive scalar by [F1]. Constant rescaling as in step 1.1 therefore identifies this lift with an irreducible subrepresentation of the source’s square-integrable right translation action. Apply the factorization in [F6]. A scalar supported at a single place $v$ belongs to $Z$ and acts only on the factor at $v$. It fixes every elementary tensor. Since tensoring with fixed unit reference vectors at all other places preserves the norm of the vector at $v$, the scalar acts as the identity on the local factor at $v$. The lifted action is strongly continuous by [F2] because the lift composes the quotient action with the continuous projection in [F3]. The insertion of a local unit with identity at all other places is continuous by the full idele product topology at infinity and the basic open boxes at finite places. For an arbitrary vector $w$ in the factor at $v$, tensoring with the other reference vectors is an isometry and carries the orbit of $w$ to the orbit of the corresponding elementary tensor under the inserted local group. The orbit map of each local vector is therefore norm-continuous, which gives strong continuity by [F2]. For the local unit group at $v$, write $Z_v$ for its scalar subgroup and $\pi_v$ for its action before passage to the quotient. Define $\rho_v(tZ_v)=\pi_v(t)$. Replacing $t$ by $tz$ with $z\in Z_v$ leaves $\pi_v(t)$ unchanged because $\pi_v(z)=I$. The product formula in [F17] gives $\rho_v((tZ_v)(sZ_v))=\pi_v(ts)=\rho_v(tZ_v)\rho_v(sZ_v)$, and the identity coset acts by the identity. Since the two actions have the same operators, they have the same closed invariant subspaces, so $\rho_v$ is irreducible. Each descended vector orbit map pulls back to the continuous local orbit map. For an open subset of the factor Hilbert space, the preimage under the descended orbit map is open by [F17] because its preimage under the quotient projection is open upstairs. The real and finite quotient topologies here are those in [F1] and [F11], so [F2] gives strong continuity. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F6, F7, F11, F17, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 The quotient map $\widetilde H\to H$ sends the real component to its projective class and each finite component to its local scalar class by [F3] and [F11]. Use unit reference vectors fixed by the source’s defining subgroups at all but finitely many finite places as in [F6]. By step 1.2, these are fixed by $O_\ell^\times$ at all but finitely many finite places and therefore by its projective image in the descended action at those places. For $h\in H$, choose a lift in $\widetilde H$ by [F3]. On elementary tensors its action is the tensor of the local actions of the lift, which agrees coordinate by coordinate with the tensor of the descended actions at the components of $h$. These actions agree on the completion by density and unitarity. [F3, F4, F6, F7, F11, step 1.2, step 3.1]

4.2 Transport the irreducible unitary local factor from step 3.1 before scalar descent along the split isomorphism used for $J_\ell$. The isomorphism and its inverse are continuous by the finite-coordinate argument in step 1.2, so the transported action remains strongly continuous by [F2]. By [F8] the resulting acting group is the $\mathbb Q_\ell$-points of the general linear algebraic group of degree two, which is reductive by Milne’s example in [F6]. The embedding in [F8] is continuous for the matrix-entry topology by [F15], since the determinant is polynomial and inversion on $\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ is continuous. Projection onto the matrix entries gives its continuous inverse on the image. Thus the local-points topology agrees with the matrix-entry topology for which the action is strongly continuous. The field $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is nonarchimedean and locally compact by [F8] and nondiscrete since the distinct nonzero powers of $\ell$ converge to zero. The smooth-vector and admissibility results in [F6] therefore apply. For $n\ge1$, entrywise reduction of an integral matrix modulo $\ell^n$ gives $I$ exactly on $I+\ell^n M_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$ by [F10]. For $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ in this set, $ad-bc$ reduces to $1$ modulo $\ell$, so it is a unit of $\mathbb Z_\ell$ by [F10]. Multiplying $A$ on either side by $(ad-bc)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}$ gives $I$. Thus these sets are the kernels of the reduction homomorphisms on $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell)$. Each kernel is a product of four translates of $\ell^n\mathbb Z_\ell$. Multiplication by $\ell^n$ and translation are homeomorphisms of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ by [F15], with inverses multiplication by $\ell^{-n}$ and translation by the opposite constant, respectively. Hence these translates are compact open by [F8]. The inherited matrix topology on the kernel is this product topology, so [F12] makes it compact. Each kernel is open in $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q_\ell)$, and these kernels form a neighborhood basis at $I$ by [F10] and the product basis of the matrix coordinate topology. Every open subgroup therefore contains a compact open subgroup from this basis. Conversely, each compact open subgroup is open, so the source’s smooth vectors are exactly those fixed by some open subgroup. The lift of the projective local factor to the same matrix group has this action and meets the hypothesis of [F5]. Whenever the local factor has $J_\ell$-fixed vectors, [F5] gives the claimed line and character. [A1, F2, F5, F6, F8, F10, F12, F15, step 1.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Work in the full restricted Hilbert tensor product supplied by [F6] and realized projectively in step 4.1. Let $V_\ell$ be the factor at $\ell$ and choose a unit vector $e\in V_\ell$, using the reference vector there if one is prescribed. Let $W$ be the closed linear span in the full product of elementary tensors with $\ell$-coordinate $e$. This is a Hilbert space by [F4]: each Cauchy sequence converges in the full product and its limit lies in the closed subspace. For each elementary tensor $x$ in the full product with $\ell$-coordinate $u$, let $w_x\in W$ be the tensor obtained by replacing $u$ by $e$. For two such elementary tensors with coordinates $u,u'$, the inner product of $w_x,w_{x'}$ is the product of the pairings over the places other than $\ell$, because the omitted pairing has been replaced by $\langle e,e\rangle=1$. Thus $x\mapsto u\otimes w_x$ preserves pairings by the binary tensor construction in [F4]. By sesquilinearity it preserves the norms of finite sums, so a sum representing zero goes to zero and the rule defines a linear isometry $T$ on the original restricted elementary span. The range is dense in $V_\ell\widehat{\otimes}W$. Indeed, for any $u\otimes w$, choose finite sums $w_n$ from the defining span of $W$ with $\|w-w_n\|<1/n$. The regrouped images contain each $u\otimes w_n$ by replacing $e$ by $u$ in each summand, and their error has norm $\|u\|\,\|w-w_n\|$ by [F4]. Finite sums of binary elementary tensors are dense by [F4]. By [A1] and [F14], $T$ extends to a bounded linear map $\widehat T$ from the original tensor completion into the binary Hilbert product. Norm equality passes to the limit along a sequence from the dense domain of $T$, so $\widehat T$ is a linear isometry. Its range is closed because every convergent sequence of images has Cauchy preimages, which converge in the complete domain. The range contains the dense range of $T$, so $\widehat T$ is surjective.
