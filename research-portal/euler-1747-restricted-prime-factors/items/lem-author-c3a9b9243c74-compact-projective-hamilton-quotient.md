## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $B$ be the rational Hamilton quaternion algebra and $G=PB^\times$ its group of units modulo nonzero scalars. At a prime $\ell$, let $\mathbb Q_\ell$ be the $\ell$-adic field and $\mathbb Z_\ell$ its ring of integers. The finite adele ring $\mathbb A_f$ consists of tuples in $\prod_\ell\mathbb Q_\ell$ with entries in $\mathbb Z_\ell$ at all but finitely many primes, with coordinatewise operations. For the full adelic group $H=G(\mathbb R)\times G(\mathbb A_f)$ and diagonal subgroup $\Gamma=G(\mathbb Q)$, the coset space $X=\Gamma\backslash H$ with its quotient topology is compact. A coset is the set $\Gamma h=\{\gamma h:\gamma\in\Gamma\}$ and the quotient topology detects open sets by their preimages in $H$. Compactness means every open cover has a finite subcover.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] The Axiom of Choice gives a choice function for a family of nonempty sets. Use it to select representatives of the projective double cosets, finite trace subcovers, and original cover members realizing the selected traces ([[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] The map $a\mapsto a1$ gives $B$ its central rational scalar structure with basis $1,i,j,k$ ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]], Justification 1.1). The Hamilton product is bilinear and its basis $1,i,j,k$ satisfies $i^2=j^2=k^2=-1$ and $ij=k=-ji$, with the other products obtained by cyclic permutation. The reduced norm is $N(x)=x_0^2+x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2$, and $x\bar x=\bar x x=N(x)1$, where $\bar x$ negates the imaginary coordinates. An order $O$ is a unital subring with a $\mathbb Z$-basis that is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $B$. Its completion $O_\ell$ is the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span of that basis in $B_\ell$ ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions]]). A division ring is a unital ring with $1\ne0$ in which every nonzero element has a two-sided inverse ([[math:def-division-ring]]).

[F2] The full adelic group $H$ is a topological group with the product topology, $G(\mathbb R)$ is compact, and the two group operations of multiplication and inversion act componentwise. The finite component of the diagonal embedding is the injective rational map into $G(\mathbb A_f)$ ([[def-author-5b8600b4f4af-full-adelic-hamilton-group]], Definition and Justification 3.1 and 4.1).

[F3] A finite scalar idele belongs to $\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ at every prime and to $\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$ at all but finitely many primes. These tuples form $\mathbb A_f^\times$ ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-scalar-adeles-and-ideles]]). A rational number $a/b$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ and $b\ne0$ belongs to $\mathbb Z_\ell$ whenever $\ell$ does not divide $b$ ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-scalar-adeles-and-ideles]], Justification 2.2). Each nonzero integer has finitely many prime divisors, and a union of two finite sets is finite ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-scalar-adeles-and-ideles]], Justification 2.2 and 1.1). The finite quaternion ideles $\widehat B^\times$ are defined by the local groups $B_\ell^\times$ and the integral unit groups $O_\ell^\times$, and $\widehat O^\times=\prod_\ell O_\ell^\times$ is compact open ([[def-author-6f52147f3ab3-finite-quaternion-ideles]]). The quotient $G(\mathbb A_f)=\widehat B^\times/\mathbb A_f^\times$ by scalar ideles identifies topologically with the restricted product of the local projective groups $G(\mathbb Q_\ell)=B_\ell^\times/\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ using the open subgroups $K_\ell$ given by the images of $O_\ell^\times$. Its tuples lie in $K_\ell$ at all but finitely many primes, and a basic open box uses open local factors with $K_\ell$ at all but finitely many primes. The projective image $K_f=\prod_\ell K_\ell$ of $\widehat O^\times$ is compact open ([[def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units]]).

[F4] The projective ideal dictionary identifies $G(\mathbb Q)\backslash G(\mathbb A_f)/K_f$ with the locally principal right fractional ideal classes of that same order $O$. A locally principal right fractional ideal is a full integral lattice stable under right multiplication by $O$ whose completion at each $\ell$ is $x_\ell O_\ell$ for some invertible local quaternion $x_\ell$. Classes are taken up to left multiplication by $B^\times$ ([[thm-adelic-right-ideal-dictionary]]). A full integral lattice $I$ is the $\mathbb Z$-span of a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $B$. Its completion $I_\ell$ is the $\mathbb Z_\ell$-span of that basis in $B_\ell$ ([[def-author-24fda87362bc-quaternion-orders-and-lattice-completions]]). Write $\Gamma_f$ for the image of $\Gamma$ under projection to $G(\mathbb A_f)$. By [F2], it is the embedded subgroup $G(\mathbb Q)$ in the dictionary. For $g\in G(\mathbb A_f)$, the double coset $\Gamma_f g K_f$ is the set of products $u g k$ with $u\in\Gamma_f$ and $k\in K_f$ ([[math:def-double-cosets-of-two-subgroups]]).

[F5] A finite product of compact spaces is compact in its product topology, and a continuous map sends a compact subset to a compact subset of its target ([[math:thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces]], [[math:thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]], [[math:def-compact-space]]). A finite subfamily is empty or is the set of entries of a finite list, with repetitions allowed ([[math:def-compact-space]]). The product topology on a binary product has a basis of open rectangles ([[math:def-product-topology]]).

[F6] Left translations in a topological group are homeomorphisms ([[math:lem-topological-group-translations-and-inversion]]). The coset projection $q(h)=\Gamma h$ satisfies $q(\gamma h)=q(h)$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma$ and is continuous for the quotient topology ([[math:def-coset]], [[math:def-quotient-topology]]). Subspace opens are intersections with ambient open sets ([[math:def-subspace-topology-top]]).

[F7] The class set of an order in a finite-dimensional division algebra over a global field is finite under the base-ring hypotheses in External input. For a number field, the ring of $S$-integers with $S$ equal to the set of all archimedean places is its ring of integers.

[F8] The ring of integers of a number field is the integral closure of $\mathbb Z$ in that field, namely the elements integral over $\mathbb Z$ ([[math:def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field]], [[math:def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]). A complex number is an algebraic integer exactly when it is integral over $\mathbb Z$, that is, a root of a monic polynomial with integer coefficients ([[math:def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]]). A rational number is an algebraic integer if and only if it is an integer ([[math:cor-rational-algebraic-integers-are-integers]]).

[F9] A finite extension of $\mathbb Q$ is a number field and a global field. The sole archimedean place of $\mathbb Q$ is its real place, induced by the usual absolute value.

[F10] A finite set is in bijection with some initial segment $\{0,\ldots,s-1\}$ of the natural numbers ([[math:def-countable]]).

[F11] The open sets of a topology with a given basis are precisely the unions of its basic open sets ([[math:def-topology-basis-subbasis]]).

[F12] Partial sums of a finite list of natural numbers are natural numbers, with the empty sum zero and each next sum obtained by adding the next entry ([[math:def-nat-finite-sum-and-product]]).

[F13] The rationals form a totally ordered field ([[math:thm-rat-ordered-field]]). In an ordered field, the sum of two positive elements is positive ([[math:def-ordered-field]]). The square of each nonzero element of an ordered field is positive ([[math:lem-of-square-positive]]).

[F14] Use the integer embedding $n\mapsto [(n,1)]$ into $\mathbb Q$ ([[math:lem-int-embeds-rat]]). The rational embedding into $\mathbb R$ and the real embedding into $\mathbb C$ are unital field embeddings ([[math:lem-rat-embeds-dense]], [[math:thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field]]). Evaluation of a polynomial in a commutative target ring is the finite sum of its mapped coefficients times the corresponding powers of the argument ([[math:def-polynomial-evaluation-and-root]]).

[F15] For natural numbers, $a\le b$ means $a+j=b$ for some natural $j$, and $a<b$ means $a\le b$ with $a\ne b$ ([[math:def-nat-order]]). This is a linear order ([[math:thm-nat-linear-order]]). Addition has identity $0$ ([[math:lem-nat-add-identity]]). Addition is commutative ([[math:lem-nat-add-commutative]]). Adding the same natural number on the right preserves and reflects both $\le$ and $<$ ([[math:lem-nat-order-add-compatible]]).

[F16] A property holding at $0$ and passing from each natural number to its successor holds for every natural number ([[math:thm-induction-principle]]).

## External input

Voight’s Corollary 27.6.20 gives finiteness of the locally principal right fractional ideal classes of an $R$-order in a finite-dimensional division algebra over a global field $F$, where $R$ is the ring of $S$-integers for a finite nonempty set $S$ containing all archimedean places. The equivalence is by left multiplication by $B^\times$ as in Lemma 27.6.8. For a number field $F$, if $S$ consists of all archimedean places, this $R$ is the ring of integers of $F$.

The opening of Voight’s §14.4 defines a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$ as a number field and a global field. His §14.4.8 identifies the real place induced by the usual absolute value as the only archimedean place of $\mathbb Q$.

## Proof

**Given:** The groups $H$ and $\Gamma$ in the statement, the quotient topology on $X$, and [A1].

**Goal:** Cover $X$ by finitely many compact subsets.

1.1 Put $O=\mathbb Z1+\mathbb Zi+\mathbb Zj+\mathbb Zk$. This span contains $1$ and is closed under subtraction. Each product of basis elements is a signed basis element, so bilinearity gives closure under multiplication. The generators form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $O$ by their $\mathbb Q$-linear independence and form a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $B$. Thus $O$ is a full order. At least one coordinate of a nonzero $x$ is nonzero by the basis in [F1]. By [F13], each nonzero coordinate has positive square and every zero coordinate contributes zero. Adding the positive terms gives $N(x)>0$ by closure under addition. The scalar $N(x)$ therefore has a rational inverse, and the norm identity in [F1] gives the two-sided inverse $\bar x/N(x)$ of $x$. The basis contains the identity $1$, so $1\ne0$ in $B$. Thus $B$ is a division ring by [F1]. Together with the central action $a\mapsto a1$ and the four-element rational basis from [F1], this makes $B$ a four-dimensional division algebra over $\mathbb Q$. [F1, F13]

2.1 Use this order for $K_f$ in [F3]. For any other full order $O'$, write $b_0,\ldots,b_3$ for the basis of $O$ and $c_0,\ldots,c_3$ for the basis of $O'$. For each $r=0,\ldots,3$, expand $b_r=\sum_{s=0}^3 a_{sr}c_s$ and $c_r=\sum_{s=0}^3 d_{sr}b_s$ with rational coefficients. Choose a nonzero integer denominator for each coefficient, using denominator $1$ for zero. Each denominator has a finite prime-divisor set by [F3]. Apply the two-set union rule from [F3] successively to the finite list of these sets to obtain a finite set of exceptional primes. Every coefficient belongs to $\mathbb Z_\ell$ outside that set by [F3]. At each prime outside that set, the expansion of every $b_r$ in the $c_s$ puts $b_r$ in $O'_\ell$, so $O_\ell\subseteq O'_\ell$. The expansions of the $c_r$ in the $b_s$ give $O'_\ell\subseteq O_\ell$ by the same span description in [F1]. Thus the completed orders, their unit groups and their projective images agree outside the finite set. The restricted products have the same tuples. Every basic box for either choice is a basic box for the other, since its local factors are open and the finite set of exceptions may include all primes where the integral factors differ. By [F11], both topologies consist of the unions of these same boxes, so the identity on tuples identifies the two topologies. The diagonal rational map is coordinatewise in both presentations, so this identification preserves $\Gamma$ in $H$. [F1, F2, F3, F11, step 1.1]

3.1 Take $F=\mathbb Q$, a degree-one extension of itself and hence a number field and a global field by [F9]. The set $S$ consisting of its real place is finite and nonempty and contains all archimedean places by [F9]. By [F7], $R$ is the ring of integers of $\mathbb Q$. By [F8], its elements are the rational numbers satisfying a monic polynomial over $\mathbb Z$. Let $\iota\colon\mathbb Q\to\mathbb C$ be the composite of the two field embeddings in [F14]. For each $f\in\mathbb Z[T]$ and $x\in\mathbb Q$, we have $\iota(f(x))=f(\iota(x))$: the unital map $\iota$ takes the image of each integer coefficient to the same integer multiple of $1$ in $\mathbb C$ and preserves the powers, products and finite sums in the evaluation formula in [F14]. By injectivity, $f(x)=0$ if and only if $f(\iota(x))=0$. With $f$ monic this identifies the integral rationals with the rational algebraic integers, which are precisely the integers by [F8]. Hence $R=\mathbb Z$. The full order and division algebra from step 1.1 then meet the hypotheses of [F7]. The ideal dictionary for that order then gives finitely many double cosets for the embedded subgroup $\Gamma_f=G(\mathbb Q)$ in [F4]. By [F10], index these double cosets by $0,\ldots,s-1$. Each double coset is nonempty by its product description in [F4], so use [A1] to choose a representative $g_i\in G(\mathbb A_f)$ for each index. By the membership rule in [F4], each finite adelic element belongs to some $\Gamma_f g_i K_f$ and so is $\gamma_f g_i k_f$ for some $\gamma_f\in\Gamma_f$ and $k_f\in K_f$. By the definition of $\Gamma_f$, some $\gamma\in\Gamma$ has finite component $\gamma_f$. [A1, F2, F4, F7, F8, F9, F10, F14, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Put $K_0=G(\mathbb R)\times K_f$. Both factors are compact, so $K_0$ is compact for their product topology. An ambient basic rectangle $U\times V$ has trace $U\times(V\cap K_f)$ on $K_0$. By [F6], every basic open rectangle in $K_0$ is such a trace. By [F5] and [F11], every ambient open is a union of basic rectangles, so its trace is a union of the traced rectangles and therefore open in the product topology on $K_0$. Conversely every product open in $K_0$ is a union of traced rectangles, each of which is subspace open by [F6]. Thus the product and subspace topologies agree. For $h=(h_\infty,\gamma_f g_i k_f)$ as in step 3.1, take the lift $\gamma=(\gamma_\infty,\gamma_f)$. The componentwise laws in [F2] give $\gamma^{-1}h=(\gamma_\infty^{-1}h_\infty,\gamma_f^{-1}(\gamma_f g_i k_f))$. In the finite factor, associativity and the inverse law reduce $\gamma_f^{-1}(\gamma_f g_i k_f)$ to $g_i k_f$. Hence $\gamma^{-1}h=(1,g_i)(\gamma_\infty^{-1}h_\infty,k_f)\in(1,g_i)K_0$. Left translation by $(1,g_i)$ is continuous, so $(1,g_i)K_0$ is a compact subset of $H$. The continuous projection $q$ sends this translate to a compact subset $C_i$ of $X$. Since $q(\gamma^{-1}h)=q(h)$, the sets $C_i$ cover $X$. [F2, F3, F5, F6, F11, step 3.1]

5.1 If $\mathcal U$ is an open cover of $X$, the traces $U\cap C_i$ with $U\in\mathcal U$ are open in $C_i$ and cover $C_i$. By compactness and [F5], choose for each $i$ a finite list of traces covering $C_i$, using the empty list if $C_i$ is empty. For each listed trace choose a member of $\mathcal U$ giving that trace, so the chosen members form a list covering $C_i$. [A1, F5, F6, step 4.1]

6.1 For each $0\le i<s$, write the list from step 5.1 as $(U_{i,j})_{0\le j<m_i}$. Set $M_i=\sum_{r<i}m_r$ for $0\le i\le s$. By [F12], $M_0=0$ and $M_{i+1}=M_i+m_i$ for $i<s$. For $i<s$ let $I_i=\{k\in\mathbb N:M_i\le k<M_{i+1}\}$. The order definition in [F15] gives $M_i\le M_{i+1}$ since $M_{i+1}=M_i+m_i$. By linearity of this order, $\{k\in\mathbb N:k<M_{i+1}\}$ is the disjoint union of $\{k\in\mathbb N:k<M_i\}$ and $I_i$. The first initial segment, for $M_0=0$, is empty since $0+k=k$ gives $0\le k$ for every natural $k$. By finite induction using [F16], the intervals $I_i$ for $i<s$ partition $\{k\in\mathbb N:k<M_s\}$, allowing empty intervals. [F12, F15, F16, step 3.1, step 5.1]

7.1 Fix $i<s$. The map $j\mapsto M_i+j$ takes $\{j\in\mathbb N:j<m_i\}$ bijectively onto $I_i$. Indeed $M_i\le M_i+j$ by the definition of order, and $M_i+j<M_{i+1}=M_i+m_i$ is equivalent to $j<m_i$ by commutativity and order compatibility in [F15]. For every $k\in I_i$, the inequality $M_i\le k$ gives a natural $j$ with $M_i+j=k$, and then $j<m_i$ by the equivalence. The index $j$ is unique because strict order of two different natural numbers would give strict order of their translates. [F15, step 6.1]

8.1 Place $U_{i,j}$ at position $M_i+j$. By the partition and bijections this gives one list of length $M_s$ containing every chosen member.
