## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For the full adelic projective Hamilton group $H=G(\mathbb A)$ with diagonal rational subgroup $\Gamma=G(\mathbb Q)$, the subgroup $\Gamma$ is closed in $H$ (its complement is open) and discrete (each singleton in $\Gamma$ is open in the subspace topology). The space $X=\Gamma\backslash H$ consists of the cosets $\Gamma h=\{\gamma h:\gamma\in\Gamma\}$ and has the quotient topology. It is locally compact Hausdorff. Right multiplication defines the right action $X\times H\to X$, $(x,a)\mapsto xa$, where $(\Gamma h)a=\Gamma(ha)$, $xe=x$ for the identity $e$ of $H$, and $(xa)b=x(ab)$ for all $x\in X$ and $a,b\in H$. This map is continuous for the product topology on $X\times H$. There exists a nonzero right-invariant Radon measure $\mu$ on $X$, and every such measure is positive on each nonempty open subset. Right invariance means $\mu(Eh)=\mu(E)$ for every Borel set $E$ and every $h\in H$, where $Eh=\{xh:x\in E\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The full group $H$ and rational embedding, the full order $O\subset B$ with its basis used to specify the finite level, and the Axiom of Choice.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice says every indexed family of nonempty sets admits a choice function ([math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice)). It applies to the Haar existence and quotient measure results used below.

[F1] The group $H$ is locally compact Hausdorff, and the diagonal embedding $G(\mathbb Q)\to H$ is a group homomorphism with image $\Gamma$ ([def-author-5b8600b4f4af-full-adelic-hamilton-group](item-def-author-5b8600b4f4af-full-adelic-hamilton-group)). The finite level $K_f=\prod_\ell K_\ell$ is open, and each $K_\ell$ is the projective image of $O_\ell^\times$. Every local order unit has at least one unit coefficient in the order basis ([def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units](item-def-finite-quaternion-adeles-and-projective-units)). The order $O_\ell$ consists of quaternions with order-basis coefficients in $\mathbb Z_\ell$, the unit ball in $\mathbb Q_\ell$, and a coefficient in $\mathbb Z_\ell$ is a unit exactly when its absolute value is $1$. The rational Hamilton norm is the sum of four squares and is multiplicative. The inverse of a nonzero rational quaternion $x$ is $\bar x/N(x)$ ([def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions](item-def-author-6f52147f3ab3-hamilton-order-completions)).

[F2] Every prime $\ell$ satisfies $\ell>1$ ([math:def-prime](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-prime)). An integer divides another when the latter is an integer multiple of the former. A positive divisor of a positive integer is at most that integer ([math:def-divides-in-z](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-divides-in-z), [math:lem-divisor-bound](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-divisor-bound)). Write rationals as fractions of integers with nonzero denominator, with equality tested by cross multiplication ([math:def-rationals](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-rationals)). The gcd of a pair of integers divides both entries and is divisible by every common divisor. It is positive if some entry is nonzero, and the gcd of $(0,0)$ is $0$ ([math:def-common-divisor-and-gcd](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-common-divisor-and-gcd), [math:cor-common-divisor-divides-gcd](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-common-divisor-divides-gcd)). For nonzero rational $a/b$, its $\ell$-adic absolute value is $\ell^{-v_\ell(a/b)}$, where $v_\ell(a/b)$ is the exponent of $\ell$ in $a$ minus its exponent in $b$ ([math:def-p-adic-absolute-value-on-the-rationals](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-p-adic-absolute-value-on-the-rationals), [math:lem-p-adic-valuation-on-q](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-p-adic-valuation-on-q)). Prime factorization supplies a prime divisor of any integer greater than one ([math:thm-fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic)). The rational absolute value extends to a multiplicative absolute value on $\mathbb Q_\ell$ ([math:thm-p-adic-completion-is-a-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-p-adic-completion-is-a-field)).

[F3] A subgroup contains the identity and is closed under multiplication and inverses ([math:def-subgroup](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-subgroup)). A quotient topology tests openness by inverse image. Cosets $\Gamma h$ depend only on the representative modulo left multiplication by $\Gamma$. Topological group multiplication and inversion are continuous, and translations are homeomorphisms since the inverse of translation by an element is translation by its inverse ([math:def-quotient-topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-quotient-topology), [math:def-coset](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-coset), [math:def-topological-group](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-topological-group)). A map is continuous exactly when open sets have open preimages. An open map sends open sets to open sets. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse ([math:def-continuous-map-top](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-continuous-map-top), [math:def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps)). For a closed subgroup $L$ of a locally compact Hausdorff group $J$, the space $J/L$ of cosets $jL$ is locally compact Hausdorff under the choice hypothesis ([Compact lifts and averaging onto $C_c(G/H)$](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-closed-subgroup-quotient-averaging-and-compact-lifts)).

[F4] A left Haar measure is a nonzero left-invariant Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff group and exists under [A1] ([math:cor-existence-of-left-and-right-haar-measures](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-existence-of-left-and-right-haar-measures)). The modular function $\Delta_J(j)$ is the positive factor by which right translation by $j^{-1}$ scales the left Haar integral. It is a homomorphism to the multiplicative group of positive reals ([math:def-modular-function-of-a-locally-compact-group](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-modular-function-of-a-locally-compact-group), [math:thm-the-modular-function-is-a-continuous-homomorphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-the-modular-function-is-a-continuous-homomorphism)). Under [A1], if $L$ is closed in $J$ and $\Delta_J|_L=\Delta_L$, then $J/L$ has a nonzero left $J$-invariant Radon measure ([math:prop-invariant-measure-on-g-mod-h-iff-modular-functions-agree](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/prop-invariant-measure-on-g-mod-h-iff-modular-functions-agree)).

[F5] The Borel sigma-algebra is the smallest sigma-algebra containing the open sets. A sigma-algebra contains the whole space and is closed under complements and countable unions ([math:def-borel-sigma-algebra](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-borel-sigma-algebra), [math:def-sigma-algebra](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-sigma-algebra)). A measure gives the empty set mass zero and is countably additive on pairwise disjoint measurable sets ([math:def-measure](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-measure)). A Radon measure is a Borel measure finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets, and inner regular on open sets. In particular the mass of an open set is the supremum of the masses of its compact subsets ([math:def-radon-measure-on-an-lch-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-radon-measure-on-an-lch-space)).

[F6] A set is closed when its complement is open. Arbitrary unions and finite intersections of open sets are open ([math:def-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-topological-space)). Subspace opens are traces of ambient open sets ([math:def-subspace-topology-top](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-subspace-topology-top)). In a Hausdorff space distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods, so singletons are closed ([math:def-hausdorff-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-hausdorff-space)). Local compactness means every point has a compact neighborhood. Every ambient open cover of a compact subset has a finite subcover, and continuous images of compact subsets are compact ([math:def-locally-compact-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-compact-space), [math:def-compact-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-compact-space), [math:lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient), [math:thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps)). Products of open sets in two factor spaces form a basis of their product topology ([math:def-product-topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-product-topology)).

[F7] A measure is monotone under inclusion of measurable sets and finitely subadditive on unions of measurable sets ([math:prop-measure-monotonicity](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/prop-measure-monotonicity), [math:thm-finite-and-countable-subadditivity-of-measures](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-finite-and-countable-subadditivity-of-measures)).

[F8] A product of four finite sets is finite by the finite product rule ([math:thm-product-rule](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-product-rule)).

## Proof

**Goal:** Verify discreteness and the modular equality, and construct the measure and action on the indicated coset space.

1.1 Suppose the finite component of $\gamma\in\Gamma$ belongs to $K_f$. Clear denominators in the order-basis coefficients of a rational representative to get an integral tuple $(n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3)$ with at least one nonzero entry. Set $d=\gcd(\gcd(n_0,n_1),\gcd(n_2,n_3))$. At least one inner gcd is positive, so $d>0$. The binary gcd properties applied to the two pairs and then to their gcds show that $d$ divides each $n_r$ and that every common divisor of all four entries divides $d$. Divide the representative by $d$ to obtain $x$ with integral coefficients $n_r/d$. If some prime $\ell$ divided every $n_r/d$, then $\ell d$ would be a common divisor of all four $n_r$ and would divide $d$. The positive divisor bound would give $\ell d\le d$, contrary to $\ell d>d$ because $\ell>1$ and $d>0$. Thus for every prime $\ell$ at least one coefficient of $x$ is not divisible by $\ell$. The rational valuation formula gives that coefficient absolute value $1$ and all integral coefficients absolute value at most $1$, so the maximum is $1$. [F1, F2]

1.2 The diagonal embedding sends the rational identity to $e$. If $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ are the images of $r_1$ and $r_2$ in $G(\mathbb Q)$, then $\gamma_1\gamma_2$ is the image of $r_1r_2$ and $\gamma_1^{-1}$ is the image of $r_1^{-1}$ by the componentwise group operations. Thus $\Gamma$ is a subgroup of $H$. Right multiplication is well defined on $X$ because changing $h$ to $\gamma h$ changes $ha$ to $\gamma ha$. The identities $xe=x$ and $(xa)b=x(ab)$ follow from the identity and associativity in $H$. The quotient map $p:H\to X$ is open because $p^{-1}(p(U))=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma U$ for open $U\subseteq H$. The map $p\times\mathrm{id}_H$ sends every basic open rectangle $U\times V$ to the open rectangle $p(U)\times V$. Its inverse image of an open rectangle $A\times V$ is $p^{-1}(A)\times V$, which is open since $p$ is continuous. Thus $p\times\mathrm{id}_H$ is a continuous open surjection, so the codomain has its quotient topology. The composite of the action with this product map sends $(h,a)$ to $p(ha)$ and is continuous. The quotient topology test then makes the action continuous. For a fixed $a\in H$, the map $x\mapsto (x,a)$ is continuous by the rectangle basis, so $x\mapsto xa$ is continuous. Its inverse is right translation by $a^{-1}$, which is continuous for the same reason. Thus each right translation is a homeomorphism. [F1, F3, F6]

2.1 At each prime $\ell$, write $x=a_\ell u_\ell$ with $a_\ell\in\mathbb Q_\ell^\times$ and $u_\ell\in O_\ell^\times$, which is possible by the definition of $K_\ell$. The maximum of the absolute values of the coefficients of $u_\ell$ is $1$ by its unit coefficient. By multiplicativity on $\mathbb Q_\ell$, the maximum for $x$ is $|a_\ell|_\ell$ times the maximum for $u_\ell$. Hence $|a_\ell|_\ell=1$ and $|a_\ell^{-1}|_\ell=1$. This gives $a_\ell^{-1}\in\mathbb Z_\ell$ and $x^{-1}=a_\ell^{-1}u_\ell^{-1}\in O_\ell$. Every rational order-basis coefficient of $x^{-1}$ now has absolute value at most $1$ at every prime. Zero coefficients are integral. For a nonzero rational coefficient write $m/n$ with $n>0$, and set $g=\gcd(m,n)>0$. Replacing the numerator and denominator by $m'=m/g$ and $n'=n/g$ gives the same fraction by cross multiplication. If a prime $\ell$ divided both $m'$ and $n'$, then $\ell g$ would be a common divisor of $m,n$ and would divide $g$, giving $\ell g\le g$ by the positive divisor bound. But $\ell>1$ and $g>0$ give $\ell g>g$. If the coefficient is nonintegral, $n'>1$. A prime divisor $\ell$ of $n'$ then has positive exponent in $n'$ and zero exponent in $m'$, so $v_\ell(m'/n')<0$. Since $\ell>1$, the absolute value $\ell^{-v_\ell(m'/n')}$ is greater than $1$. Therefore $x,x^{-1}\in O$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 There is a fixed integer $D\ge1$ clearing all denominators of the coefficients of the norm polynomial in the order basis. Thus $N(x)$ and $N(x^{-1})$ are positive multiples of $1/D$. Their product is $1$, so $N(x)\le D$. Each Hamilton coordinate has square at most $D$ and absolute value at most $D$. Write each entry of the fixed matrix from Hamilton to order-basis coordinates as $m_{rs}/n_{rs}$ with $m_{rs}\in\mathbb Z$ and $n_{rs}\ge1$. Since $n_{rs}\ge1$ and the Hamilton coordinate $x_s$ lies between $-D$ and $D$, the summand $(m_{rs}/n_{rs})x_s$ lies between $-D|m_{rs}|$ and $D|m_{rs}|$. Adding the four bounds shows that the $r$-th order-basis coordinate has absolute value at most $D\sum_{s=0}^3|m_{rs}|$. Thus $M=D(1+\sum_{r,s=0}^3|m_{rs}|)$ is a common integer bound. Each order-basis coordinate belongs to the finite set $S=\{-M,-M+1,\ldots,M\}$, enumerated by $j\mapsto j-M$ for $j=0,\ldots,2M$. The product rule makes $S^4$ finite, and each tuple in it represents at most one projective class. Thus $\Gamma\cap(G_\infty\times K_f)$ is finite. [F1, F2, F8, step 2.1]

4.1 Remove the finitely many nonidentity points of this intersection from $G_\infty\times K_f$. Since singletons are closed in $H$, the result is an open identity neighborhood $V$ with $V\cap\Gamma=\{e\}$. This trace is open in $\Gamma$, and translation makes every singleton of $\Gamma$ open in $\Gamma$. Continuity of $(r,s)\mapsto r^{-1}s$ gives open identity neighborhoods $W_1,W_2$ with $W_1^{-1}W_2\subseteq V$. Put $W=W_1\cap W_2$. If $\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in hW\cap\Gamma$, closure under inverses and multiplication puts $\gamma_1^{-1}\gamma_2$ in $\Gamma$. Writing $\gamma_i=hw_i$ with $w_i\in W$ shows $\gamma_1^{-1}\gamma_2=w_1^{-1}w_2\in W^{-1}W\subseteq V$. Hence $\gamma_1^{-1}\gamma_2\in V\cap\Gamma=\{e\}$, so $\gamma_1=\gamma_2$. Each $hW$ thus contains at most one point of $\Gamma$. For any $h\in H\setminus\Gamma$, the open neighborhood $hW$ is disjoint from $\Gamma$ or meets it at one point $\gamma\ne h$. In the latter case $hW\setminus\{\gamma\}$ is open because the singleton is closed. In either case $h$ has an open neighborhood in $H\setminus\Gamma$. The complement is therefore open as a union of such neighborhoods, so $\Gamma$ is closed and discrete. [F1, F3, F6, step 3.1]

5.1 Each element of $\Gamma$ is conjugate within $\Gamma$ to its inverse. Indeed write a rational representative as $x=x_0+z$ with $z=ai+bj+ck$ pure imaginary. If $z=0$ the class is the identity. Otherwise choose $y=bi-aj$ when $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$ and $y=i$ when $(a,b)=(0,0)$. In both cases $y\ne0$ and the Hamilton multiplication rules give $yz+zy=0$. Since $N(y)>0$, this yields $yxy^{-1}=x_0-z=\bar x=N(x)x^{-1}$. The scalar factor disappears in the projective class. The discrete topology on $\Gamma$ is Hausdorff and both its inversion and its multiplication are continuous, since $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma\times\Gamma$ are discrete. Each singleton is an open compact neighborhood of its point, so $\Gamma$ is locally compact. Both $H$ and $\Gamma$ therefore have modular functions under [A1]. For either modular function $f$, multiplicativity gives $f(e)=f(e)^2$, hence $f(e)=1$ by positivity. Then $f(g)f(g^{-1})=f(e)=1$, so $f(g^{-1})=f(g)^{-1}$. Multiplicativity and commutativity of real multiplication give $f(ugu^{-1})=f(u)f(g)f(u)^{-1}=f(g)$. For $\gamma\in\Gamma$, the conjugacy just proved applies in both groups, giving $f(\gamma)=f(\gamma^{-1})=f(\gamma)^{-1}$. Positivity gives $f(\gamma)=1$. Thus $\Delta_H(\gamma)=\Delta_\Gamma(\gamma)=1$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$. [A1, F1, F4, F6, step 4.1]

6.1 The closed-subgroup result makes $H/\Gamma$ locally compact Hausdorff. The modular equality gives a nonzero left-invariant Radon measure $\lambda$ there. Inversion on $H$ descends to the bijection $\phi(h\Gamma)=\Gamma h^{-1}$, because replacing $h$ by $h\gamma$ changes the inverse to $\gamma^{-1}h^{-1}$. The two coset topologies are quotient topologies, so inversion on $H$ induces continuous maps $\phi$ and $\phi^{-1}$ in the two directions. The homeomorphism carries compact neighborhoods and disjoint open neighborhoods from $H/\Gamma$ to $X$, so $X$ is locally compact Hausdorff. [A1, F3, F4, F6, step 4.1, step 5.1]

7.1 For any continuous map between topological spaces, the sets whose preimages are Borel form a sigma-algebra because preimages commute with complements and countable unions and take the whole codomain to the whole domain. It contains all open sets, so preimages of Borel sets are Borel. Applying this in both directions, the homeomorphism $\phi$ bijects the Borel sets of $H/\Gamma$ and $X$. Define $\mu(E)=\lambda(\phi^{-1}(E))$ for Borel $E\subseteq X$. The empty set has mass zero. Preimages of a pairwise disjoint sequence are pairwise disjoint, and the preimage of its union is their union, so countable additivity of $\lambda$ gives countable additivity of $\mu$. Also $\mu(X)=\lambda(H/\Gamma)>0$ since $\lambda$ is nonzero. Open supersets of $E$ correspond under $\phi^{-1}$ to open supersets of its preimage, and compact subsets of an open set correspond to compact subsets of its preimage. Finite mass on compact sets, outer regularity on Borel sets and inner regularity on open sets thus follow from the same properties of $\lambda$. [F3, F5, F6, F7, step 6.1]

8.1 For Borel $E\subseteq X$ and $a\in H$, the translate $Ea$ is Borel because it is the preimage of $E$ under the continuous right translation by $a^{-1}$. The identity $\phi(a^{-1}h\Gamma)=\phi(h\Gamma)a$ gives $\phi^{-1}(Ea)=a^{-1}\phi^{-1}(E)$. By left invariance of $\lambda$, this yields $\mu(Ea)=\lambda(a^{-1}\phi^{-1}(E))=\lambda(\phi^{-1}(E))=\mu(E)$. [F4, F5, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 1.2]

9.1 Let $\mu$ be any nonzero right-invariant Radon measure on $X$ and $U$ any nonempty open set. Right multiplication is transitive because $h^{-1}h'$ sends $\Gamma h$ to $\Gamma h'$. The open right translates of $U$ therefore cover $X$. For a compact subset $K$, fix any point $z$ outside $K$. Open neighborhoods of points of $K$ disjoint from some open neighborhood of $z$ cover $K$ by Hausdorff separation. Take a finite subcover and intersect the corresponding open neighborhoods of $z$ to obtain an open neighborhood of $z$ disjoint from $K$. Thus $K$ is closed and Borel. Choose finitely many translates $Uh_i$ covering $K$. Monotonicity and finite subadditivity give $\mu(K)\le\mu(\bigcup_i Uh_i)\le\sum_i\mu(Uh_i)$. If $\mu(U)=0$, right invariance makes the sum zero for every such cover. Inner regularity on the open set $X$ then gives $\mu(X)=0$, and monotonicity forces $\mu$ to be the zero measure. Thus $\mu(U)>0$. [F5, F6, F7, step 6.1, step 1.2, step 8.1] ∎
