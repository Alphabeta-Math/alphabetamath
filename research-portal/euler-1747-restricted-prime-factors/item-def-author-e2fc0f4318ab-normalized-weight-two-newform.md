## Definition

Assume countable choice, meaning that a sequence of nonempty sets has a choice function. Use the weight-two cusp space $S_2(N,\epsilon)$ with $f|_2\gamma=\epsilon(d_\gamma)f$ on $\Gamma_0(N)$ and $q=\exp(2\pi i z)$ at infinity. For $M$ a proper positive divisor of $N$ and $\epsilon_M$ a Dirichlet character modulo $M$ inducing $\epsilon$ by reduction on units, use the lower-level images $z\mapsto g(tz)$ with $g\in S_2(M,\epsilon_M)$ and $t$ a positive divisor of $N/M$. The old subspace is the complex span of those images. Write $W$ for the old subspace. Use the Petersson inner product
$$\langle f,g\rangle_N=\int_{\Gamma_0(N)\backslash\mathfrak H} f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,dx\,dy,\qquad z=x+iy.$$
Here $dx\,dy$ denotes planar Lebesgue measure $\lambda_2$. The quotient integral is the complex Lebesgue integral on a measurable fundamental region. For such a region $F$, there is a $\Gamma_0(N)$-invariant set $X\subseteq\mathfrak H$ with null complement such that $F\cap X$ meets each orbit of the fractional linear action of $\Gamma_0(N)$ in $X$ exactly once. The new subspace is the orthogonal complement of $W$, consisting of those $f\in S_2(N,\epsilon)$ for which $\langle f,g\rangle_N=0$ for every $g\in W$.

For a positive integer $m$ use the classical Hecke formula in weight two
$$(T_m f)(z)=m\sum_{\substack{ad=m\\a,d>0}}\epsilon(a)d^{-2}\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}f\bigl((az+b)/d\bigr).$$
A normalized weight-two cuspidal newform, also called a normalized primitive form, is a cusp form in the new subspace with $a_1=1$ in its expansion $\sum_{n\ge1}a_n q^n$, and with $T_m f$ equal to a scalar multiple of $f$ for every $m\ge1$.

The nonnegative integral is monotone by its supremum definition and agrees with the simple integral on nonnegative simple measurable functions. In particular it gives $b\lambda_2(B)$ on a constant $b\ge0$ times the indicator of a measurable set $B$ of finite area. A real function on a measure space is measurable if every strict superlevel set is measurable. A nonnegative measurable function with finite range in $[0,+\infty)$ is simple. For a finite constant $b\ge0$ and a measurable set $B$, the function $b\mathbf{1}_B$ takes values in $\{0,b\}$ and its strict superlevel sets are among the empty set, $B$ and the whole space. A complex measurable function is integrable when its absolute integral is finite. Its integral is computed from the positive and negative parts of its real and imaginary parts, and is complex-linear. [F9] On an open subset of $\mathbb C$, holomorphy means complex differentiability at every point. [math:def-complex-differentiability-holomorphic-and-entire](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-complex-differentiability-holomorphic-and-entire) A holomorphic function on an open subset of $\mathbb C$ is continuous there by complex differentiability at each point. ## Justification

**Given:** Countable choice, the level $N$, the character $\epsilon$ and the slash action on the cusp spaces in the definition.

1.1 Put $A=\operatorname{diag}(t,1)$ for a lower-level image. If $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(N)$, the matrix $A\gamma A^{-1}$ has integral entries, determinant one, lower left entry $c_\gamma/t$ divisible by $M$ and lower right entry $d_\gamma$. The latter is a unit modulo $N$ by the determinant-one relation and hence also modulo $M$. Using $A\gamma=(A\gamma A^{-1})A$, the slash law and $g|_2 A=t\,g(tz)$ give the required transformation law with character $\epsilon$. For any $\sigma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, the point $A\sigma\infty$ is rational or infinity by the projective boundary action. Use the cusp representative construction to choose $\sigma'$ with $\sigma'\infty=A\sigma\infty$. The positive-determinant matrix $(\sigma')^{-1}A\sigma$ fixes infinity, so it is upper triangular. Its slash factor is constant and its action multiplies imaginary parts by a positive constant. The uniform cusp decay of $g|_2\sigma'$ now implies that of $(z\mapsto g(tz))|_2\sigma$, and $g(tz)$ is holomorphic by [F1]. Thus all lower-level images lie in the complex vector space $S_2(N,\epsilon)$ supplied by [F1]. Their span in this space is a subspace, including the zero subspace if there are no images. [F1, F2, F3, F4]

1.2 For any $\sigma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ the map $\varphi_\sigma:z\mapsto\sigma z$ is a bijection of the open set $\mathfrak H$ with inverse $\varphi_{\sigma^{-1}}$ by [F5]. Its denominator is nonzero on $\mathfrak H$ and the derivative of $z\mapsto\sigma z$ is $(c_\sigma z+d_\sigma)^{-2}$ by [F7]. Its real differential is multiplication by this complex number. The real matrix of multiplication varies continuously with $z$ because $(c_\sigma z+d_\sigma)^{-2}$ is continuous there. This applies to the inverse matrix as well, so $\varphi_\sigma$ is a $C^1$ diffeomorphism of $\mathfrak H$. In complex dimension one the matrix of multiplication has that number as its sole complex entry, so the real determinant of the differential is its squared modulus. Thus the absolute Jacobian determinant is $J_\sigma(z)=|c_\sigma z+d_\sigma|^{-4}$. [F5, F7]

2.1 Write $\Gamma=\Gamma_0(N)$ and $D=\{z=x+iy\in\mathfrak H:|x|<1/2,\ |z|>1\}$. Its boundary in $\mathfrak H$ is null since it lies in the zero set of $(4x^2-1)(x^2+y^2-1)$. By step 1.2 and [F6] each modular translate of the boundary is null. The modular group is countable because bounding the absolute values of its integer entries leaves only finitely many matrices. The union of all modular translates of the boundary is therefore null by [F6]. The complement is invariant under the full modular group. On it, every full modular orbit meets $D$ once with trivial stabilizer modulo $\{\pm I\}$, since an orbit meeting the closure and avoiding the boundary meets the interior. Two integral determinant-one matrices congruent entrywise modulo $N$ lie in the same right coset of $\Gamma$ by [F5], since their product $\gamma_1\gamma_2^{-1}$ is congruent to the identity and therefore belongs to $\Gamma$. There are at most $N^4$ entrywise residue patterns. Choose a finite list of representatives $\sigma_i$ of the right cosets $\Gamma\sigma_i$. Put $F_0=\bigcup_i\sigma_i D$. Every full modular translate of a point in $D$ lies in the $\Gamma$-orbit of one of its translates by the $\sigma_i$. If $\sigma_i z$ and $\sigma_j w$ with $z,w\in D$ are $\Gamma$-equivalent, then $z=w$ by the full modular orbit condition, and the class of $(\gamma\sigma_j)^{-1}\sigma_i$ fixes $z$ in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ for some $\gamma\in\Gamma$. The stabilizer is trivial there, so $\sigma_i=\pm\gamma\sigma_j$. Since $\{\pm I\}\subseteq\Gamma$, this gives $i=j$. In particular $F_0$ is a disjoint union of open sets and is a fundamental region. [F1, F3, F5, F6, step 1.2]

2.2 Fix $f,g\in S_2(N,\epsilon)$ and put $h=f\overline g$. Use the Jacobian from step 1.2. The slash formula gives $h(\sigma z)J_\sigma(z)=(f|_2\sigma)(z)\overline{(g|_2\sigma)(z)}$. In particular $h(\gamma z)J_\gamma(z)=h(z)$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma$, because $\epsilon(d_\gamma)$ has modulus one. The same density identity holds for $|h|$. Since $f$ and $g$ are holomorphic on $\mathfrak H$, they are continuous by [F9]. Complex conjugation preserves distances, and the identity $uv-u'v'=u(v-v')+(u-u')v'$ shows continuity of multiplication since $u$ stays bounded near $u'$. Thus $h=f\overline g$ is continuous. Taking modulus, real part or imaginary part preserves continuity since each of these maps changes by at most the modulus of the difference of its inputs. Take the trace sigma-algebra $\mathcal L(\mathbb R^2)|_{\mathfrak H}$ of the planar Lebesgue sigma-algebra and define $\lambda_{\mathfrak H}(A)=\lambda_2(A)$ for each trace set $A$. Such sets are ambient Lebesgue measurable because $\mathfrak H$ is open and hence Lebesgue measurable by [F6]. The value on the empty set and countable additivity on disjoint trace sets follow directly from those for $\lambda_2$, since their unions are the same in $\mathfrak H$ and in the plane. This gives the measure space used on $\mathfrak H$. For $u=|h|$, $\operatorname{Re}h$ or $\operatorname{Im}h$ and each real $a$, the set $\{z\in\mathfrak H:u(z)>a\}$ is the preimage of the open ray $(a,\infty)$ under $u$, so it is open in $\mathfrak H$ by [F14] and hence open in the plane. It is Borel and belongs to the trace sigma-algebra, so the criterion in [F8] makes each of the three functions measurable on this space. The positive and negative parts of the real and imaginary parts are measurable by [F8]. Consequently $|h|$ and each of those four nonnegative functions remain measurable after multiplication by the indicator of any Lebesgue measurable subset of $\mathfrak H$. Their differences give the measurable real and imaginary parts of the complex restriction as well. [F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, F9, F12, F14, step 1.2]

3.1 Apply change of variables using the diffeomorphisms in step 1.2 and the measurable absolute integrand in step 2.2. Then the absolute integral on $\sigma_i D$ is the integral on $D$ of $|(f|_2\sigma_i)\overline{(g|_2\sigma_i)}|$. The slash transforms are holomorphic by [F1], so the continuity argument of step 2.2 applies to the modulus of their product with one factor conjugated. By [F1], $z\mapsto(f|_2\sigma_i)(Nz)$ and $z\mapsto(g|_2\sigma_i)(Nz)$ are holomorphic and have period one. They tend uniformly to zero as the imaginary part tends to infinity. Apply the period-one expansion theorem to these two rescaled functions and undo the scaling. The product is then bounded by $C e^{-cy}$ for $y\ge Y$, for some $C,c>0$ and $Y\ge2$. The Borel set $K=D\cap\{x+iy:y<Y\}$ lies in a bounded rectangle and has finite area. Its closure is compact and lies in $\mathfrak H$, since $y\ge\sqrt{3}/2$ there. Hence the modulus has a finite bound $A\ge0$ on $K$ by [F9]. The function $A\mathbf{1}_K$ is a nonnegative simple measurable majorant of the modulus restricted to $K$, with range contained in $\{0,A\}$. By [F8] its integral is $A\lambda_2(K)<\infty$. Each strip $B_k=\{x+iy:|x|<1/2,\ Y+k\le y<Y+k+1\}$, for $k\ge0$, has area one because adding the omitted vertical edge gives a half-open unit rectangle and that edge is null. The majorant $Ce^{-c(Y+k)}\mathbf{1}_{B_k}$ is nonnegative simple measurable by [F8], with range contained in $\{0,Ce^{-c(Y+k)}\}$. It bounds the modulus restricted to $D\cap B_k$ and has integral $Ce^{-c(Y+k)}$ by the unit area of $B_k$. Countable additivity bounds the tail by the convergent sum of these bounds. Thus the integral of $|h|$ on the finite union $F_0$ is finite. [F1, F6, F8, F9, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2]

3.2 For the source comparison, take $\Gamma_1(N)$ from [F13], put $P=\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and let $\overline{\Gamma_1}$ and $\overline{\Gamma_0}$ be the images in $P$ of $\Gamma_1(N)$ and $\Gamma_0(N)$. The defining congruences give $\Gamma_1(N)\subseteq\Gamma_0(N)$. If $\gamma\in\Gamma_1(N)$ then $\epsilon(d_\gamma)=\epsilon(1)=1$ by the character definition in [F1], so the fixed-character forms have trivial slash action on $\Gamma_1(N)$. They still vanish at all cusps since the decay condition in [F1] holds for every determinant-one cusp representative. Two integral determinant-one matrices congruent modulo $N$ are in the same right coset of $\Gamma_1(N)$ by [F5], since their quotient in the order used in step 2.1 is the identity modulo $N$ and therefore belongs to $\Gamma_1(N)$. For either subgroup, projection onto $P$ sends its right cosets onto the right cosets of its projective image. Both projective indices in $P$ are therefore finite by [F5]. The right cosets of $\overline{\Gamma_1}$ in $\overline{\Gamma_0}$ are among its right cosets in $P$. Let $r=[\overline{\Gamma_0}:\overline{\Gamma_1}]$ be their number by [F5] and choose lifts $\tau_j\in\Gamma_0(N)$ of their representatives. Write $X$ for the invariant conull set of step 2.1, on which the projective action is free. The union of $\tau_j(F_0\cap X)$ is a measurable fundamental region for $\Gamma_1(N)$ on $X$. Indeed, each $\overline{\Gamma_0}$-orbit meets $F_0\cap X$ once. For that representative $z$, the points $\tau_j z$ cover the $\overline{\Gamma_1}$-orbits inside its $\overline{\Gamma_0}$-orbit by the coset decomposition, and two such translates can be $\overline{\Gamma_1}$-equivalent only if their right cosets coincide, by freeness at $z$. [F1, F3, F5, F6, F13, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $E$ be another measurable fundamental region and use the trace measure $\lambda_{\mathfrak H}$ from step 2.2 for the integrals. Work on the intersection of the invariant conull sets for $F_0$ and $E$, whose complement in the half-plane is the union of two null sets. By [F6], the discarded sets are measurable and null. By [F8], the integrals of $|h|$ and the four nonnegative parts from step 2.2 over either discarded set are zero. Enumerate the elements $\gamma_i$ of $\Gamma$ by increasing maximum absolute matrix entry and order each finite group of ties lexicographically. Partition the conull part of $F_0$ according to the first $\gamma$ in this enumeration sending a point into $E$. Write $X_*$ for the common conull set. Each set $A_i=F_0\cap X_*\cap\gamma_i^{-1}E$ is measurable by [F12] and [F6], since $F_0$, $X_*$ and $E$ are measurable and the inverse modular map is a diffeomorphism by step 1.2. Remove the finitely many earlier sets $A_j$ from $A_i$ in succession. The first-hit piece $A_i\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}A_j$ is therefore measurable by the set-difference rule in [F12]. Its image under $\gamma_i$ is measurable by [F6]. The images partition the conull part of $E$: they cover it by orbit coverage, and an intersection would give the same representative in $F_0$, which belongs to only one piece. Applying change of variables on $\mathfrak H$ with the indicator of each image and the density identity from step 2.2 equates the absolute integrals on paired pieces. Countable additivity of the nonnegative integral now gives the same finite absolute integral on $E$ and $F_0$. The same change of variables applies to the positive and negative parts of $\operatorname{Re}h$ and $\operatorname{Im}h$, since the Jacobian is positive. The four integrals on each piece are finite, so recombination equates the complex integrals on paired pieces. The absolute integral over $E\cup F_0$ is bounded by the sum of the two absolute integrals since the indefinite integral of $|h|$ is a measure. Thus $h$ restricted to $E\cup F_0$ is integrable. Countable additivity of its integral, including the discarded null pieces with zero contribution, gives equality of the complex integrals on $E$ and $F_0$. [F6, F8, F12, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Step 2.2 gives $h(\tau_j z)J_{\tau_j}(z)=h(z)$, since every $\tau_j$ belongs to $\Gamma_0(N)$. Applying the same change-of-variables argument as in step 4.1 to these finitely many disjoint translates shows that the integral on the $\Gamma_1(N)$ region equals $r$ times the integral on $F_0$. The source factor $y^k/y^2$ is one at $k=2$. Each right coset of $\overline{\Gamma_0}$ in $P$ splits into $r$ right cosets of $\overline{\Gamma_1}$ by right multiplication of its subgroup decomposition, so [F5] gives $[P:\overline{\Gamma_1}]=r[P:\overline{\Gamma_0}]$. The normalized Petersson product of [F13] on the fixed-character forms is therefore $[P:\overline{\Gamma_0}]^{-1}\langle f,g\rangle_N$. This factor is positive, so the vanishing conditions agree. [F5, F8, F13, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 3.2]

5.2 The complex integral is linear in $f$. Conjugation of the integral conjugates the integrand by the real and imaginary part formula, so the pairing is Hermitian. For $f\ne0$, the nonzero locus is the nonempty preimage of the open set $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ under $f$. It is open by [F14] and the continuity from [F9]. It therefore intersects the invariant conull set for $F_0$. Take a $\Gamma$-translate of such a point in $F_0$. The transformation law keeps $f$ nonzero there. Since $F_0$ is open and $|f|^2$ is continuous by step 2.2, $|f|^2$ is bounded below by some $b>0$ on a small half-open rectangle $B$ of positive area inside $F_0$. By [F8], $b\mathbf{1}_B$ is nonnegative simple measurable with range contained in $\{0,b\}$. Monotonicity and the indicator integral in [F8] give $\int_{F_0}|f|^2\,dx\,dy\ge b\lambda_2(B)>0$. Thus the pairing is a complex inner product. The old span $W$ is a subspace by step 1.1, so [F10] applies to the displayed vanishing condition. For each $g\in W$ that condition is preserved by linear combinations in the first argument, and it holds for $f=0$. Hence the new subspace is a linear subspace. [F1, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F14, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 4.1]

6.1 Each summand in the Hecke formula is a constant multiple of the weight-two slash transform by $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&d\end{pmatrix}$, whose determinant is $ad>0$. The slash transforms are holomorphic by [F1], so by [F9] they are complex differentiable at every point of $\mathfrak H$. The derivative rules in [F7] give pointwise complex differentiability for scalar multiples, sums of two such functions and the zero function. These functions are holomorphic by [F9]. The zero function is the identity for pointwise addition, which is associative and commutative. Thus the holomorphic functions form the additive commutative monoid required by [F4]. Repeated addition yields a holomorphic finite Hecke sum, and the scalar equality is well defined as equality of functions there. The expansion at infinity in the cusp-space definition makes the coefficient-one condition unambiguous. By [F11], $\epsilon$ is induced by a primitive character $\chi$ whose modulus divides $N$ and equals the conductor of $\epsilon$. The matrix $-I$ lies in $\Gamma_0(N)$ and acts trivially under the weight-two slash formula, since its fractional linear action is the identity and its slash factor is $(-1)^{-2}=1$. For a nonzero $f\in S_2(N,\epsilon)$, the transformation law applied to $-I$ gives $f=\epsilon(-1)f$, hence $\epsilon(-1)=1$. The integer $-1$ is a unit modulo $N$, so $\chi(-1)=\epsilon(-1)=1$ by [F2]. Thus the parity hypothesis in [F11] holds for the source formula. On the terms with $\gcd(a,N)=1$, [F2] gives $\epsilon(a)=\chi(a)$ and $a^2/m=m/d^2$ since $ad=m$. Terms with $\gcd(a,N)>1$ have $\epsilon(a)=0$ by the definition of the level character, so including them gives the formula in the Definition. For $f=0$, the displayed sum is zero for any level character. Whenever the cusp space contains a nonzero form, the displayed operators therefore agree with the source operators at every positive integer $m$. The common eigenform condition uses all those indices. [F1, F2, F4, F7, F9, F11] ∎

## Source notes

Best et al., *Computing classical modular forms*, §4.1 gives the normalized cuspidal eigenform convention. Section 6.1 uses the scaled lower-level forms in a basis and gives the classical Hecke formula. In weight two its prefactor on the positive factorization $ad=m$ is $a^2/m=m/d^2$. Extending the level character by zero on nonunits accounts for the restriction $\gcd(a,N)=1$ in that source. The construction of the pairing and the admissibility of the lower-level images are proved above.

Diamond and Im, *Modular forms and modular curves*, §6.1 gives the fixed-character old space as the span of all admissible proper-level cusp images and the new space as its Petersson orthogonal complement. The conductor restriction agrees with requiring $\epsilon_M$ to induce $\epsilon$. If $\epsilon_M$ induces $\epsilon$, its primitive ancestor also induces $\epsilon$ by composition of reductions on units and zero extension at level $N$. Conversely, if the conductor of $\epsilon$ divides $M$, inducing its primitive ancestor to $M$ gives such an $\epsilon_M$. At weight two the source map is $g(z)\mapsto t\,g(tz)$, so its image agrees with that of the unscaled map.

Steps 3.2 and 5.1 count the translates using the right cosets of the projective images and compare the resulting integral with the normalization in §3.6, Eq. (3.6.1).
