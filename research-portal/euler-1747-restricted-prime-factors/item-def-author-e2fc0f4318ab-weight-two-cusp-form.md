## Definition

Let $N$ be a positive integer and let $\epsilon$ be a Dirichlet character modulo $N$, a homomorphism from the invertible residue classes modulo $N$ to $\mathbb C^\times$ extended by zero on nonunits. Write $\mathfrak H=\{z\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}$ and $\Gamma_0(N)=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):N\mid c_\gamma\}$, where $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ consists of integral matrices of determinant one and $c_\gamma,d_\gamma$ are their lower left and lower right entries. For a real matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $\det(A)=ad-bc>0$, use $Az=(az+b)/(cz+d)$ and the weight-two action $(f|_2 A)(z)=\det(A)(cz+d)^{-2}f(Az)$ on $\mathfrak H$.

For the boundary $\mathbb Q\cup\{\infty\}$, write $[r:s]$ for the class of a nonzero rational pair under multiplication by nonzero rational scalars. Identify $[r:s]$ with $r/s$ if $s\ne0$, and $[1:0]$ with $\infty$. For a rational matrix $A$ of positive determinant use $A[r:s]=[ar+bs:cr+ds]$. A cusp of $\Gamma_0(N)$ is an orbit $\{\gamma[r:s]:\gamma\in\Gamma_0(N)\}$ for this action.

The space $S_2(N,\epsilon)$ consists of holomorphic functions $f$ on $\mathfrak H$ satisfying $f|_2\gamma=\epsilon(d_\gamma)f$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(N)$ and vanishing at all cusps. The vanishing condition is that for every $\sigma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and every $\eta>0$, there is a real $Y$ such that $|(f|_2\sigma)(x+iy)|<\eta$ for all real $x$ and $y>Y$. At infinity write $q=\exp(2\pi i z)$ and $f(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_n q^n$. The $a_n$ are its Fourier coefficients at infinity.

## Justification

Use the order-preserving embeddings $\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Q\hookrightarrow\mathbb R$ ([math:lem-int-embeds-rat](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-int-embeds-rat), [math:lem-rat-embeds-dense](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-rat-embeds-dense)). They preserve $0,1$, sums and products, so integral matrix products and determinants agree with their values in either larger field, and $N$ has positive real image.

For a rational matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ with $\Delta=ad-bc\ne0$, set $J=\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}$. Row-by-column multiplication gives $AJ=JA=\begin{pmatrix}ad-bc&0\\0&ad-bc\end{pmatrix}=\Delta I$ ([math:def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix)). Since $\mathbb Q$ is a field, $\Delta^{-1}$ is rational ([math:thm-rat-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-rat-field)). Pulling this scalar through the products gives $A(\Delta^{-1}J)=(\Delta^{-1}J)A=I$ ([math:thm-matrix-multiplication-laws](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-matrix-multiplication-laws)). Thus $A^{-1}=\Delta^{-1}J$. For an integral matrix of determinant one the inverse is the integral matrix $J$.

The identity matrix lies in $\Gamma_0(N)$ since its lower left entry is zero. For $\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma_0(N)$, the lower left entry of $\gamma\delta$ is $c_\gamma a_\delta+d_\gamma c_\delta$ and that of $\gamma^{-1}$ is $-c_\gamma$ by the inverse formula. Both are divisible by $N$ by closure under integer linear combinations and negation ([math:lem-divisibility-basic](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-divisibility-basic)). These are the three subgroup conditions for $\Gamma_0(N)\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ ([math:def-subgroup](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-subgroup), [math:def-modular-group-action-on-the-upper-half-plane](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-modular-group-action-on-the-upper-half-plane)).

Write $[u]_N$ for the class of $u$ modulo $N$. Two classes are equal exactly when $u\equiv v\pmod N$, meaning $N\mid(u-v)$ ([math:def-integers-modulo-n](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-integers-modulo-n), [math:def-congruence-modulo-an-integer](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-congruence-modulo-an-integer)). Multiplication of classes is $[u]_N[v]_N=[uv]_N$ ([math:def-addition-and-multiplication-modulo-n](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-addition-and-multiplication-modulo-n)). Since $a_\gamma d_\gamma-b_\gamma c_\gamma=1$ and $N\mid c_\gamma$, this gives $[a_\gamma]_N[d_\gamma]_N=[1]_N$. Thus $[d_\gamma]_N$ is a unit with inverse $[a_\gamma]_N$ ([math:def-unit-group-modulo-n-and-euler-totient](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-unit-group-modulo-n-and-euler-totient)). Because $N\ge1$, the class $[u]_N$ is a unit exactly when $\gcd(u,N)=1$ ([math:thm-unit-criterion-modulo-n](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-unit-criterion-modulo-n)). The extension by zero on nonunits therefore gives the arithmetic function of [math:def-dirichlet-character-modulo-q](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-dirichlet-character-modulo-q). The lower right entry of a product, $c_\gamma b_\delta+d_\gamma d_\delta$, gives $[d_{\gamma\delta}]_N=[d_\gamma]_N[d_\delta]_N$ by the same congruence arithmetic ([math:lem-congruence-respects-integer-arithmetic](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-congruence-respects-integer-arithmetic)). Thus $\gamma\mapsto[d_\gamma]_N$ is a group homomorphism to the units, and composing with $\epsilon$ gives the character $\gamma\mapsto\epsilon(d_\gamma)$ of the subgroup ([math:def-group-homomorphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-group-homomorphism), [math:def-dirichlet-character-modulo-q](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-dirichlet-character-modulo-q)). In particular $\epsilon(1)=1$ because $\epsilon(1)=\epsilon(1)^2$ and $\epsilon(1)\ne0$.

The denominator $cz+d$ for a real matrix $A$ of positive determinant is nonzero on $\mathfrak H$. If $c\ne0$, its only zero is the real number $-d/c$. If $c=0$, then $ad>0$ gives $d\ne0$. These facts use the field operations and order on $\mathbb R$ ([math:thm-reals-ordered-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-reals-ordered-field)). The nonzero denominator therefore defines $(cz+d)^{-2}$ by the complex integer power rule ([math:def-complex-integer-powers](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-complex-integer-powers)). For $z=x+iy$, multiplying by the conjugate of the denominator gives $\operatorname{Im}((az+b)\overline{(cz+d)})=(ad-bc)y$. Thus $\operatorname{Im}(Az)=\det(A)\operatorname{Im}z/|cz+d|^2>0$, as in the determinant-one calculation of [math:def-modular-group-action-on-the-upper-half-plane](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-modular-group-action-on-the-upper-half-plane). The two permutations in the determinant formula give $\det(A)=ad-bc$ ([math:def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix)). Write $j(A,z)=cz+d$. Row-by-column multiplication ([math:def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix)) gives $A(Bz)=(AB)z$ and $j(AB,z)=j(A,Bz)j(B,z)$. Determinant multiplicativity gives $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$ ([math:thm-determinant-multiplicative](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-determinant-multiplicative)), which is positive by the real field order ([math:thm-reals-ordered-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-reals-ordered-field)). Substitution in the slash formula gives $(f|_2 A)|_2 B=f|_2(AB)$, and the identity matrix acts as the identity on functions.

For $z\in\mathfrak H$ set $r=\operatorname{Im}z>0$. The coordinate formula for the complex metric gives $|\operatorname{Im}(w-z)|\le |w-z|=d_{\mathbb C}(z,w)$ ([math:def-complex-metric-convergence-and-continuity](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-complex-metric-convergence-and-continuity)). Every $w$ in the open ball $B(z,r)$ satisfies $d_{\mathbb C}(z,w)<r$ ([math:def-metric-ball](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-metric-ball)), so $\operatorname{Im}w\ge r-|\operatorname{Im}(w-z)|>0$. Thus $B(z,r)\subseteq\mathfrak H$, which is open by the metric definition ([math:def-metric-topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-metric-topology)).

The affine functions $az+b$ and $cz+d$ are holomorphic by the constant, identity and linearity rules. The same derivative rules for quotients and products give holomorphy of $z\mapsto Az$ and $z\mapsto\det(A)(cz+d)^{-2}$ on $\mathfrak H$, where the denominator is nonzero ([math:thm-algebra-of-complex-derivatives](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-algebra-of-complex-derivatives)). Since $A$ maps the open set $\mathfrak H$ into itself, the chain rule makes $f(Az)$ holomorphic there for holomorphic $f$ ([math:thm-chain-rule-for-complex-derivatives](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-chain-rule-for-complex-derivatives)). The product rule now gives holomorphy of $f|_2 A$ on $\mathfrak H$.

The homogeneous-coordinate formula agrees with that in [math:def-real-projective-line-and-its-sl2-action](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-real-projective-line-and-its-sl2-action). For a rational $A$ of positive determinant, the inverse computed above has rational entries. If $A$ sent a nonzero rational column to zero, multiplication by $A^{-1}$ would give that the original column was zero. Rescaling the column rescales its image by the same factor. The positive determinant condition is preserved by products and inverses because $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$ and $\det(A^{-1})=1/\det(A)$ ([math:thm-determinant-multiplicative](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-determinant-multiplicative)). The identity fixes every class, and associativity of matrix multiplication gives $A(B[r:s])=(AB)[r:s]$ ([math:thm-matrix-multiplication-laws](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-matrix-multiplication-laws)). This gives a left action on the rational classes ([math:def-group-action](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-group-action)) and hence the stated cusp orbits on restriction to $\Gamma_0(N)$. For a finite boundary point $x\in\mathbb Q$, choose integers $p,q$ with $q\ne0$ and $x=p/q$ ([math:def-rationals](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-rationals)), so $[p:q]$ represents $x$. Since $(p,q)\ne(0,0)$, the gcd $g=\gcd(p,q)$ is positive ([math:def-common-divisor-and-gcd](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-common-divisor-and-gcd)). The pair $(r,s)=(p/g,q/g)$ is integral and coprime by [math:cor-gcd-quotients-coprime](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-gcd-quotients-coprime). Rescaling by $1/g$ gives $[r:s]=[p:q]$. Bézout's identity gives integers $u,v$ with $rv-su=1$ ([math:thm-bezout-identity](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-bezout-identity)), so $\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}r&u\\s&v\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$ and $\sigma\infty=[r:s]=x$. The identity represents $\infty$. Thus every boundary point has a representative of the form $\sigma\infty$.

Put $U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, an element of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$. By the group-power definition, the natural powers are the unique sequence starting at $I$ with each next term obtained by right multiplication by $U$ ([math:def-group-power](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-group-power)). The matrices $\begin{pmatrix}1&m\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ for $m\ge0$ satisfy this recursion because the first is $I$ and right multiplication by $U$ increases the upper right entry by one ([math:def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix)). For $m<0$, the group-power definition gives $U^m=(U^{-m})^{-1}$, whose upper right entry is $m$ by the determinant-one inverse formula above. Hence $U^m=\begin{pmatrix}1&m\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ for all integers $m$.

If $\sigma'\infty=\gamma\sigma\infty$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(N)$, the integral determinant-one matrix $(\gamma\sigma)^{-1}\sigma'$ fixes $\infty$. Its lower left entry is zero. Its integral diagonal entries $a,d$ satisfy $ad=1$, so each divides $1$. Each is $1$ or $-1$ by [math:lem-units-of-z](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-units-of-z), and their product forces both to have the same value $s\in\{1,-1\}$. With upper right entry $b$, it equals $sU^{sb}$. It acts by $z\mapsto z+sb$ with weight factor one. The slash composition law and transformation condition give $(f|_2\sigma')(z)=\epsilon(d_\gamma)(f|_2\sigma)(z+sb)$. Multiplication by the fixed nonzero scalar and translation of real $x$ preserve uniform vanishing. This proves independence of the representative of the cusp.

At $m=N$ the power formula gives $U^N=\begin{pmatrix}1&N\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. The congruence $U^N\equiv I\pmod N$ is entrywise and is preserved under products by congruence arithmetic. Thus $\gamma=\sigma U^N\sigma^{-1}$ is in $\Gamma_0(N)$ with $[d_\gamma]_N=[1]_N$. Since $\sigma U^N=\gamma\sigma$ and $\epsilon(d_\gamma)=1$, the slash composition law yields $(f|_2\sigma)|_2 U^N=f|_2\sigma$. The matrix $U^N$ acts by translation by $N$ with weight factor one.

Consequently $h(z)=(f|_2\sigma)(Nz)$ has period one. It is holomorphic by the chain rule because $z\mapsto Nz$ is holomorphic and maps $\mathfrak H$ into itself for $N>0$. The quantified vanishing condition gives a bound $|h(z)|<1$ on a whole upper half-plane by taking $\eta=1$ and $\operatorname{Im}z>Y/N$. Using all $\eta>0$ gives zero as the uniform limit there as $\operatorname{Im}z\to\infty$. By [math:thm-q-expansion-principle-at-the-cusp](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-q-expansion-principle-at-the-cusp), the descended holomorphic function of $\exp(2\pi i z)$ extends to zero with value zero. Conversely, an extension with value zero gives uniform vanishing by the limit assertion of that theorem. Undoing the rescaling gives the expansion of $f|_2\sigma$ in $\exp(2\pi i z/N)$ with zero constant coefficient, and gives exponential decay as $\operatorname{Im}z\to\infty$. For $\sigma=I$ we can instead use period one, since $U\in\Gamma_0(N)$ has lower right entry $1$. The theorem then gives the unique expansion in $q$ and the coefficients of the definition. These expansions use complex power series ([math:def-complex-series-power-series-and-absolute-convergence](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-complex-series-power-series-and-absolute-convergence)), complex integer powers ([math:def-complex-integer-powers](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-complex-integer-powers)) and the constant $\pi$ ([math:def-pi-via-first-positive-cosine-zero](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-pi-via-first-positive-cosine-zero)).

The zero function satisfies the conditions. For $f,g\in S_2(N,\epsilon)$ and $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C$, the pointwise combination $\alpha f+\beta g$ is holomorphic by linearity of the derivative. The slash formula is linear in the function, so the transformation law persists. For fixed $\sigma$ and $\eta>0$, take a common tail on which both transforms have modulus below $\eta/(1+|\alpha|+|\beta|)$. The triangle inequality and multiplicativity of modulus ([math:lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws)) bound the modulus of the transformed combination by $\eta$. Thus $S_2(N,\epsilon)$ is a complex vector space with pointwise sum and scalar multiplication, with the vector space axioms inherited pointwise from the field operations on $\mathbb C$ ([math:def-vector-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-vector-space), [math:thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field)).
