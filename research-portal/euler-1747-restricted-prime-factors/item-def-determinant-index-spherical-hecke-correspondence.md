## Definition

Assume countable choice, meaning that every sequence of nonempty sets has a choice function. Let $\ell$ be a prime, an integer greater than one with positive divisors only $1$ and itself. Let $F=\mathbb Q_\ell$ be the field obtained by completing $\mathbb Q$ for the $\ell$-adic distance and extending its field operations. Its valuation $v_\ell$ has $v_\ell(\ell)=1$, satisfies $|a|_\ell=\ell^{-v_\ell(a)}$ for $a\ne0$, and has $v_\ell(0)=+\infty$. Put $O=\mathbb Z_\ell=\{a\in F:v_\ell(a)\ge0\}$.

Write $G=\mathrm{PGL}_2(F)$ for the group of invertible two-by-two matrices over $F$ modulo nonzero scalar matrices. The projection $q$ sends a matrix $A$ to its class $[A]$, and $[A][B]=[AB]$. Give $F$ its $\ell$-adic metric topology and $F^4$ the product topology. Give the invertible matrices the subspace topology under their identification with a subset of $F^4$ by their entries. Give $G$ the quotient topology, in which a subset is open exactly when its inverse image under $q$ is open. Let $H=\mathrm{GL}_2(O)$ be the group of matrices with inverses over $O$, and set $K=q(H)$. The group $G$ is Hausdorff, and $K$ is compact and open. Here Hausdorff means distinct points have disjoint open neighborhoods, a topological group has continuous multiplication and inversion, and a compact set admits a finite subcover from each open cover.

For a nonnegative integer $e$, let
$$D_{\ell,e}=\{A\in M_2(O):\det A\ne0,\ v_\ell(\det A)=e\},\qquad S_{\ell,e}=q(D_{\ell,e}).$$
Here $M_2(O)$ is the set of two-by-two matrices over $O$ and $\det\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{pmatrix}=ad-bc$. Thus a projective class lies in $S_{\ell,e}$ when it has an integral matrix representative with determinant of valuation $e$. The **determinant-index spherical Hecke correspondence of index $\ell^e$** is the spherical operator of the indicator $\mathbf 1_{S_{\ell,e}}$, which is one on $S_{\ell,e}$ and zero elsewhere.

More generally a spherical Hecke operator here is the right convolution action of a complex weight $\phi$ that vanishes outside a compact subset and is invariant under $K$ on both sides, meaning $\phi(khk')=\phi(h)$ for $k,k'\in K$. A right action of $G$ on a set $X$ is a map $(x,h)\mapsto xh$ satisfying $x1=x$ and $(xh)j=x(hj)$. Use the space $\mathbb C^X$ of functions $X\to\mathbb C$ with addition $(f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x)$, scalar multiplication $(af)(x)=af(x)$ and zero the constant zero function. Right translation is the complex-linear map on $\mathbb C^X$ given by $(R_h f)(x)=f(xh)$. A complex-linear map preserves addition and complex scalar multiplication. On the subspace of functions satisfying $f(xk)=f(x)$ for every $k\in K$, the action is
$$(T_\phi f)(x)=\sum_{hK\in G/K}\phi(h)f(xh).$$
Here $hK=\{hk:k\in K\}$ is the coset formed by multiplying on the right by $K$, and $G/K$ denotes the set of these cosets. The cosets partition $G$, and the sum ranges over the finitely many cosets where $\phi$ is nonzero. In particular
$$(T_{\ell,e}f)(x)=\sum_{hK\in S_{\ell,e}/K} f(xh),$$
where $S_{\ell,e}/K$ denotes the cosets contained in $S_{\ell,e}$.

In the measure formulation use a left Haar measure $\mu$, a nonzero regular Borel measure invariant under left translation and finite on compact sets. Here Borel means defined on the smallest collection containing all open sets and closed under complements and countable unions, and regularity means outer approximation of Borel sets by open sets and inner approximation of open sets by compact sets. Normalize by $\mu(K)=1$. Such a measure is available under the additional assumption of the axiom of choice, meaning that any family of nonempty sets has a choice function. For this integral the measurable domain is $(G,\mathcal B(G))$ and the measure space is $(G,\mathcal B(G),\mu)$, where $\mathcal B(G)$ is the Borel sigma-algebra of $G$. Equip $\mathbb C$ with the Borel sigma-algebra generated by the topology of the Euclidean metric $d_{\mathbb C}(z,w)=|z-w|$. Equip the real and imaginary components' codomain $\mathbb R$ with the Borel sigma-algebra of its usual metric $d_{\mathbb R}(a,b)=|a-b|$. View the nonnegative functions used in the simple integral as taking values in $\overline{\mathbb R}$ with the Borel sigma-algebra of its order topology. With this normalization the convolution action is the complex Lebesgue integral of $h\mapsto\phi(h)f(xh)$ over $G$ against $\mu$, for each fixed $x\in X$.

For a finite list of distinct primes $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n$ and nonnegative integer exponents $e_i$, use the product group $P=\prod_{i=1}^n G_i$ of the corresponding groups $G_i$. Its elements are tuples and products are taken coordinate by coordinate. Let $\iota_i(h)$ be the tuple with $h$ in coordinate $i$ and identities elsewhere. On any set $X$ with a right action of $P$, apply the local operators along $\iota_i$ to functions invariant on the right under $\prod_i K_i$. These operators commute, and their product is
$$\left(\prod_{i=1}^n T_{\ell_i,e_i}f\right)(x)=\sum_{(h_iK_i)_i\in\prod_i(S_{\ell_i,e_i}/K_i)} f\bigl(x(h_1,\ldots,h_n)\bigr).$$
The Cartesian product in the sum consists of tuples with one coset from each local set. For the empty list the product operator is the identity.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Countable choice and the prime, local field and valuation have the meanings specified above. The completion has field operations extending those of $\mathbb Q$, and every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse. The valuation is additive on products of nonzero elements, and the units of $O$ are precisely the elements with valuation zero ([countable choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-countable-choice), [prime](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-prime), [adic completion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-field-of-p-adic-numbers), [field structure of the completion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-p-adic-completion-is-a-field), [normalized valuation](item-def-author-24fda87362bc-local-adic-valuation)).

[F2] Give $F$ the metric topology of the $\ell$-adic distance. The projective quotient map is a group homomorphism. The entry topology is the subspace topology from the product topology on $F^4$. With these topologies on the matrix group and its quotient, $G$ is a Hausdorff topological group and $K=q(H)$ is compact open, by the local compact-open result under countable choice. A Hausdorff space is locally compact when each point has a compact neighborhood ([projective group](item-def-degree-two-projective-linear-group), [metric topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-metric-topology), [product topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-product-topology), [subspace topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-subspace-topology-top), [quotient topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-quotient-topology), [topological group](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-topological-group), [compact-open integral subgroups](item-lem-author-d45da46285be-triangular-cosets), [locally compact space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-compact-space)).

[F3] Over a commutative ring the two-by-two determinant is $ad-bc$, the determinant is multiplicative, and a square matrix is invertible exactly when its determinant is a unit ([determinant](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix), [multiplicativity](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-determinant-multiplicative), [invertibility criterion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit)).

[F4] The fixed-exponent map $AH\mapsto[A]K$ is a bijection from the cosets in $D_{\ell,e}$ onto those in $S_{\ell,e}$. The former have one triangular representative with diagonal $(\ell^r,\ell^{e-r})$ and upper entry $b$ for every $0\le r\le e$ and $0\le b<\ell^r$. In particular the projective support is a disjoint union of finitely many cosets of the form $hK$ ([coset](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-coset), [fixed-index classification](item-lem-degree-two-spherical-index-cosets)).

[F5] For a Hausdorff topological group with compact open $K$, the spherical convolution algebra consists of compactly supported complex weights invariant on both sides by $K$. A representation, meaning an action by invertible complex-linear maps that respects products, carries the operator of such a weight on its $K$-fixed vectors by summing the weighted actions of the coset representatives ([spherical convolution and action](item-def-author-d45da46285be-spherical-hecke-algebra)).

[F6] A left Haar measure is invariant under left translation. Under the axiom of choice it exists on every locally compact Hausdorff group. Its mass on a nonempty compact open subgroup is finite and strictly positive ([left Haar measure](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-left-haar-integral-and-left-haar-measure), [axiom of choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice), [Haar existence](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-existence-of-left-and-right-haar-measures), [positivity and finiteness](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-haar-measure-is-positive-on-nonempty-open-sets-and-finite-on-compact-sets)).

[F7] Finite sums are invariant under bijective reindexing. An iterated sum over finite sets equals the sum over their Cartesian product. The group laws are associativity and the identity and inverse laws. Finite sums of complex numbers use associative, commutative addition and are independent of enumeration. A finite Cartesian product of finite sets is finite, including the singleton product of the empty list ([group laws](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-group), [finite sums](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-finite-sum-in-a-commutative-monoid), [finite sum reindexing and product sums](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-finite-sum-reindexing-and-fubini), [finite products of sets](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-product-rule)).

[F8] The Euclidean metric $d_{\mathbb C}(z,w)=|z-w|$ on $\mathbb C$ induces its usual topology. The Borel sigma-algebra on $\mathbb C$ is generated by the open sets of this topology. Open sets are Borel, and the Borel sets form a sigma-algebra. A measurable space is a set with a sigma-algebra, and a measure space is a measurable space equipped with a measure. For the real and imaginary components use the Borel sigma-algebra of the usual metric on $\mathbb R$. For nonnegative simple functions use the Borel sigma-algebra of the order topology on $\overline{\mathbb R}$, the topology generated by all its strict lower and upper rays. A function is measurable when preimages of measurable sets are measurable. On a measure space, a measurable function with finite range in $[0,+\infty)$ viewed in $\overline{\mathbb R}$ is nonnegative simple. The integral of a nonnegative function constant on finitely many disjoint measurable sets and zero elsewhere is the sum of its constant values multiplied by the respective measures. On nonnegative simple measurable functions the Lebesgue integral agrees with this formula. A complex measurable function is integrable when its modulus has finite integral. Each real integral is the difference of the integrals of the positive and negative parts when both are finite. ## Justification

**Given:** The countable choice assumption, prime, local groups, exponents and right actions in the definition. For the existence of the measure in the measure formulation, also assume the axiom of choice.

**Goal:** Verify the spherical support, the well-defined operator and unit weights, and the product correspondence.

1.1 A matrix in $D_{\ell,e}$ is invertible over $F$ by the determinant criterion because its nonzero determinant is a unit in the field $F$. For $U,V\in H$ the determinants have valuation zero. The product $UAV$ is integral and its determinant still has valuation $e$, by multiplicativity and additivity of the valuation. The same property for $U^{-1},V^{-1}\in H$ gives equality of the translated supports $kS_{\ell,e}k'=S_{\ell,e}$ for $k,k'\in K$. This construction includes every integral matrix of determinant valuation $e$, including those whose entries are all divisible by $\ell$ when they occur. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 By the fixed-exponent bijection the support is a finite union of distinct cosets $hK$. Left translation by $h$ is continuous by [F11], and $hK$ is open as the preimage of $K$ under the continuous left translation by $h^{-1}$. Each coset is compact as a continuous image of $K$. By the union axiom of the quotient topology, $S_{\ell,e}$ is open. For compactness, fix an ambient open cover of the union and induct on the number of cosets, using [F13]. The empty list of cover members covers the union of zero cosets. If finitely many cover members cover the first cosets, apply [F11] to obtain a finite subcover of the next compact coset by members of the same ambient cover. Concatenating the two finite lists gives a finite cover of the enlarged union. Thus the full union has a finite subcover and is compact by the ambient criterion. Thus the indicator is a compactly supported weight invariant on both sides by $K$. The same bijection allows the coset sum for this indicator to be taken over $AH$ in $D_{\ell,e}$, with each triangular representative contributing exactly once through its projective class. [F2, F4, F5, F11, F13, F14, step 1.1]

3.1 The operations on $\mathbb C^X$ satisfy the vector space axioms by evaluation at each $x\in X$, with additive inverse $x\mapsto-f(x)$. For complex scalars $a,b$, right translation satisfies $R_h(af+bg)(x)=af(xh)+bg(xh)$, so it is complex-linear. The action laws give $R_1f=f$ and $R_hR_jf(x)=f((xh)j)=f(x(hj))$, so $R_h$ has inverse $R_{h^{-1}}$ and these operators constitute a representation. The $K$-fixed functions contain zero, and for fixed $f,g$ and every $k\in K$, linearity gives $R_k(af+bg)=af+bg$. Thus the fixed functions form the stated right $K$-invariant subspace. The spherical action formula applies by [F5]. The open cosets partition $G$ and therefore cover the compact set outside which $\phi$ vanishes. Let $\mathcal C$ be the finite subcover supplied by the ambient open-cover criterion in [F11]. If $\phi(h)\ne0$, then $h$ belongs to the compact set and therefore lies in a coset in $\mathcal C$. That coset is $hK$ by the coset partition, so the contributing cosets form a subset of $\mathcal C$ and are finite. Replacing $h$ by $hk$ leaves both $\phi(h)$ and $f(xh)$ unchanged. To evaluate at $xk_0$ with $k_0\in K$, reindex by $hK\mapsto k_0hK$, a bijection with inverse multiplication by $k_0^{-1}$. The coefficient is unchanged by left invariance, so the output is right $K$-invariant. 4.1 The translates of $K$ give compact neighborhoods of all points, so $G$ is locally compact and Hausdorff. Under the additional choice assumption [F6] gives a left Haar measure $\mu_0$ with $0<\mu_0(K)<\infty$. Set $c=1/\mu_0(K)$ and $\mu(E)=c\mu_0(E)$ for every Borel set $E$. By [F18], $c$ is positive and finite, and multiplication by $c$ and by its reciprocal $1/c$ preserves order on the finite nonnegative reals. Both multiplications fix $+\infty$ by [F17], so multiplication by $c$ is an order isomorphism of $[0,+\infty]$ and carries greatest lower bounds and least upper bounds to those of the scaled sets. Multiplication by $c$ distributes over addition of nonnegative extended reals by real distributivity for finite terms and the rules for $+\infty$ otherwise. By induction it scales each finite partial sum by $c$, and taking the supremum gives the same scaling for the series. Thus $\mu(\varnothing)=0$, and countable additivity of $\mu_0$ gives countable additivity of $\mu$. Applying the same scaling to the infimum of the masses of open supersets of a Borel set and the supremum of the masses of compact subsets of an open set gives the two regularity identities for $\mu$. The scaled measure is left invariant and finite on compact sets because $\mu_0$ has those properties and $c$ is positive and finite. Since $\mu(K)=c\mu_0(K)=1$, it is nonzero and is a normalized left Haar measure. For every such normalization $\mu(hK)=1$ by left invariance. The integrand is constant on each of the finitely many contributing cosets and zero elsewhere by step 3.1. These cosets are disjoint and open, hence Borel, and the complement of their union is also Borel. For every set $B$ in the Euclidean Borel sigma-algebra on $\mathbb C$, the preimage of $B$ is a union of pieces from this finite measurable partition, so the integrand is measurable. The real and imaginary components themselves, the modulus, and the positive and negative parts of both components are constant on the same pieces. For the real Borel codomain of the components and the extended-real Borel codomain of the nonnegative functions, the preimage of any Borel set is again a union of these measurable pieces. Thus the components are measurable, and the modulus and the four parts are nonnegative simple measurable functions by [F8]. Each of the five nonnegative functions is zero outside the contributing cosets, each of which has measure one. The simple formula and its agreement with the nonnegative Lebesgue integral in [F8] therefore give finite integrals. Taking the positive-minus-negative differences and recombining into the complex integral yields the finite coset sum with mass one per coset. 4.2 The product group laws hold in each coordinate, with coordinatewise identities and inverses. For distinct coordinates the embedded elements commute, since at every coordinate at least one element is the identity. Each local operator preserves invariance under its own $K_i$ by step 3.1. It preserves invariance under every other $K_j$ because a right shift by $\iota_j(k)$ can be moved past $\iota_i(h)$ inside each summand and then dropped by invariance of $f$. Since the embedded $K_j$ generate $\prod_j K_j$ by coordinatewise factorization, the common invariant space is preserved. Write $V$ for the functions in $\mathbb C^X$ invariant under the right action of $\prod_i K_i$. They form a subspace because zero and every linear combination of invariant functions are invariant at each point. For $1\le i\le n$, let $T_i$ denote $T_{\ell_i,e_i}$ acting along $\iota_i$, restricted to $V$. Step 3.1 gives linearity, and the invariance just proved makes each $T_i$ a linear self-map of $V$. The identity map is linear. The composition of two linear self-maps is linear by applying their linearity laws in succession. Composition is associative because either grouping of $A,B,C$ sends $f$ to $A(B(Cf))$, and composing with $\mathrm{id}_V$ on either side leaves a map unchanged. Thus these maps form a monoid under composition. For $0\le j\le n$, let $B_j$ be the product of the first $j$ maps from the list $T_1,\ldots,T_n$. By [F19], $B_0=\mathrm{id}_V$ and $B_{j+1}=B_j\circ T_{j+1}$ for $0\le j<n$. The operator product in the Definition is $B_n$. [F7, F9, F19, given, step 3.1]

5.1 Let $C_i=S_{\ell_i,e_i}/K_i$. For a tuple of cosets in the first $j$ positions write $u_j=(h_1,\ldots,h_j,1,\ldots,1)$ using their representatives in $P$.
