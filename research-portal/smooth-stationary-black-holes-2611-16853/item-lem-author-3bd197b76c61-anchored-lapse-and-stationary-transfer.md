## Statement

Work under the Axiom of Choice in the setting of the interior graph-geometry lemma, with its fixed compact sets $Q\subset Q_+^\circ\subset\mathbb R_t\times X$ and supplied stationary graphs $\Sigma_j=\{(u_j(x),x):x\in X\}$. Suppose the connected compact portion $L_j\subset\Sigma_j\cap Q$ contains an anchor $p_{*,j}$ where $N_j(p_{*,j})\ge a>0$, with common $a$. Let the positive lapse solve $\Delta_{h_j}N_j=V_jN_j$ with $0\le V_j=|k_j|^2+\operatorname{Ric}(g_j)(n_j,n_j)$ on the buffered chart neighborhoods. Then $N_j\ge c>0$ on $L_j$, with a common constant under the preceding geometric controls. Stationary projection has uniformly bounded inverse differential on $L_j$ and smooth local inverses on base balls of a common positive radius about its projected points. For every fixed compact base set $K\subset X$ whose entire lift $\Gamma_j(K)=\{(u_j(x),x):x\in K\}$ lies in $L_j$ for every j, uniform local temporal graph bounds transfer to uniform full $C^{m,\alpha}(K)$ stationary height bounds for every integer $m\ge0$ and $0<\alpha<1$, measured in fixed finite base charts. In particular the full $C^{4,\alpha}(K)$ norms are uniformly bounded. The zeroth-order height bound is supplied by containment in the fixed compact stationary tube, not by the lapse equation alone.

Here stationary projection is the product map $\pi(t,x)=x$, and the supplied smooth height $u_j:X\to\mathbb R$ parametrizes its graph by $x\mapsto(u_j(x),x)$. These are conventions in the general stationary product of the interior graph-geometry lemma.

For clarity, fix finitely many smooth base charts $\phi_\ell:U_\ell\to\mathbb R^3$ and relatively compact open sets $V_\ell$ with $\overline V_\ell\subset U_\ell$ whose union covers $K$. For a smooth function $u$ on a neighborhood of $K$, set $K_\ell=\phi_\ell(K\cap\overline V_\ell)$ and $v_\ell=u\circ\phi_\ell^{-1}$ on its open domain near $K_\ell$. The full compact-set quantity used here is

$$\|u\|_{C^{m,\alpha}(K)}:=\max_\ell\left(\sum_{r=0}^m\sup_{z\in K_\ell}\max_{|\beta|=r}|D^\beta v_\ell(z)|+\sum_{|\beta|=m}\sup_{\substack{z,w\in K_\ell\\z\ne w}}\frac{|D^\beta v_\ell(z)-D^\beta v_\ell(w)|}{|z-w|^\alpha}\right).$$

All empty suprema are zero; for $K=\varnothing$ the quantity is zero. Derivatives are those of the given ambient smooth function in the open chart domains, then restricted to $K_\ell$. This is a norm of those restricted derivative fields (the ambient $m$-jet), rather than a quantity determined by values on $K$ alone. The charts and smaller sets are fixed independently of j; constants may depend on them.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Uniform local graphs, metric and second-form bounds and a uniformly bounded connected finite cover are [lem-author-3bd197b76c61-interior-graph-geometry-and-finite-cover](item-lem-author-3bd197b76c61-interior-graph-geometry-and-finite-cover).

[F2] Multiplicative interior Harnack on a coordinate ball is [lem-author-58255dfd955f-local-potential-harnack](item-lem-author-58255dfd955f-local-potential-harnack). In dimension three, with $m=\sqrt{\det(h_{ab})}$, $A=(mh^{ab})$ and $q=mV$, its half-ball comparison constant depends only on $3$, $M/\lambda$ and $r^2Q/\lambda$, provided $\xi^TA\xi\ge\lambda|\xi|^2$, $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M$ and $0\le q\le Q$. It assumes a smooth metric, a nonnegative $C^2$ pointwise solution, a bounded nonnegative potential and ZFC, including [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice), already inherited from the setting of [F1]. It imposes no exterior, boundary-data or end-limit hypothesis. The finite-chain composition and insertion of the positive anchor are proved below.

[F3] The induced geometry and lapse use [def-spacelike-maximal-hypersurface-data](item-def-spacelike-maximal-hypersurface-data). The graph and projection are the explicit product maps in the Statement, in the general setting of [F1]. The open-chart derivative and Holder notation is [math:def-holder-spaces-c-k-alpha-and-their-scaled-norms](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-holder-spaces-c-k-alpha-and-their-scaled-norms); the full compact-set quantity is defined explicitly in the Statement by restricting these ambient derivative fields and their pairwise difference quotients to the fixed chart pieces.

[F4] The induced Laplacian is the positive-divergence operator $\Delta_{h_j}N_j=\operatorname{tr}_{h_j}\operatorname{Hess}_{h_j}N_j$ of [math:def-laplace-beltrami-operator-as-trace-of-the-hessian](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-laplace-beltrami-operator-as-trace-of-the-hessian), applied to the smooth induced Riemannian metric. The ambient Ricci tensor is the contraction in clause (c) of [physics:def-gr-curvature-tensor-and-derived-tensors](https://alphabetamath.cc/physics/item/def-gr-curvature-tensor-and-derived-tensors), for a smooth time-oriented Lorentzian four-metric. This supplier has domain mathematics and imposes no physical field equation.

[F5] For a smooth map between open subsets of $\mathbb R^3$, an invertible derivative gives a smooth local inverse $G$ with $DG=(DF\circ G)^{-1}$ by [math:lem-choice-free-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-in-euclidean-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-choice-free-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-in-euclidean-space). Its hypotheses are local and require no uniform neighborhood size; quantitative derivative bounds are established below.

[F6] The mean-value inequality for a totally differentiable map on a convex open Euclidean domain turns a uniform operator bound on its derivative into the same Lipschitz bound. [math:thm-mean-value-inequality-for-total-derivatives](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-mean-value-inequality-for-total-derivatives) For a nonempty closed bounded subset of $\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge1$, every continuous real function attains its extrema. [math:thm-euclidean-heine-borel-pseudocompactness-and-extreme-values](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-euclidean-heine-borel-pseudocompactness-and-extreme-values) These inputs apply to smooth coordinate maps and the closed three-dimensional coordinate ball used below.

## Proof

**Given:** The positive lapse equation, nonnegative potential and anchored connected portion. **Goal:** Propagate a quantitative lower bound and transfer regularity.

1.1 The operator and curvature conventions are [F4]. By [F1], $|k_j|$ and the coordinate normal components are bounded. The Ricci components in [F4] depend smoothly on the ambient metric, its inverse and its coordinate derivatives through order two; joint smoothness and compactness of the background parameter-position set bound these components uniformly. Thus the contraction with the bounded normal components is bounded, and $V_j$ is uniformly bounded. The local induced metric coefficients and their required derivatives have uniform bounds and common ellipticity in the buffered charts of [F1]. Consequently $m_j=\sqrt{\det((h_j)_{ab})}$ has common positive lower and upper bounds, $A_j=(m_jh_j^{ab})$ has common positive ellipticity and upper bounds, and $q_j=m_jV_j$ has a common nonnegative upper bound. The common radii, metric bounds and buffers in [F1] allow its bounded connected cover of $L_j$ to be covered by coordinate half-balls with uniformly bounded cardinality, each inside a larger controlled coordinate ball; their overlap graph along $L_j$ is connected. Indeed one can take the small intrinsic covering balls in [F1] inside these coordinate half-balls by the lower metric bound, retaining their centers and witnessed overlaps. The larger coordinate balls have a common radius chosen below the chart buffer. On each such ball the smooth positive lapse is a $C^2$ pointwise solution with the nonnegative bounded potential specified above. The inherited choice hypothesis in [F2] holds, so [F2] gives a common constant $C\ge1$ comparing any two lapse values in each half-ball. [F1, F2, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $J$ be a common upper bound for the number of these covering half-balls and choose the anchor $x_*\in L_j$ with $N_j(x_*)\ge a$. For $x\in L_j$, take covering half-balls containing $x_*$ and $x$. A path in the connected overlap graph can have repeated vertices deleted, leaving at most $J$ vertices. At each successive overlap choose a point in $L_j$ lying in the two half-balls. Comparing consecutive values, from $x_*$ through those points to $x$, uses at most $J$ of the local inequalities of step 1.1. Multiplication yields $N_j(x_*)\le C^J N_j(x)$, hence $N_j(x)\ge a/C^J$. Put $H=C^J$. This uses an actual anchor, not an individual asymptotic limit alone; the finite choices and multiplication introduce no new analytic premise. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, choose, algebra]

3.1 Let $\pi(t,x)=x$ be stationary projection and parametrize a local temporal graph near a point $p\in L_j$ by coordinates $y$. The determinant of $D(\pi|_{\Sigma_j})$ is, up to smooth bounded nonzero background and graph-volume factors, $-g_j(T,n_j)=N_j$: compare the ambient volume of $(T,\partial_{y^1},\partial_{y^2},\partial_{y^3})$ with the product volume. The local tangent vectors and background coordinate changes have common upper bounds by [F1]. Step 2.1 therefore gives a common lower absolute determinant bound at $p$; the cofactor formula and the upper matrix-entry bounds give a common inverse-matrix bound there. In a fixed base chart write $F_j(y)=\pi(\iota_j(y))$, where $\iota_j$ parametrizes the temporal graph and $\iota_j(0)=p$. The map is smooth on an open chart domain and its derivative at zero is invertible, so [F5] supplies a smooth local inverse $G_j$ with $DG_j=(DF_j\circ G_j)^{-1}$. For each $r\ge2$, differentiating $F_j\circ G_j=\operatorname{id}$ $r$ times gives $DF_j(G_j)D^rG_j+R_{j,r}=0$, where $R_{j,r}$ is a finite sum of products of derivatives of $F_j$ through order $r$ and of $G_j$ through order $r-1$. Thus uniform bounds for these derivatives follow recursively wherever the inverse-matrix bound holds. [F1, F3, F5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Choose a fixed finite base atlas near $\pi(Q)$, with relatively compact smaller chart regions covering $\pi(Q)$ inside larger regions, including the fixed buffered chart pieces used for $K$. Carry out the following construction in each such chart for every center whose projection is in its closed smaller region, and take common constants over this finite collection. By [F1] the centered temporal domains contain a common closed coordinate ball $\overline B_\rho(0)$ inside their buffers, after shrinking $\rho>0$; their projections lie in the larger base charts. Let $B_0\ge1$ bound $\|DF_j(0)^{-1}\|$ from step 3.1 and let $M_2$ bound $\|D^2F_j\|$ on the buffered domains. Shrink $\rho$ so that $B_0M_2\rho\le1/2$. The mean-value inequality in [F6], applied first to $DF_j$ on a containing convex buffered ball and then to $y\mapsto F_j(y)-DF_j(0)y$ on the open radius-$\rho$ ball, gives $|F_j(y)-F_j(z)-DF_j(0)(y-z)|\le M_2\rho|y-z|$, hence $|F_j(y)-F_j(z)|\ge(2B_0)^{-1}|y-z|$. Continuity extends this inequality to the closed ball. The same derivative estimate gives invertibility of $DF_j(y)$ and $\|DF_j(y)^{-1}\|\le2B_0$ throughout the ball. Set $\varepsilon=\rho/(4B_0)$. For any base point $b$ with $|b-F_j(0)|<\varepsilon$, the function $|F_j(y)-b|^2$ attains a minimum on the nonempty closed bounded ball by [F6]. On its boundary, $|F_j(y)-b|\ge\rho/(2B_0)-|b-F_j(0)|>|b-F_j(0)|$, so a minimizing point is interior. At an interior minimum, each coordinate difference quotient has nonnegative right and nonpositive left limits, so all first derivatives vanish, giving $DF_j(y)^T(F_j(y)-b)=0$; invertibility forces $F_j(y)=b$. The segment inequality gives uniqueness, and [F5] makes this inverse smooth. Thus the inverse exists on the whole base ball $B_\varepsilon(F_j(0))$, with inverse-matrix bound $2B_0$. The recursion in step 3.1 gives every fixed finite derivative bound on these common-radius balls. All constants are independent of j and the center $p\in L_j$. [F1, F5, F6, step 3.1, choose, algebra]

5.1 Fix a compact base set $K$ satisfying the lifted-containment condition in the Statement. Every point of its graph lies in $L_j$, so step 4.1 applies at every such point. The stationary time coordinate composed with $\iota_j\circ G_j$ is the existing stationary height $u_j$ on each inverse ball; global graph uniqueness makes these expressions agree. The time coordinate and every fixed derivative of the buffered temporal parametrizations have common bounds by [F1]. In particular $|u_j|\le\sup_Q|t|<\infty$ on $K$, and the graph lifts of the inverse balls remain in $Q_+$, where $|t|\le\sup_{Q_+}|t|<\infty$; these bounds follow by a finite cover of the compact tubes by bounded product-coordinate boxes. Composition and the inverse derivative bounds therefore give uniform derivatives of every fixed finite order on the common-radius base balls. In each fixed base chart $\phi_\ell$, pairs $z,w\in K_\ell$ with $|z-w|<\varepsilon/2$ are joined by a straight segment in the inverse ball centered at the first point; the whole ball lies in the larger chart, even when the segment leaves $K_\ell$. The mean-value bound in [F6], applied to the order-$m$ coordinate derivative fields on that convex ball, gives $|D^m v_{j,\ell}(z)-D^m v_{j,\ell}(w)|\le C_{m+1}|z-w|$ there. For pairs at distance at least $\varepsilon/2$, the order-$m$ supremum bound gives the difference quotient bound $2C_m(2/\varepsilon)^\alpha$. These two estimates bound the order-$m$ Holder seminorm for every $0<\alpha<1$, including $m=0$. For every multi-index $\beta$ of order $m$, these estimates apply to $D^\beta v_{j,\ell}$, where $v_{j,\ell}=u_j\circ\phi_\ell^{-1}$; all lower-order suprema are already bounded. Taking the finite sums and maximum in the compact-set formula of the Statement gives the asserted full $C^{m,\alpha}(K)$ bounds and in particular the full $C^{4,\alpha}(K)$ bound. This covers exactly the target graph points in the controlled anchored portions. [F1, F3, F6, step 3.1, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
