## Statement

Under the preceding hypotheses, every tangential derivative $D_tD^Iw$, with I tangential and $|I|=m-2$, lies in $C^{2,\alpha}$ up to a smaller flat boundary patch. Consequently all derivatives of w of total order $m+1$ containing at least $m-1$ tangential derivatives have continuous Holder extensions to that patch.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] For $v=D^Iw$, with $I$ tangential and $|I|=m-2$, the tangential quotient $\delta_hv$ is a classical $C^{2,\alpha}$ solution on a fixed smaller half-ball, has zero flat-face trace, and has uniformly $C^{0,\alpha}$ forcing, values and first derivatives as $h\to0$. The preceding flattened equation has smooth coefficients on the admissible jet domain and a symmetric uniformly positive principal matrix on a compact graph-jet neighborhood. [lem-author-c9fb06089ef2-differentiated-equation-and-difference-quotient](item-lem-author-c9fb06089ef2-differentiated-equation-and-difference-quotient) [lem-author-c9fb06089ef2-flattened-dirichlet-quasilinear-equation](item-lem-author-c9fb06089ef2-flattened-dirichlet-quasilinear-equation)

[F2] On a fixed bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain, with fixed uniformly elliptic $C^{0,\alpha}$ principal coefficients, the Dirichlet boundary Schauder theorem bounds the $C^{2,\alpha}$ norm of a given classical solution by its supremum norm, its $C^{0,\alpha}$ forcing norm and its $C^{2,\alpha}$ boundary-data norm. We use zero lower-order coefficients. [math:thm-boundary-schauder-estimate-for-the-dirichlet-problem](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-boundary-schauder-estimate-for-the-dirichlet-problem)

[F3] An equicontinuous pointwise bounded family of real continuous functions on a nonempty compact metric space has compact closure in the supremum metric. A compact metric space is sequentially compact under the stated choice hypotheses. [math:thm-arzela-ascoli-for-real-ck](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-arzela-ascoli-for-real-ck) [math:thm-metric-compactness-equivalences](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-metric-compactness-equivalences)

[F4] Work in ZFC. The assumed Axiom of Choice implies Dependent Choice and Countable Choice, supplying the choice hypotheses of [F2] and [F3]. [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice) [math:thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice)

[F5] In every Euclidean dimension there is a smooth cutoff $\eta$ equal to one on the closed unit ball and zero outside the radius-two ball, with $0\le\eta\le1$. Translation and rescaling give $\eta((x-p)/r)$ and the derivative formula $D^\beta[\eta((x-p)/r)]=r^{-|\beta|}(D^\beta\eta)((x-p)/r)$. [math:lem-schwartz-cutoffs-from-the-standard-smooth-step](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-schwartz-cutoffs-from-the-standard-smooth-step)

[F6] For $f$ continuously differentiable on an open interval containing $[a,b]$, $f(b)-f(a)=\int_a^b f^{\prime}(s)\,ds$, with oriented endpoints. We apply this to interior coordinate segments and then use continuity to reach the flat face. [math:lem-fundamental-theorem-and-integration-by-parts](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-fundamental-theorem-and-integration-by-parts)

[F7] The $C^{k,\alpha}$ boundary-extension class requires bounded derivative fields through order $k$, continuous extensions of these fields to the closure, and a finite top-order $\alpha$-Hölder seminorm. For scalar fields, subtraction of the two products gives $[ab]_\alpha\le\|a\|_\infty[b]_\alpha+\|b\|_\infty[a]_\alpha$. [math:def-holder-spaces-c-k-alpha-and-their-scaled-norms](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-holder-spaces-c-k-alpha-and-their-scaled-norms)

[F8] Coordinate differentiations of a $C^k$ function commute through order $k$. We use this at the already known orders of $w$, and for the $C^2$ improved tangential fields. [math:thm-symmetry-of-higher-mixed-partials](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-symmetry-of-higher-mixed-partials)

[F9] The scalar sum and product rules apply to coordinate restrictions, and the one-variable chain rule gives the translated and rescaled cutoff derivatives in [F5]. Iterating the product rule twice differentiates the cutoff times the quotient. [math:lem-derivative-algebra-and-chain-rules](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-derivative-algebra-and-chain-rules)

[F10] A closed bounded subset of finite-dimensional Euclidean space is compact for its Euclidean metric topology. [math:cor-heine-borel-in-the-product-topology](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-heine-borel-in-the-product-topology)

## Proof

**Given:** The preceding hypotheses, in particular $w\in C^{m,\alpha}$, $m\ge4$, $0<\alpha<1$, the flattened equation and homogeneous flat-face condition in [F1], and ZFC as in [F4].

**Goal:** Prove the stated tangential derivative gain on a smaller flat boundary patch.

1.1 Fix a boundary point and translate its coordinates to $p=0$. Work in a smaller half-ball from [F1]. A fixed smooth bounded subdomain $\Omega$ with a flat face near $0$ can be constructed as follows. Start with a sufficiently small ball of radius $a$ centered at $ae_3$. Its lower hemisphere is $\ell(x^{\prime})=a-\sqrt{a^2-|x^{\prime}|^2}$ for $|x^{\prime}|<a$. Choose $0<2\rho<a/2$ and the two-dimensional cutoff $\theta(x^{\prime})=\eta(x^{\prime}/\rho)$ from [F5]. Replace that lower graph by $(1-\theta)\ell$ and leave the upper hemisphere unchanged. This surface is smooth: near $|x^{\prime}|=a$ it is the original sphere, while on the modified cap it is a smooth graph. Its enclosed domain lies in $x_3>0$, has flat lower face for $|x^{\prime}|<\rho$, and its closure lies in the closure of the original half-ball if $a$ is small enough. Choose $r>0$ sufficiently small that $\Omega\cap B_{4r}=B_{4r}^+$ and $4r<\rho$. Take the three-dimensional cutoff $\chi(x)=\eta(x/(2r))$. It equals one on $B_{2r}$ and vanishes outside $B_{4r}$. Thus $z_h=\chi\delta_hv$ has zero data on the flat face by [F1] and is identically zero near the remaining boundary. The domain and cutoff are fixed independently of sufficiently small admissible $h$. [F1, F5, given, construct]

1.2 We first prove a local interchange rule requiring only one of the two second mixed derivatives. Let $u\in C^1$ near an interior coordinate rectangle, let $a,b$ be distinct coordinate directions, and suppose $H=\partial_b\partial_a u$ exists and is continuous there. For all sufficiently small oriented $s,h$, two applications of [F6] give $u(x+se_a+he_b)-u(x+he_b)-u(x+se_a)+u(x)=\int_0^s\int_0^h H(x+re_a+qe_b)\,dq\,dr$. Divide by $h$ and let $h\to0$. The left side tends to $\partial_bu(x+se_a)-\partial_bu(x)$ because $u\in C^1$. The right side tends to $\int_0^s H(x+re_a)\,dr$: its error is bounded by $|s|\sup_{r,q}|H(x+re_a+qe_b)-H(x+re_a)|$, with $r$ between $0$ and $s$ and $q$ between $0$ and $h$. This supremum tends to zero by continuity on the fixed compact rectangle. Indeed, otherwise a sequence of witnessing pairs has a convergent subsequence by [F10], [F3] and [F4], and its two arguments converge to the same point, contradicting continuity. Divide the resulting identity by $s$ and let $s\to0$. Continuity of $H$ gives existence of $\partial_a\partial_bu(x)$ and equality $\partial_a\partial_bu(x)=H(x)$. The argument applies at every interior point. [F3, F4, F6, F10, algebra]

2.1 Write $R_h=a^{ij}(\delta_hv)_{ij}$ for the forcing in [F1]. The coordinate product rule [F9] and symmetry of $a^{ij}$ give $a^{ij}(z_h)_{ij}=\chi R_h+2a^{ij}\chi_i(\delta_hv)_j+a^{ij}\chi_{ij}\delta_hv$. The coefficients have fixed $C^{0,\alpha}$ bounds and ellipticity constants on this subdomain by [F1]. Every term on the right is uniformly $C^{0,\alpha}$ by [F1], the fixed smooth cutoff and the product inequality in [F7]. For each nonzero sufficiently small $h$, $z_h$ is already a classical $C^{2,\alpha}$ solution with zero boundary data and uniformly bounded supremum norm. The choice hypotheses follow from [F4]. Apply [F2] with $b=c=0$ and $g=0$ to obtain $\|z_h\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\overline\Omega)}\le C$, with $C$ independent of $h$. [F1, F2, F4, F5, F7, F9, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Choose a positive admissible sequence $h_j\to0$ and set $K=\overline{B_r^+}$, a nonempty compact metric space by [F10]. The finitely many fields $D^\beta z_{h_j}$, $|\beta|\le2$, are uniformly bounded on $K$. Their top-order fields are uniformly equicontinuous by the common Hölder bound from step 2.1. For $|\beta|\le1$, join two interior points of $B_r^+$ by changing their coordinates one at a time. All intermediate segments lie in $B_{2r}^+\subset\Omega$, and their total length is at most $\sqrt3$ times the endpoint distance. Applying [F6] to each segment and using the uniform next-derivative bound gives a common Lipschitz bound; continuity extends it to $K$. By [F4] the choice assumptions hold. Apply [F3] successively to the finitely many families and retain subsequences. This yields a single subsequence for which $D^\beta z_{h_j}\to Z_\beta$ uniformly on $K$ for every $|\beta|\le2$. For interior $x\in B_r^+$, $\chi=1$ and differentiability of $v$ give $Z_0(x)=D_tv(x)$. Both sides have continuous extensions, so this identity also holds on the flat face. [F1, F3, F4, F6, F7, F10, step 1.1, step 2.1, choose]

4.1 For any coordinate segment in $B_r^+$ and $|\beta|\le1$, [F6] gives $D^\beta z_{h_j}(x+te_i)-D^\beta z_{h_j}(x)=\int_0^t D^{\beta+e_i}z_{h_j}(x+se_i)\,ds$. Uniform convergence permits passage to the limit: the integral error is at most $|t|\|D^{\beta+e_i}z_{h_j}-Z_{\beta+e_i}\|_\infty$. Dividing the limiting identity by $t$ and using continuity of $Z_{\beta+e_i}$ gives $\partial_iZ_\beta=Z_{\beta+e_i}$ in $B_r^+$. These fields already extend continuously to $K$. For $|\beta|=2$, passage to the limit in $|D^\beta z_{h_j}(x)-D^\beta z_{h_j}(y)|\le C|x-y|^\alpha$ preserves the Hölder bound on $K$. Hence [F7] gives $D_tv\in C^{2,\alpha}(K)$. For each purely tangential multi-index $J$ of length $m-1$, write $J=I+e_t$; [F8] at the already known order $m-1$ identifies $D^Jw=D_tD^Iw$. Thus every purely tangential field $D^Jw$ of order $m-1$ is $C^{2,\alpha}$ on $K$. [F1, F6, F7, F8, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The only case not immediately ordered as two derivatives of an improved tangential field is an outermost tangential derivative with two normal differentiations among its preceding $m$ derivatives. Write those preceding derivatives, using [F8] only through the known order $m$, as $D_3^2D^Iw$, where $I$ is tangential and $|I|=m-2$. Put $v=D^Iw$ and $u=D_3v$. The given $C^{m,\alpha}$ regularity makes $v\in C^2$ and $u\in C^1$ in the interior. By [F8] for $v$, $D_tu=D_3D_tv$. By step 4.1, $D_tv\in C^{2,\alpha}(K)$, so $D_3D_tu=D_3^2D_tv$ exists and is continuous, with a Hölder extension to $K$. Apply step 1.2 to this $u$ with $a=t$ and $b=3$. It yields $D_tD_3^2D^Iw=D_3^2D_tD^Iw=D_3^2D^Jw$, where $J=I+e_t$ is purely tangential of order $m-1$. Thus the previously missing ordering exists and equals a second derivative of the improved field $D^Jw$, including its continuous Hölder extension. [given, F7, F8, step 4.1, step 1.2, algebra]

6.1 To exhaust all orderings, let $\gamma$ be a multi-index of length $m+1$ with $\gamma_3\le2$, and consider any ordered derivative with this multiset of coordinate directions. Its first $m$ applied differentiations commute by [F8], so it suffices to treat $D_iD^\beta w$, where $|\beta|=m$ and $\beta+e_i=\gamma$. Unless $i$ is tangential and $\beta_3=2$, the multi-index $\beta$ contains a purely tangential multi-index $J$ of length $m-1$. Write $\beta=J+e_j$ and use the known order-$m$ commutation to obtain $D^\beta w=D_j(D^Jw)$. Step 4.1 supplies $D_iD_j(D^Jw)$ with its continuous Hölder extension. The exceptional case is exactly step 5.1. These fields agree for a fixed $\gamma$: if $\gamma_3=2$, all equal $D_3^2D^{\gamma-2e_3}w$ by the preceding cases; if $\gamma_3=1$, an outer tangential direction $t$ is compared with the outer normal direction using $J=\gamma-e_t-e_3$ and $D_tD_3(D^Jw)=D_3D_t(D^Jw)$ by [F8] on the improved $C^2$ field; if $\gamma_3=0$, any two distinct outer directions $i,j$ are compared in the same way using $J=\gamma-e_i-e_j$. In the last two cases all reordered derivatives through order $m$ are already equal by [F8]. Hence every claimed ordering exists and is identified without assuming $w\in C^{m+1}$. There are only finitely many relevant $I,t$, so take a common smaller patch if needed; the identified second derivatives have the continuous Hölder extensions supplied by step 4.1 and [F7]. This proves the stated consequence. [given, F7, F8, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
