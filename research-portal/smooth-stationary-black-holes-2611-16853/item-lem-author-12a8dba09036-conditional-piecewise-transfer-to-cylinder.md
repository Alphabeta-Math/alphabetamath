## Statement

Let $E=\mathbb R_t\times B$ be a connected smooth four-dimensional stationary product with two smooth time-oriented Lorentzian metrics $g$ and $\widehat g$ and common complete stationary flow $\Phi_s(t,b)=(t+s,b)$ generated by $T=\partial_t$. Use the inherited ZFC convention. Supply a coordinate end $B_\infty\subset B$ identified with $\{x\in\mathbb R^3:r=|x|>a\}$, where $a>0$. On $\mathbb R_t\times B_\infty$, assume the routing metric bounds $g_{00}=-1+2M/r+e(x)$, $e=O_6(r^{-2})$, $g_{0i}=O_6(r^{-2})$ and $g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}=O_6(r^{-1})$, for $M\in\mathbb R$. Fix $R_a>a$ and $C\ge0$ such that $|e(x)|\le Cr^{-2}$ for $r\ge R_a$, choose $R_f\ge\max(R_a,8|M|,2\sqrt C)$, and use the $g$ time orientation for which $T$ is future at large radius. Fix any $R\ge R_f$. In this end define $C_R(s)=\{(s,x):|x|=R\}$ and its comparison cylinder $\mathscr C_R=\bigcup_{s\in\mathbb R}C_R(s)=\{(t,x):t\in\mathbb R,|x|=R\}$. This is a coordinate definition; no causal character of the cylinder for $\widehat g$ is assumed.

Assume a transfer operation sends each smooth future $g$-timelike curve on a compact interval in $E$ to a finite piecewise smooth future $\widehat g$-timelike curve on a compact interval in $E$, with the same initial point and the same ordered spatial path in $B$ (allowing an increasing reparametrization and preserving both spatial endpoints). Assume every $\Phi_s$ preserves the future cone of $\widehat g$. If $p\in E$ has future $g$-timelike access inside $E$ to some specified $z\in U_f=\mathbb R_t\times\{r>R_f\}$, then a finite piecewise smooth future $\widehat g$-timelike curve from $p$ ends on $\mathscr C_R$, hence on some $C_R(s)$ at finite stationary time. No assumption that $p$ starts inside that cylinder is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The routing supplier applies to the original metric on the supplied product end with the metric bounds, constants, radius and future orientation stated above. It gives a smooth future $g$-timelike route from $z$ to $r=R$ staying in $r\ge R_f$. The differentiated bounds and the radius $r=|x|$ use the asymptotic-data convention. [F2] The Lorentzian, time-direction and inherited ZFC conventions are those of the stationary-data interface. The supplied global flow has the group law, is smooth, preserves both metrics by stationarity, and preserves $E$ for all real times. Its explicit formula fixes the spatial projection $\pi_B(t,b)=b$ and gives $\Phi_s(C_R(u))=C_R(u+s)$. [def-stationary-lorentzian-data](item-def-stationary-lorentzian-data) [math:def-local-and-global-flow](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-local-and-global-flow)

[F3] Apply the open-set/geodesic-piece supplier with ambient spacetime $(E,g)$ and open set $U=E$. Its conclusion $U\subset I^-_g(U)\cap I^+_g(U)$ makes that chronological exterior equal to $E$. Since $U_f\subset U$ and the access curve lies in $E$, its finite smooth geodesic replacement from $p$ to $z$ lies in $E$. Chronological access and future timelike curves use the stated reachability convention. [lem-author-12a8dba09036-open-end-causal-convexity-and-geodesic-pieces](item-lem-author-12a8dba09036-open-end-causal-convexity-and-geodesic-pieces) [physics:def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability](https://alphabetamath.cc/physics/item/def-gr-causal-curves-and-reachability)

[F4] Finite concatenations of future timelike curves with matching endpoints remain future timelike; increasing affine changes of compact parameter intervals preserve them. A finite piecewise smooth curve is locally absolutely continuous. These are clauses (c), (d) and (e) of the causal-curve calculus, used under the inherited ZFC convention, which supplies its Countable Choice and Dependent Choice hypotheses. ## Proof

**Given:** The stationary product, coordinate end, routing bounds, explicit access point $z$, and regularity-preserving transfer operation in the Statement.

**Goal:** Produce a finite piecewise smooth future $\widehat g$-timelike curve from $p$ to $\mathscr C_R$.

1.1 Apply [F3] to the supplied future $g$-timelike access curve from $p$ to $z$. Append the far-tail route of [F1], which is applicable since $r(z)>R_f$ and $R\ge R_f$. This gives a finite chain of smooth future $g$-timelike pieces $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$ in $E$, with successive matching endpoints, initial point $p$, and final point on $\mathscr C_R$. Parametrize each piece on $[0,1]$ using [F4]. This construction allows the access point and the original point to lie outside the target cylinder. [given, F1, F3, F4]

2.1 Transfer $\gamma_1$ using the stated operation. Suppose a finite piecewise smooth future $\widehat g$-timelike chain has been obtained through the transfer of $\gamma_j$, with endpoint $y_j$. Spatial endpoint preservation gives $\pi_B(y_j)=\pi_B(\gamma_{j+1}(0))$. Write $y_j=(u_j,b_j)$ and $\gamma_{j+1}(0)=(v_j,b_j)$, and set $s_j=u_j-v_j$. Transfer $\gamma_{j+1}$ at its original initial point and apply the fixed map $\Phi_{s_j}$ to its entire output. By [F2] and future-cone preservation this translated output is defined throughout its compact parameter interval, lies in $E$, is future $\widehat g$-timelike, and starts at $y_j$. Smoothness of $\Phi_{s_j}$ preserves each smooth piece, so the output still has only finitely many smooth pieces. The translation also preserves its spatial endpoints. [given, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Iterate step 2.1 over the finite original chain. Use increasing affine reparametrizations to place the transferred pieces on consecutive compact intervals and concatenate them by [F4]. The total number of smooth subpieces is a finite sum of finite numbers, so the resulting curve is finite piecewise smooth and future $\widehat g$-timelike, with initial point $p$. Its final spatial point is that of $\gamma_m(1)$, whose radius is $R$; hence its endpoint lies on $\mathscr C_R$, or equivalently on $C_R(s)$ for its finite real time coordinate $s$. This is arrival on the cylinder and requires no initial interior position. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
