## Statement

In the regular mass-one stationary vacuum/Harrison construction, with original exterior section maximal and $T|_S\not\equiv0$, fix $0<\delta<q_*\le1$. Suppose a connected smooth spacelike maximal section $\Sigma_*$ with boundary S already exists at q*, its interior is a stationary graph, and its normalized end height has the stated decay $|D^j\widetilde u_*|\le C r_{q_*}^{-3/4-j}$ for $0\le j\le4$. Then for every sufficiently close physical parameter $q\in[\delta,1]$ there is a smooth spacelike maximal section with the same boundary S, whose interior is a global stationary graph and whose normalized end height satisfies the same decay order through four derivatives.

Fix an arbitrary Hölder exponent $\alpha$ with $0<\alpha<1$ throughout. In the fixed chart generated by the smooth future timelike transverse field $Y$, equal to the normal near S and to T at infinity, the nearby sections are a unique local $C^1$ branch $w(q)$ in $X=\{w\in C^{4,\alpha}_{-3/4}:w|_S=0\}$, with $w(q_*)=0$. Uniqueness is only among sufficiently small admissible flow graphs. Each section is smooth up to S; no smooth parameter dependence in all higher weighted norms is asserted. The interval and constants may depend on the supplied reference slice and a positive parameter lower bound. At q*=1, the analytic implicit-function argument may use the supplied smooth metric extension in q and then restrict to the physical interval. This proves relative openness, not existence for all parameters or global uniqueness of maximal sections.

The original-section maximality used here is assumed on the actual physical exterior section $C=\Sigma_0\cap\mathcal E$, with the original metric $g$, so $H_g|_C=0$. The selected physical end retains its stated mass-one normalization. Maximality of an independently labelled chart-side section or another supplied maximal section does not replace this premise. No equality between the hypersurface identification and the physical stationary end chart is assumed. ## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

**Goal:** Establish the stated regularity or continuation conclusion.

1.1 Put $\beta=-3/4$, $\bar h=q_*^{-2}h_*$ and $\bar V=q_*^2V_*$. By [F3], the reference is a connected smooth exterior, $\bar h-\delta\in C^{3,\alpha}_{-1}$, $0\le\bar V\in C^{2,\alpha}_{-4}$, and X and $Z=C^{2,\alpha}_{\beta-2}$ are Banach. Thus [F4] makes $\bar L=\Delta_{\bar h}-\bar V:X\to Z$ a bounded isomorphism. Also [F3] makes multiplication by $a_*=-g_{q_*}(Y,n_*)$ a bounded isomorphism of X. The derivative is $A_*=q_*^{-2}\bar L M_{a_*}$. Its inverse on a target value f is $M_{a_*^{-1}}\bar L^{-1}(q_*^2f)$, a bounded composition. This verifies actual invertibility, not merely zero Fredholm index. [F3, F4, F12, F13, F17, given, algebra]

2.1 The admissible map from [F3] is C1 on an open neighborhood in $\mathbb R\times X$, with value zero at $(q_*,0)$ because the reference is maximal. Apply [F6] with parameter space the real line, unknown space X and target Z, using the bounded inverse from step 1.1. It gives a unique local C1 branch $w(q)$ with $w(q_*)=0$ and $\mathcal H(q,w(q))=0$. Shrink the neighborhood so its graphs stay embedded, spacelike and fixed pointwise on S as in [F14]. If an endpoint metric extension was used, retain only the physical parameters. [F3, F6, F13, F14, F17, step 1.1]

3.1 Each w(q) initially lies in C4-alpha up to S. On every compact core or finite end chart, [F7] gives a smooth uniformly elliptic quasilinear equation with zero boundary height on S. The chain [F8]–[F11] applies: the interior smoothness is first established by classical difference quotients; tangential quotients at the boundary obey the exact Dirichlet Schauder theorem; the elliptic equation then recovers the other normal derivatives. Hence each w(q) is smooth up to S and throughout the interior. No prior smoothness is assumed to apply the classical quotient estimates. This changes no weighted-norm or boundary condition of the branch. [F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, F14, step 2.1]

3.2 On the end the transverse field is T, so in the fixed original spatial coordinates the new original-time height is $u_q(x)=u_*(x)+w(q)(x)$. Put $c_q=d_q/(4q)$. By [F3], $c_{q_*}>0$; [F16] makes $d_q$ a continuous polynomial in q. Since $q_*>0$, shrink the parameter neighborhood so q, $d_q$, $c_q$ and their reciprocals have common positive bounds. By [F15], $u_*(x)=c_{q_*}^{-1}\widetilde u_*(q_*x)$. The assumed reference estimates therefore give $|D_x^ju_*|\le C r^{-3/4-j}$ for $0\le j\le4$, by constant coordinate scaling; no fourth-derivative Hölder seminorm is needed for this inference. The weighted norm of w(q) in [F13] gives $|D_x^jw(q)|\le C r^{-3/4-j}$ for the same orders, locally uniformly in q by the weighted-norm continuity of the branch. Adding these estimates and using $\widetilde u_q(x_q)=c_q u_q(x_q/q)$, $r_q=qr$ and $D_{x_q}^j\widetilde u_q=c_q q^{-j}(D_x^ju_q)(x_q/q)$ gives $|D_{x_q}^j\widetilde u_q|\le C r_q^{-3/4-j}$ for $0\le j\le4$, with locally uniform constants. In particular the normalized end height tends to zero, giving the required end conditions before the global projection is invoked. [F3, F13, F15, F16, given, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Since w(q) tends to zero in X, shrink the parameter neighborhood again to satisfy the fixed-reference smallness conditions in [F5]. The candidates are now smooth by step 3.1, maximal by step 2.1 and have the required end data by step 3.2. The supplied lapse, boundary return, properness and one-sheet projection result therefore turns each into a global stationary graph without presupposing that conclusion or transformed global hyperbolicity. Its boundary remains S. This proves the asserted nearby smooth-slice construction and relative openness under the supplied reference hypotheses. [F1, F2, F5, F14, F15, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
