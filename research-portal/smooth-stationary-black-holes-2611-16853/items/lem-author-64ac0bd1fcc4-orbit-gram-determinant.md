## Statement

Let $X_1=T$ and $X_2=Z$ be the generators of the action in [[lem-author-64ac0bd1fcc4-commuting-global-symmetry-action]]. Define their Gram matrix by $H_{ij}=g(X_i,X_j)$ and their signed orbit-area square by $W=-\det H$. Under an invertible real change of basis $X'_i=A_i{}^jX_j$, one has $H'=AHA^{\mathsf T}$ and $W'=(\det A)^2W$. Consequently invertibility of the orbit Gram matrix and the sign of $W$ do not depend on that basis. Use the metric conventions in [[def-stationary-lorentzian-data]]. This algebra does not prove that $W$ is positive anywhere.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The stationary flow $\phi_t$ is generated by $T$, and the normalized axial flow $\psi_s$ is generated by $Z$. The generators are smooth ambient vector fields and the metric is symmetric and bilinear. A basis change here is an invertible two-by-two real matrix at the point under consideration. [[lem-author-64ac0bd1fcc4-commuting-global-symmetry-action]] [[def-stationary-lorentzian-data]] [[def-author-64ac0bd1fcc4-global-axisymmetric-comparison-data]] [[def-circle-action-and-invariant-normal-data]]

[F2] The determinant of a real square matrix is its finite Leibniz sum over permutations. We use this definition for each real two-by-two matrix here. [[math:def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]

[F3] Matrix multiplication is given by $(BC)_{ij}=\sum_k B_{ik}C_{kj}$, and transpose by $(B^{\mathsf T})_{ij}=B_{ji}$. These definitions apply to the real two-by-two matrices here. [[math:def-ring-matrix-product-identity-and-transpose]]

[F4] For square matrices $B,C$ of the same positive finite size over a commutative ring, $\det(BC)=\det(B)\det(C)$ and $\det(B^{\mathsf T})=\det B$. Apply these results over $\mathbb R$ at size two. [[math:thm-determinant-multiplicative]] [[math:thm-determinant-of-transpose]]

[F5] A real square matrix of positive finite size is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero. [[math:thm-real-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-nonzero]]

## Proof

**Given:**The generators, metric and change of basis at one point.

**Goal:** Compute the transformed matrix and signed determinant.

1.1 Bilinearity gives $g(X'_i,X'_j)=A_i{}^kA_j{}^\ell H_{k\ell}$. By the definitions of matrix product and transpose this is exactly $H'=AHA^{\mathsf T}$. [given, F1, F3]

2.1 Apply determinant multiplicativity twice and transpose invariance to obtain $\det H'=\det(A)\det(H)\det(A^{\mathsf T})=(\det A)^2\det H$. Negating this equality gives $W'=(\det A)^2W$. [F2, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Since $A$ is invertible, $\det A\ne0$ by the real-matrix criterion; hence $(\det A)^2>0$. The determinant identity therefore gives $\det H'\ne0$ if and only if $\det H\ne0$. Applying the same criterion to $H'$ and $H$ proves invertibility invariance, and the positive multiplier preserves the sign of $W$, including its zero value. [given, F5, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
