## Statement

Use the global stationary comparison data, and set $M_{\rm ext}=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb R}\phi_t(S_{\rm ext})$ and $D=I_M^+(M_{\rm ext})\cap I_M^-(M_{\rm ext})$. For every $s\in\mathbb R$, $\phi_s(M_{\rm ext})=M_{\rm ext}$ and $\phi_s(D)=D$. Thus the stationary symmetry acts on the entire specified exterior; a restriction of its flow to a small neighborhood would not supply this conclusion for arbitrary times.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The comparison data supply a smooth time-oriented spacetime $(M,g,N)$ and a complete stationary Killing flow $\phi_t$ generated by $T$, defined on all of $M$ for every real time. The Killing condition is $\mathcal L_Tg=0$. [[def-author-64ac0bd1fcc4-global-axisymmetric-comparison-data]] [[def-causal-exterior-and-boundary-data]]

[F2] Chronological futures and pasts are defined by future timelike curves in the specified ambient manifold. [[def-causal-exterior-and-boundary-data]]

[F3] A global flow is smooth on $\mathbb R\times M$, satisfies $\phi_0=\mathrm{id}_M$ and $\phi_s\circ\phi_t=\phi_{s+t}$, and hence each $\phi_s$ has smooth inverse $\phi_{-s}$. [[math:def-local-and-global-flow]]

[F4] For a smooth tensor field $A$ and a smooth vector field $X$ with flow $\Phi_t$, the condition $\mathcal L_XA=0$ implies $\Phi_t^*A=A$ at all defined times. Apply this to $A=g$ and $X=T$ using the Killing condition in F1. [[math:prop-a-tensor-field-is-invariant-under-a-flow-if-and-only-if-its-lie-derivative-vanishes]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data and assumptions in the Statement.

**Goal:** Establish the claimed conditional conclusion.

1.1 The global flow law gives $\phi_s(\bigcup_t\phi_t(S_{\rm ext}))=\bigcup_t\phi_{s+t}(S_{\rm ext})=M_{\rm ext}$. Applying F4 to $g$ and $T$ gives $\phi_s^*g=g$ for every real $s$, so each $\phi_s$ is an isometry. Each $\phi_s$ preserves time orientation: its derivative maps timelike vectors continuously as $s$ varies from zero, where $\phi_0=\mathrm{id}_M$, so a future timelike vector cannot cross to the other timelike component. [F1, F3, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 Composing a future timelike curve with the time-orientation-preserving isometry $\phi_s$ gives such a curve with the corresponding image endpoints. Applying also its inverse $\phi_{-s}$ yields $\phi_s(I_M^\pm(M_{\rm ext}))=I_M^\pm(M_{\rm ext})$. Intersect the two equalities. Completeness is used in the first step and in the inverse for all real $s$; no local-to-global extension is proved. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
