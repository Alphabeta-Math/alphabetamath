## Statement

At $q=0$, use the metric on the coordinate sections $t=\mathrm{constant}$ from the spatial end lemma. Under $x=e^\rho\omega$, $\omega\in\mathbb S^2$, it has the form
$$h_0=d\rho^2+\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}+B,\qquad B=O(e^{-\rho}),$$
where $\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}$ is the unit round sphere metric and the coefficients of $B$, including derivatives through order five in $\rho$ and smooth angular charts, are $O(e^{-\rho})$ on a finite chart cover. This is the asserted asymptotically cylindrical spatial geometry. The coordinate spheres have
$$\operatorname{Area}_{h_0}(\{r=R\})=4\pi+O(R^{-1})\longrightarrow4\pi.$$
The unit radius, and hence $4\pi$, uses the mass-one leading coefficient from the denominator calculation. This statement concerns spatial coordinate sections, not a product decomposition of the full spacetime metric.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [lem-author-84ac8de0c2f0-spatial-harrison-end](item-lem-author-84ac8de0c2f0-spatial-harrison-end) gives $h_0=r^{-2}(\delta_{ij}+E_{ij})dx^idx^j$, with $E_{ij}=O_5(r^{-1})$ in the differentiated convention of [def-stationary-asymptotic-decay-data](item-def-stationary-asymptotic-decay-data).

[F2] [math:def-riemannian-volume-form-on-an-oriented-manifold](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-riemannian-volume-form-on-an-oriented-manifold) defines the area form of a positive-definite metric by the square root of its determinant in a positive chart.

[F3] [math:thm-a-regular-level-set-is-an-embedded-submanifold](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-a-regular-level-set-is-an-embedded-submanifold) applies to a smooth map with a nonempty regular-value fibre and makes that fibre an embedded submanifold of the expected codimension.

[F4] [math:prop-pullback-of-a-riemannian-metric-is-riemannian-exactly-for-immersions](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/prop-pullback-of-a-riemannian-metric-is-riemannian-exactly-for-immersions) gives a smooth positive-definite pullback metric for a smooth immersion into a Riemannian manifold.

[F5] [math:def-oriented-smooth-manifold-and-oriented-chart](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-oriented-smooth-manifold-and-oriented-chart) defines an orientation as a smooth choice of positive tangent-frame ray, with positively oriented charts representing that choice.

[F6] [math:def-integral-of-an-oriented-chart-supported-top-form](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-integral-of-an-oriented-chart-supported-top-form) defines the integral of a smooth top form compactly supported in one oriented chart by its signed coordinate integral. Only finite chart sums on this compact sphere are needed; their independence is proved below.

[F7] [math:thm-change-of-variables-for-compact-jordan-sets](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-change-of-variables-for-compact-jordan-sets) gives the absolute-Jacobian integral formula for an injective $C^1$ map on an open Euclidean set with invertible differential, restricted to a compact Jordan set, for bounded Riemann-integrable functions.

[F8] [math:thm-multidimensional-integral-properties](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-multidimensional-integral-properties) supplies linearity, monotonicity, the absolute-value estimate and coordinate-slice additivity for Riemann-integrable functions on a nondegenerate rectangle. Apply these properties to the compactly supported zero extensions in [F6], on common bounding rectangles; repeated grid cuts also give finite-union additivity.

[F9] [math:thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives) gives $D(g\circ f)(a)=Dg(f(a))\circ Df(a)$ when $f$ is totally differentiable at $a$ and $g$ is totally differentiable at $f(a)$. Here $f(\rho,u)=e^\rho\omega(u)$ is smooth on each open angular chart and maps into the Cartesian end for sufficiently large $\rho$; each component of $E$ is $C^5$ there by [F1]. Thus the rule applies successively to its spatial derivatives through the required order.

[F10] [math:lem-derivative-algebra-and-chain-rules](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-derivative-algebra-and-chain-rules) supplies the scalar sum and product differentiation rules for functions differentiable at the point in question. Apply these rules along each $\rho$ or angular coordinate line to the finite contractions in step 3.1. The error components are $C^5$ and the coordinate factors are smooth, so the required derivatives exist through order five.

## Proof

**Given:** The positive spatial end metric and its mass-one leading term.

**Goal:** Show the cylindrical limit and compute the limiting coordinate-sphere area.

1.1 Let $F(y)=|y|^2$ on $\mathbb R^3$. On $F^{-1}(a^2)$, $a>0$, its differential is $dF_y(v)=2y\cdot v$, which is nonzero since $y\ne0$; the fibre is nonempty. By [F3], the unit sphere $\mathbb S^2=F^{-1}(1)$ and, for $R>R_0$, the coordinate sphere $S_R=F^{-1}(R^2)$ in the spatial end are smooth embedded surfaces. Their tangent spaces are $y^\perp$: differentiating the level equation gives containment, and both spaces have dimension two. The inclusion $j:\mathbb S^2\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^3$ has injective differential, and $\iota_R:\mathbb S^2\to E$, $\iota_R(\omega)=R\omega$, has differential $v\mapsto Rv$, also injective, with smooth inverse $x\mapsto x/R$ onto $S_R$. Thus [F4] constructs the round metric $\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}=j^*\delta$ and the induced sphere metric $k_R=\iota_R^*h_0$, both smooth and positive definite. Give $\mathbb S^2$ the orientation in [F5] for which $(v,w)$ is positive precisely when $\det(\omega,v,w)>0$. This determinant is a smooth nowhere-zero alternating form on each tangent plane, so the orientation is well defined; transport it to $S_R$ by $\iota_R$. The positive metrics and this orientation meet the hypotheses of [F2]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct]

2.1 Use the six hemisphere charts $H_{i,s}=\{\omega:s\omega_i>0\}$, $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $s\in\{-1,1\}$, with coordinates given by the other two components, ordered positively for the orientation in step 1.1. Fix $\chi(u)=0$ for $u\le1/4$ and $\chi(u)=\exp(-1/(u-1/4))$ for $u>1/4$, and put $\psi_{i,s}(\omega)=\chi(s\omega_i)/\sum_{j,t}\chi(t\omega_j)$. The denominator is positive because $\max_i|\omega_i|\ge1/\sqrt3$. Thus these six smooth nonnegative weights sum to one, with each support compactly contained in its hemisphere. Write $a=(i,s)$ and $I_a$ for the chart integral in [F6]. For any smooth two-form $\eta$ on the sphere, define $J(\eta)=\sum_a I_a(\psi_a\eta)$; each summand meets [F6]'s compact-support hypothesis. Define the induced coordinate-sphere area by $\operatorname{Area}_{h_0}(S_R)=J(\operatorname{vol}_{k_R})$, using the oriented parametrization $\iota_R$ from step 1.1. The independence argument below proves that this definition is independent of chart and partition choices. [F2, F5, F6, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Differentiate $x=e^\rho\omega$ using [F9] and [F10]: $dx=e^\rho(\omega\,d\rho+d\omega)$, with $\omega\cdot d\omega=0$. Thus $r^{-2}\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j=d\rho^2+\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}$. In the error term the two differential factors cancel $r^{-2}$, leaving coefficients obtained by contracting $E(e^\rho\omega)$ with the smooth vectors $\omega$ and angular derivatives of $\omega$. On a compact angular chart domain with coordinates $u$, [F9] gives $\partial_\rho(E\circ f)=x^i(\partial_iE)\circ f$ and $\partial_{u^a}(E\circ f)=r(\partial_{u^a}\omega^i)(\partial_iE)\circ f$. Repeated use of [F9] and the coordinatewise product rule [F10] shows by induction that each mixed derivative of a contracted error coefficient of total order $k\le5$ is a finite sum of terms $r^j c(u)(\partial^\beta E_{il})(r\omega(u))$, where $|\beta|=j\le k$ and $c$ is smooth and bounded on that compact domain. At order zero this follows from the displayed contraction. A further derivative either differentiates $r^j c(u)$, retaining that form with the same $j$, or differentiates the composed spatial derivative, adding one factor $r$, one bounded angular factor and one spatial derivative, giving $j+1$. All needed derivatives of the angular factors are bounded on the compact domain. By [F1], every resulting term is bounded by $Cr^j r^{-1-j}=Cr^{-1}=Ce^{-\rho}$. The six hemisphere charts in step 2.1, restricted to $s\omega_i>1/3$, still cover the sphere because $\max_i|\omega_i|\ge1/\sqrt3$; their closures are compact inside their original charts. Taking the largest of finitely many constants proves the asserted bounds through order five on a finite cover, and hence the precise cylinder statement. [F1, F9, F10, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 First let a smooth two-form $\zeta$ have compact support in the overlap of two positive charts. In the first chart, a sufficiently fine fixed rectangular grid gives finitely many closed squares meeting that support, all contained in the overlap: the compact support has positive distance from the complement of this open coordinate overlap. Their union $K$ is compact Jordan and contains the support in its interior. The transition map on the open overlap is injective, smooth and has invertible differential with positive determinant. The top-form transformation law and [F7], applied on $K$ to the bounded smooth coefficient in the second chart, equate the two integrals; the integrands vanish outside $K$ and its image. The zero extensions in [F6] and finite additivity in [F8] therefore give equality of the two chart integrals of $\zeta$. Now let $(V_b,\theta_b)$ be any finite positive-chart decomposition with smooth nonnegative weights summing to one and each support compactly contained in $V_b$. Each $\psi_a\theta_b\eta$ has compact support in $H_a\cap V_b$. Using the equality just proved and finite linearity in [F8] gives $J(\eta)=\sum_{a,b}I_a(\psi_a\theta_b\eta)=\sum_{a,b}I_b(\psi_a\theta_b\eta)=\sum_b I_b(\theta_b\eta)$. This also applies to a locally finite subordinate partition: compactness of the sphere reduces its nonzero weights to finitely many, by taking a finite subcover of neighborhoods each meeting only finitely many supports. If $\eta$ itself has compact support in one positive chart $V$, the same overlap equality gives $J(\eta)=\sum_a I_V(\psi_a\eta)=I_V(\eta)$. Thus $J$ is the global oriented integral, agreeing with chart integrals and independent of partition. Negative charts give the same values because their orientation sign in [F6] cancels the negative transition determinant. A change of oriented parametrization pulls back the form and its chart decomposition to positive charts with the same local coefficients, so it also preserves $J$. Hence step 2.1 defines the intrinsic induced area. [F5, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

4.1 In the notation of step 1.1, restriction of step 3.1 to $r=R$ gives $k_R=\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}+\epsilon_R$ with $\|\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}^{-1}\epsilon_R\|\le C/R$ uniformly. By [F2], the ratio of its positive area form to the round area form is $a_R=\sqrt{\det(I+\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}^{-1}\epsilon_R)}=1+O(R^{-1})$ uniformly: determinant and square root are smooth near the positive identity, and the uniform smallness keeps the matrices in that neighborhood. In the global integral established in steps 2.1 and 3.2, $\operatorname{Area}_{h_0}(S_R)=J(a_R\operatorname{vol}_{\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}})$. Since the six weights are nonnegative, [F8] applied to their chart integrals and $|a_R-1|\le C/R$ gives $|\operatorname{Area}_{h_0}(S_R)-J(\operatorname{vol}_{\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}})|\le(C/R)J(\operatorname{vol}_{\gamma_{\mathbb S^2}})$. Each round chart integrand is smooth with compact support, so this finite round area is well defined. [F2, F6, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 In spherical coordinates $\omega=(\sin\vartheta\cos\varphi,\sin\vartheta\sin\varphi,\cos\vartheta)$, $0<\vartheta<\pi$, $0<\varphi<2\pi$, the pullback of the round metric is $d\vartheta^2+\sin^2\!\vartheta\,d\varphi^2$ and this coordinate order is positive for the orientation of step 1.1. The omitted meridian and poles have zero area: they form finitely many smooth arcs and points, whose portions in the six compact chart supports have two-dimensional content zero: a smooth arc is locally Lipschitz, and subdividing its bounded parameter interval into intervals of length $\eta$ covers its image by $O(\eta^{-1})$ squares of area $O(\eta^2)$. The smooth area densities on the compact supports are bounded. Apply [F7] on compact Jordan pieces away from the meridian and poles, where spherical coordinates and hemisphere-chart coordinates are related by smooth diffeomorphisms with invertible differential. Letting the excluded coordinate strips and caps shrink adds zero in the limit by the preceding bounded-density estimate. Thus changing coordinates in the finite integrals of step 4.1 computes the round area as $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi\sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta d\varphi=4\pi$. Step 4.1 now gives $\operatorname{Area}_{h_0}(S_R)=4\pi+O(R^{-1})\to4\pi$. The coefficient is one in the round metric because the mass-one spatial leading term in [F1] is exactly $r^{-2}\delta$, as used in step 3.1. [F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
